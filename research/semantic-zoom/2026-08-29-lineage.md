## What this adds to the by-inspection lineage

The 2026-07-08 note built its lineage from what Cockburn et al. 2009 and
Bederson 2011 cite, and named four convergent ancestors: Furnas 1986, Perlin &
Fox 1993, Furnas & Bederson 1995, and Nelson's StretchText. The API run
confirms the first two directly and reaches Pad++ rather than the space-scale
paper, which appears in neither bibliography at the ≥2 threshold. Nelson has no
citation record here, as the note expected.

What the by-inspection lineage could not see is the branch structure. The
convergent ancestors fall into three groups: the Pad systems, the zoom-mechanics
studies of how the view moves, and a set of applications (table lens,
perspective wall, calendar and photo browsers) that use the technique without
theorising it. The note's argument works entirely in the first group. The second
group — speed-dependent zooming, smooth viewing models, working-memory costs —
is the evidence base for the *how fast and how far* question, and the pattern
does not currently draw on it.

The descendant side is still the weak half, as the note said it would be. One
survey is all that cites two of the anchors, so the question of where this line
went after 2012 remains open by citation and answered only by the note's own
2023–26 reading.
