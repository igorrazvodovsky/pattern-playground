## Context

From `query.yml`: a pattern library whose graph has a controlled edge vocabulary grounded in Winston/Chaffin/Herrmann meronymy and SKOS. Foundations are design material/substrate entries distinct from patterns (moves) and qualities (lenses). Residual pattern–foundation adjacency sits in `related`; the strawman mints `serves` (pattern → foundation), notes naming the station in the author's words, anatomy controlled per foundation.

Questions:

1. Does pattern-language literature already name the pattern→frame relation? Is `serves` colliding with or duplicating an established term?
2. Is the completion-frame reading (a larger structure calling for smaller moves to complete its stations) an established construct?
3. Does the ontology literature treat "serves a station in an arrangement" as a documented relation — functional parthood, participation? If serves is really functional parthood, the right home might be `enables`.
4. Do pattern languages with a foundation/material layer exist, and how do they type links into it? Known failure modes of layer-specific relation types?

## Convergent findings

### 1. The canon merges the serves-shape into one generic upward link; `serves` collides with nothing

Alexander's only explicit inter-pattern relation is the upward completion link — Noble: "The uses relationship is the only explicit relationship between patterns in Alexander's original architectural patterns," defined as "patterns which help another pattern." Zimmer, Meszaros & Doble, and Noble's synthesis offer nothing finer on the upward direction than uses/used-by; van Welie's aggregation absorbs completion outright ("can include others that complete it"). No source names a distinct relation for *serving a station of a larger frame* as opposed to *being a component of a composite*.

Cuts both ways. There is no collision or duplication (Q1: answered, negative) — but also no precedent to import: Alexander's up-link covers both what this project calls `enables` and what the strawman calls `serves`, undistinguished. The mint is a decomposition of the canon's broadest relation, the same status as `hosts` and `enacts` — the evidence for the split must come from the corpus's own authored notes (the delegation cluster), not from literature.

Papers: Noble 1998, Zimmer 1995, Meszaros & Doble 1997, van Welie & van der Veer 2003, Alexander 1977.

*Draft project takeaway*: the literature's single upward link ("helps to complete", uses, aggregation) is the merged ancestor of both `enables` and `serves`; minting `serves` decomposes an established relation rather than duplicating an established term.

### 2. "Station in a frame" is a documented ontological construct: participation and description-defined roles — and it is *not* parthood

Three independent formal traditions converge on the strawman's core claim that serving a station is neither meronymic nor taxonomic:

- Keet & Artale (extending the same Winston et al. taxonomy the project's part–whole relations stand on) class *participation* — endurant participates in perdurant/process — inside the part-whole family but outside mereological parthood. If a foundation's anatomy is process-like (delegation's lifecycle, assistance's cognitive cycle), a pattern relates to it by participation, not composition. This directly answers Q3's worry: `serves` is *not* functional parthood in disguise, so `enables` is not its right home.
- The OBO Relation Ontology gives participation two of its ten core relations (`has_participant`, `has_agent`), explicitly distinct from `part_of` — precedent that in a deliberately compact controlled relation vocabulary, participation still earns its own type rather than being folded into parthood.
- Descriptions & Situations formalises the frame reading exactly: a description (foundation) *defines roles* (stations); entities (patterns) *play* them. D&S generalises Fillmore's frame semantics — a frame with named frame-elements that get filled — and Riehle's composite-pattern role diagrams are the same construct inside the software-pattern canon.

Naming: the formal kin are `participates-in` (RO, Keet) and `plays` (D&S). `serves` is a legitimate local name; its definition should record this kinship in the SKOS-alignment row (no SKOS equivalent; nearest formal relatives RO `participates_in`, D&S role-playing).

Papers: Keet & Artale 2008, Smith et al. 2005, Gangemi & Mika 2003, Riehle 1997, arXiv 2101.00286.

*Draft project takeaway*: `serves` is the participation/role-playing relation — in the part-whole family but not parthood — which is why it is neither `enables` (component-integral) nor `instantiates` (genus-species); the ontology canon splits exactly where the strawman splits.

### 3. Vertical connectivity is load-bearing for language coherence

Alexander: no pattern is an isolated entity; each helps complete the larger ones above it. Salingaros makes it structural: a loose collection of patterns is not a system; if lower-level details are unconnected to higher levels "the system is not coherent and cannot work," and the richness of between-level connections is a validity criterion for the language itself. Leaving pattern–foundation adjacency untyped in `related` withholds precisely the cross-level connectivity that coherence rides on — support for typing the vertical residue rather than declaring it honest.

Papers: Alexander 1977, Salingaros 2000.

*Draft project takeaway*: between-level connectivity is a validity criterion for a pattern language, not decoration; the pattern–foundation residue is cross-level structure the graph currently cannot see.

### 4. What the strawman would be wrong about (corrections and cautions)

- *Material foundations are not participation.* The prose-cluster claims ("assistance lives largely as microcopy… made of prose") are *constitution/stuff-of* — a different Winston/Keet relation from participation. The literature supports the strawman's four-arm tiebreaker (material → `enables`, foundation-side) and warns against absorbing material claims into `serves`. One type for both would repeat the merge the canon never unpicked.
- *D&S would reify the station.* In the formal treatment the role is first-class: `A serves B` compresses "A plays role R, and B defines R." The strawman deliberately keeps R as prose in the note (per no-pre-legislation and the consumer contract). That is a known trade-off, not an error — but it means station-level queries (group a foundation's incoming serves by station, find unclaimed stations) read prose, an actor's judgement, never a machine match. Same posture as `situationalHints`.
- *No precedent for a typed foundation layer in HCI pattern languages* (Q4): design-systems practice uses "foundations" as a term but links into it untyped; the academic literature has no worked example of layer-specific relation types to borrow failure modes from. Genuinely novel territory — record as a gap, watch the type's own per-count signal rather than assuming safety.

Papers: Keet & Artale 2008, Gangemi & Mika 2003, Dearden & Finlay 2006 (absence evidence).

*Draft project takeaway*: keep material-constitution claims out of `serves` (they are `enables`' substrate arm), and accept knowingly that the station lives in prose — the D&S-style reified role is the road not taken, recorded here in case a future query layer needs it.
