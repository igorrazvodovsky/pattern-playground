# Practice survey: attention and tempo — 2026-08-29

Query: what shipping products do about interruption, notification tempo, and attention. Companion to the same-day literature note; practice evidence only.

> *Provenance.* Gathered via web search 2026-08-29; each claim linked to vendor docs or reputable write-ups. Vendor documentation (Apple/Google developer docs, help-centre pages, official blogs) is flagged as such; where a claim rests on third-party reporting or UI observation, the section says so. Two vendor pages resisted verification: Apple's HIG notifications page is JS-rendered, so the interruption-level and badge quotations were pulled from Apple's developer-docs JSON instead; several default values (Slack's notification schedule, Gmail's snooze presets, Google Calendar's 10-minute lead) appear nowhere in vendor prose and are documented only by third parties reading the UI.

## 1. OS-level attention infrastructure

Both platforms impose a small, fixed urgency vocabulary on app developers, then give the actor an override layer that always wins.

*iOS interruption levels.* Apple's `UNNotificationInterruptionLevel` has exactly four cases ([developer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unnotificationinterruptionlevel), vendor): `passive` ("adds the notification to the notification list without lighting up the screen or playing a sound"), `active` (the default — presents immediately, lights the screen, can play a sound), `timeSensitive` (additionally "breaks through system controls such as Notification Summary and Focus"; "the user can turn off the ability for time sensitive notification interruptions"), and `critical` (bypasses the mute switch). The gradient is graded by who vouches: passive is self-declared restraint, active is the default, time-sensitive is self-declared urgency the actor can revoke per app, and critical requires an [entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.usernotifications.critical-alerts) Apple grants by hand after a request form (vendor; the days-to-weeks review and life-safety bar are [third-party](https://newly.app/how-to/critical-alerts-entitlement)). Time-sensitive needs only an Xcode capability, no approval ([WWDC 2021 session](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10091/), vendor) — an honour system backed by one-tap revocation.

*Apple's restraint doctrine.* The [HIG notifications page](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications) (vendor) argues restraint from revocation risk: "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing… people may turn off all notifications from your app." Errors get an alert, not a notification; foreground apps should surface new information "in a way that's discoverable but not distracting… such as incrementing a badge".

*iOS Focus.* Per-Focus allow-lists of people and apps, and since iOS 16 also silence-lists; a per-Focus toggle for time-sensitive breakthrough; Focus filters that narrow content *inside* apps (a Mail account, a calendar set), with an API so third-party apps can do the same; activation by schedule, location, app usage, or "Smart Activation" ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-up-a-focus-iphd6288a67f/ios), vendor; fine detail corroborated via [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-16-focus/), third-party). Focus state shares across devices by default ([macOS guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/manage-focus-options-on-mac-mchl74e8c77b/mac), vendor), and Focus status is socially legible: apps can tell contacts notifications are silenced, and a sender "can choose to notify you anyway" — a human-level breakthrough valve mirroring the app-level one.

*iOS Scheduled Summary.* Opt-in, off by default: the actor picks delivery times and which apps get batched; the setup picker shows each app's average daily notification count to inform the choice ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-notification-settings-iph7c3d96bab/ios), vendor; picker detail [third-party](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/use-notification-summary/)). It delays and bundles rather than blocks; time-sensitive notifications break through it immediately (vendor, per the interruption-level docs above).

*Android channels and importance.* Since Android 8.0 every notification must belong to a channel; the importance ladder runs `IMPORTANCE_HIGH` / `DEFAULT` / `LOW` / `MIN` / `NONE`, shown to actors as Urgent / High / Medium / Low / None ([developer docs](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/channels), vendor — note the developer constants and user-facing labels are offset by one rung). The power asymmetry is explicit in the doc: once a channel exists the developer cannot raise its importance or change its sound in code; the actor can change all of it per channel. Android 13 flipped notifications to deny-by-default: new installs must request the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` runtime permission ([developer docs](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission), vendor). Conversations are a separate class: person-to-person notifications get their own shade section, and the *actor* (not the developer) can mark one a priority conversation, which by default breaks through Do Not Disturb ([developer docs](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/conversations); [Google help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9069335), both vendor — the help page now frames DND inside a broader "Modes" system). [Digital Wellbeing](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9346420) (vendor) adds app timers, a Focus mode, Bedtime mode, and dashboards counting notifications and unlocks.

*Convergence within the area.* Both platforms landed on the same three-layer structure: a developer-declared urgency vocabulary, an actor override layer that wins, and a scheduled/mode layer that batches or silences by context. Both gate the top rung — Apple by hand-approved entitlement, Android by making the *actor* the one who grants DND breakthrough. Android 13's deny-by-default is the larger default shift; nothing on iOS matches it.

## 2. Workplace tools' interruption budgets

*Slack.* Notifications run on a "notification schedule" — hours when notifications are *allowed*, paused outside them ([help centre](https://slack.com/help/articles/214908388-Pause-your-Slack-notifications), vendor). The widely reported default is 8am–10pm every day, but Slack's own pages never publish the times — treat that figure as UI-observed ([third-party](https://blog.buddieshr.com/slack-notifications-setup-guide/)). Admins can set workspace-wide default DND hours that hold until an individual sets their own ([help centre](https://slack.com/help/articles/214888418-Set-default-Do-Not-Disturb-hours), vendor). Breakthrough is designed and rate-limited: a sender messaging someone in DND is told notifications are paused and can "send a notification anyway" — once per day per person (vendor, same pause article), plus a VIP list of people who always get through. Per channel: All new posts / Just mentions / mute; muted channels still badge on mentions and keywords ([help centre](https://slack.com/help/articles/360056534254-Manage-notifications-for-specific-channels-and-direct-messages), vendor). Global default triggers: DMs, mentions, keywords, replies in followed threads ([notifications guide](https://slack.com/help/articles/360025446073-Guide-to-Slack-notifications), vendor). Mobile waits for desktop inactivity — one minute after locking the screen, or ten minutes after cursor activity stops ([help centre](https://slack.com/help/articles/201355156-Configure-your-Slack-notifications), vendor). Scheduled send anchors to "Tomorrow morning, 9:00 AM", or Monday morning late in the week (mechanics [vendor](https://api.slack.com/messaging/scheduling); preset wording [third-party](https://www.tomsguide.com/uk/how-to/how-to-schedule-slack-messages)). Catch Up is mobile card-at-a-time triage — swipe right to mark read, swipe left to keep unread ([help centre](https://slack.com/help/articles/226410907-View-all-your-unread-messages), vendor); launch coverage framed it as built for 30-second phone check-ins ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2024/1/10/24026571), third-party).

*Microsoft Teams.* "Quiet time" (mobile only) silences during set hours plus whole quiet days, with a toggle syncing the schedule to Outlook mobile ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/quiet-time-in-microsoft-teams-for-mobile-devices-174c4d2d-c7c1-4228-80a7-031c14f9bcf2), vendor). Delivery vocabulary per activity type: "Banner and feed" vs "Only show in feed" ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/notifications-settings/manage-notifications-in-microsoft-teams), vendor). Priority access lets named individuals break through DND (vendor-documented; groups cannot be prioritised, and Microsoft's own Q&A records that Windows OS-level DND can silence Teams entirely so app-level priority access sometimes fails — the OS and app budgets fight each other; [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5774877/priority-notifications-not-working-on-dnd-teams-de), semi-official forum). Sender-side escalation: "Important" vs "Urgent", where urgent re-notifies every 2 minutes for 20 minutes until read ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/chat/mark-a-message-as-important-in-microsoft-teams), vendor; cadence corroborated [third-party](https://www.howtogeek.com/696416/how-to-mark-a-message-in-microsoft-teams-as-important-or-urgent/)). Meetings auto-suppress: Busy status, optional DND while screen-sharing, and a mute-during-meetings toggle ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/mute-notifications-during-a-meeting-in-microsoft-teams-534dd438-5604-44e4-b0d3-9c6167332621), vendor).

*Gmail.* Google states its importance signals plainly — whom you email, what you open, what you reply to, keywords in mail you read, what you star or archive — with hover-to-explain and click-to-correct as the training loop ([support](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/186543), vendor). One classifier drives three surfaces: Priority Inbox layout ([support](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/11349123), vendor), mobile "High priority only" notifications (vendor: [Android help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/1075549) and the [Workspace blog](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2018/06/gmail-ios-high-priority-notifications.html), which frames it as overload relief), and nudges — unanswered received mail and unreplied sent mail resurface at the top of the inbox ("Received 3 days ago. Reply?"), both settings on by default (settings [vendor](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6585); on-by-default corroborated [third-party](https://www.howtogeek.com/750967/how-to-disable-email-reminder-nudges-in-gmail/)).

*Outlook / Viva.* Scheduled send is server-side ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/mail/delay-or-schedule-sending-email-messages-in-outlook), vendor); Viva Insights adds suggestions keyed to the *recipient's* working hours — explicitly framed as reducing disruption to colleagues ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/schedule-send-in-outlook), vendor). The Viva focus plan auto-books up to two hours of focus time per day, sets a "Focusing" presence, and silences Teams chat, priority contacts excepted ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/focus-with-viva-insights-41e6274d-a1ac-42a5-8e9e-ac432e0447c9), vendor). Viva quiet time mutes Outlook and Teams mobile on one shared schedule and pairs it with "personalized insights on how consistently you're disconnecting" ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/quiet-time-in-viva-insights-ec70888d-8840-4f20-9819-af6bfc17e143), vendor).

## 3. Notification inboxes in work tools

The pull channel that absorbs what didn't interrupt is now standard equipment, and the tools differ mainly in where they put the tuning surface.

*GitHub.* The structural split is participating (auto-subscribed to a thread by commenting or being @mentioned) vs watching (subscribed to a whole repository), each routable separately to web inbox, email, or both; four watch levels per repository — all activity, releases only, custom event picks, ignore; and per-organisation email routing ([configuring notifications](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/managing-subscriptions-and-notifications-on-github/setting-up-notifications/configuring-notifications), vendor). Inbox triage: Done (retained about five months), Saved (kept indefinitely), Unsubscribe — which silences a thread *until direct address re-subscribes you* (@mention, team mention, review request) — plus built-in views by reason and up to 15 custom filters ([managing the inbox](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/managing-subscriptions-and-notifications-on-github/viewing-and-triaging-notifications/managing-notifications-from-your-inbox), vendor). There is no snooze: Saved is a shelf, not a timer.

*Linear.* The inbox "shows in-app notifications for work that needs attention"; auto-subscription on create/assign/mention; triage verbs are snooze, delete, mark read, unsubscribe, all keyboard-driven ([Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/inbox), vendor). Arrival is not configurable — all subscribed activity lands, capped at 2,000 open notifications. Linear's tuning surface is which triage verbs exist, not which events fire.

*Figma.* Bell-plus-red-dot notification centre for comments, invitations, and requests; email granularity for file comments (all / mentions and replies only / none); desktop and push channels off until enabled; the in-app modal itself cannot be disabled ([help centre](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039813234-Manage-your-notification-preferences), vendor).

*Notion.* Sidebar inbox aggregating mentions, replies, assignments, reminders, and invitations; per-page subscription levels (All updates / Important updates / Replies and @mentions); mobile push, desktop push, email, and Slack as separate toggles ([help](https://www.notion.com/help/updates-and-notifications), vendor). Two defaults are notable: email is suppressed while you are active in the app, and push is debounced — desktop push after roughly ten seconds, mobile push only after about five minutes of inactivity ([notification settings](https://www.notion.com/help/notification-settings), vendor). The channels are explicitly ordered so the interruptive one fires only if the ambient one went unseen.

*Convergence within the area.* These tools converge on interrupt-for-direct-address, inbox-for-ambient — GitHub encodes it structurally (participating vs watching; Unsubscribe re-armed only by direct address), Notion temporally (escalate only if unseen), Linear and Figma by auto-subscription rule. The shared vocabulary is borrowed from email: inbox, subscribe, mention, snooze, Done, archive, mark read.

## 4. Badging and unread marks

Apple's badge doctrine is narrow: "Use a badge only to show people how many unread notifications they have", never for other numbers; don't make badging the only channel, because "people can turn off badging for your app"; don't mimic a badge with custom UI ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications), vendor). iOS gives a per-app Badges toggle ([support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108781), vendor), and a Focus can hide Home Screen badges wholesale (shipped setting; walkthrough [third-party](https://www.howtogeek.com/749698/how-to-hide-app-notification-badges-in-focus-mode-on-iphone/)). Android defaults to a dot without a number; developers can suppress the dot per channel, and Google's own doc recommends doing so for ongoing notifications and calendar reminders ([developer docs](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/badges), vendor) — the dot-not-count default is itself a restraint decision relative to iOS.

Slack is the clearest shipped grading of the mark itself: a red badge with a number only for things addressed to you (DMs, mentions, keywords, followed threads), a plain dot for mere unread activity, and a preference to turn the dot off entirely ([notifications guide](https://slack.com/help/articles/360025446073-Guide-to-Slack-notifications), vendor; the dot-off preference confirmed in a [vendor support tweet](https://x.com/slackhq/status/991717446900953089)).

The badge-fatigue literature is thinner than expected. Braze — a customer-engagement vendor telling its own customers to restrain themselves — argues "red dot blindness" by analogy to banner blindness and recommends sending low-priority alerts with no sound and no badge ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/beware-red-dot-badging), vendor blog, no data). Engagement figures circulating for badges (16–25% open-rate lifts) trace only to unverified Medium pieces; treat them as folklore. No well-documented case of a major product removing badges outright with an announcement was found — the verifiable evidence is grading (Slack's dot vs number), suppression options, and platform defaults that carry less information.

## 5. Reminders and scheduled self-interruption

Snooze is now the standard verb for turning an incoming item into a scheduled self-interruption, and the preset menus converge hard. Gmail snooze moves mail to a Snoozed section and returns it at the chosen time ([support](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/7622010), vendor); its presets — later today, tomorrow 8am, this weekend, next Monday 8am — are documented only by third parties reading the UI ([mailmeteor](https://mailmeteor.com/blog/snooze-in-gmail)), and the menu cannot be customised. Slack's "Remind me about this" offers 20 minutes / 1 hour / 3 hours / tomorrow / next week plus custom ([help centre](https://slack.com/help/articles/208423427-Set-a-reminder), vendor confirms list-plus-custom; enumeration third-party — and the presets were hard-coded to 9am anchors until 2020, [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/2020/04/15/slack-finally-lets-us-set-custom-message-reminder-times-because-not-everyone-starts-work-at-9am/)); `/remind` takes natural language but refuses sub-day recurrence. GitHub's inbox has no snooze at all — Saved is untimed — though separate "scheduled reminders" deliver pending review requests to Slack at chosen times ([docs](https://docs.github.com/en/subscriptions-and-notifications/how-tos/managing-your-scheduled-reminders), vendor). Android has OS-level per-notification snooze (15/30 minutes, 1/2 hours), but it must be enabled first ([Google help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9079661), vendor); a third-party headline calling it "the best Android feature you forgot about" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/03/19/android-notification-snoozing-how-to-use/)) is itself weak evidence the affordance goes undiscovered.

Follow-up nudging is asymmetric across vendors: Gmail's is ambient and on by default (both directions — reply to this, follow up on that); Outlook's equivalent is a paid Copilot capability, prompt-driven rather than ambient ([support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/outlook/triage-email-with-microsoft-365-copilot-in-outlook), vendor). Briefly, on calendars: Google Calendar's default event notification is 10 minutes before (UI-observed, community-confirmed); Apple Reminders triggers on time, location, or car pairing, firing on whichever comes first ([support](https://support.apple.com/guide/reminders/add-dates-or-locations-to-reminders-remnd4b206fb/mac), vendor).

## 6. AI-agent-era attention surfaces

*Agent completion.* The agentic coding products converge on exactly two alert-worthy events — done and needs-input — and stay quiet otherwise; progress is pull-based. Claude Code fires a notification "when Claude finishes a task or pauses for a permission prompt, and you appear to be away from the terminal" ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/terminal-config), vendor) — the only presence-gated notification found in this survey; desktop notification by default only in three terminals, terminal bell or a `Notification` hook elsewhere. Codex CLI's `notify` hook receives a single event, `agent-turn-complete` (vendor-adjacent repo config; guides [third-party](https://www.aidonenow.com/codex-cli-notifications)) — and the CLI shipped with no notification at all, users filing feature requests to get one ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/4998), third-party evidence of the gap). Codex cloud tasks reply into the originating Slack thread and post completed work as pull requests ([vendor docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/third-party/slack)); enterprise admins can restrict it to links only. Devin lives in Slack threads with a status chip — working / blocked / done — a live worklog, and opt-in private DMs for status updates; actors can tell it `sleep` or `mute` ([Devin docs](https://docs.devin.ai/integrations/slack), vendor). GitHub's Copilot coding agent is the purest reuse of existing machinery: it opens a draft PR, works in commits, "then add[s] you as a reviewer when it has finished, triggering a notification" ([docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/using-github-copilot/coding-agent/using-copilot-to-work-on-an-issue), vendor) — completion is literally a review request. Cursor's cloud agents mark Slack progress with emoji reactions (running / completed / failed) and notify in-thread when the PR is ready ([docs](https://cursor.com/docs/integrations/slack), vendor). None of these products invented a new notification surface; the PR, the Slack thread, and the terminal bell carry it all. A recurring rough edge: the alert layer is the part that fails silently — Codex mobile pushes not delivered ([issue](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/32908)), Cursor Slack notifications not firing ([forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/no-slack-notification-background-agent/152122)), both third-party bug reports.

*AI notification summaries.* Apple Intelligence summarises long or stacked notifications on the lock screen and floats Priority Notifications to the top ([support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/summarize-notifications-reduce-interruptions-iph1fbe7d2b9/ios), vendor), shipped default-on with iOS 18.1. In December 2024 it summarised BBC News notifications as "Luigi Mangione shoots himself" — false — and the BBC complained; other errors credited a darts title before the final and outed Rafael Nadal ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/12/17/apple_intelligence_bbc_complaint), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/apples-ai-disastrously-rewrote-a-bbc-headline-to-say-luigi-mangione-shot-himself-2000538599), third-party; BBC's own pages block crawlers). Apple's retreat in iOS 18.3 beta: summaries disabled entirely for the News & Entertainment category, all summaries restyled in italics, a "beta, may contain errors" warning at enable time, per-app disable from the lock screen ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/16/apple-pauses-ai-notification-summaries-for-news-after-generating-false-alerts), third-party). iOS 26 brought news summaries back opt-in per category behind a red warning — "Summarization may change the meaning of original headlines. Verify information." ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/07/22/ios-26-beta-4-notification-summaries/), third-party). Samsung's One UI 8.5 shipped the same feature shape later, with per-app exclusions from day one (all [third-party reporting](https://www.sammobile.com/news/one-ui-8-5-ai-notification-summaries-galaxy-devices/)).

*Slack AI.* Recaps — "a new recap every morning with what you missed from the previous day" — plus channel/thread summaries, pitched as catching up on channels "you want to follow but don't need to read in real time" ([vendor](https://slack.com/help/articles/25076892548883-Guide-to-AI-features-in-Slack)). Initially a paid add-on, now folded into tiers with Recaps gated to Business+/Enterprise+ — the digest, the attention-management feature proper, sits in the upper tiers. Structurally the same move as Apple's: compress the stream rather than reduce it.

*Proactive AI.* ChatGPT Tasks ([help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-scheduled-tasks-in-chatgpt), vendor) and Gemini Scheduled Actions ([help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16316416), vendor) are AIs that initiate notifications on a schedule; both launch capped and opt-in, and monitoring-style tasks notify only when there is something to report — notify-on-exception as a marketed pattern.

## 7. Anti-patterns and public pushback

*Growth abuse.* LinkedIn paid $13M to settle a class action over "Add Connections", which sent up to two unconsented reminder emails under the member's own name to contacts who ignored the first invite ([Time](https://time.com/4062519/linkedn-spam-settlement/), third-party) — escalating unanswered outreach carried a legal cost, not just a reputational one. Duolingo is the self-aware case: a bandit algorithm picks each day's reminder from ~200M notification observations with explicit novelty decay ([vendor blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/)), the pushiness is marketed as a joke ([Duolingo Push](https://www.push.duolingo.com/), vendor), and reminders stop after being ignored — "Hi, this is Duo. These reminders don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them for now." ([archived email](https://reallygoodemails.com/emails/these-reminders-dont-seem-to-be-working), third-party archive; no first-party statement of the exact trigger window was found). The stop-notice is simultaneously a de-escalation policy and, per third-party marketing accounts, its most effective notification.

*Permission priming.* Apple's App Review guideline 4.5.4: notifications "must not be required for the app to function", marketing pushes need explicit opt-in with an in-app opt-out, and "abuse of these services may result in revocation of your privileges" ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), vendor) — marketing pushes were banned outright until March 2020. Apple's provisional authorisation (iOS 12+) delivers quietly on trial, with Keep / Turn off inline, before any prompt (vendor feature; current HIG wording unverified, corroborated [third-party](https://www.airship.com/blog/apple-push-notification-guidelines-update/)). Google's web guidance endorses the double-permission pre-prompt and warns "the worst thing you can do is to show the permission dialog to users as soon as they land on your site" ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/push-notifications-permissions-ux), vendor). Duolingo's own copy test — benefit-framed opt-in copy lifting German-learner opt-in 8% ([vendor blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/copy-testing-experiments)) — shows priming shipped as growth practice, not just guidance.

*Repositioning on attention.* Basecamp's "Work Can Wait": a per-person work-hours schedule outside which notifications are silenced, with the always-on alternative explicitly labelled "24/7/365" ([launch post](https://signalvnoise.com/svn3/basecamp-3-work-can-wait/); [help docs](https://3.basecamp-help.com/article/86-how-notifications-work), both vendor). Fried frames the boundary as the tool's responsibility, not the user's discipline. one sec intercepts app launches with a breathing pause and a dismiss option; unusually, its effect is peer-reviewed — a 6-week PNAS study (n=280, co-authored by the app's developer) found roughly 57% fewer problematic social-app opens ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2213114120), academic/third-party). Apple's Screen Time is the platform-level version: Downtime, App Limits — ignorable by default, hardenable by choice ([support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-started-with-screen-time-iphbfa595995/ios), vendor). Freedom and Opal occupy the same commercial niche.

## Convergences

- A small fixed vocabulary of interruption levels, imposed by the platform on developers: four on iOS (passive / active / time-sensitive / critical), five on Android (Urgent / High / Medium / Low / None). Both gate the top rung — Apple by hand-approved entitlement, Android by making the actor the grantor of DND breakthrough.
- The actor override always wins. Android states it outright: the developer's importance value is only a starting default; the actor can retune every channel and the developer cannot raise it back in code. iOS time-sensitive is one tap to revoke per app.
- Interrupt for direct address, inbox for the rest. GitHub (participating vs watching), Linear, Figma, and Notion all auto-subscribe on mention/assignment/ownership and let ambient activity pool in a pull inbox. GitHub's Unsubscribe re-arms only on direct address.
- Escalate only if unseen. Notion sends email only when you are not active in the app and debounces push behind the in-app inbox; Slack delays mobile push until desktop goes inactive; Claude Code notifies only when you appear to be away from the terminal. Three independent vendors ordering channels so the interruptive one fires only after the ambient one failed.
- Every DND ships with a designed, rate-limited pressure valve: Slack's once-per-day "notify anyway", Teams urgent messages (re-ping every 2 minutes for 20) and priority contacts, iOS Focus's "notify anyway" for senders and time-sensitive for apps, Viva focus time's priority-contact exception. Vendors treat breakthrough as a channel to design, not a failure of the budget.
- Time-boxed notification budgets with the same shape everywhere: allowed-hours window plus whole quiet days (Slack notification schedule, Teams/Viva quiet time, Basecamp work-hours, Android Bedtime mode).
- Snooze-as-self-scheduling with converged anchors: later today, tomorrow morning 8/9am, next Monday — Gmail snooze, Slack reminders, Slack scheduled send, and Outlook's recipient-working-hours suggestions all steer deferred interruptions to the start of the next working block.
- Badges graded, not binary: nothing / dot / count, with the count reserved for directed communication (Slack ships this explicitly; Android's default dot-without-number is the platform version).
- Agentic products notify on exactly two events — done and needs-input — stay quiet otherwise, and reuse existing channels (the PR review request, the Slack thread, the terminal bell) rather than inventing notification surfaces.
- AI summaries compress the stream rather than reduce it (Apple, Samsung, Slack), mark AI output typographically, and offer per-app or per-channel exclusion.

## What this challenges

- Default-on AI summarisation of notifications has failed publicly and forced a retreat. Apple shipped notification summaries default-on, generated false headlines under the BBC's name, paused the whole News & Entertainment category, and re-shipped opt-in behind a red warning telling actors to verify. Any sequence step that leans on AI compression of the interrupt stream has to carry that provenance obligation — the summary must be visibly not the source.
- Defaults carry the weight, and vendors will not say so. No vendor publishes adoption or customisation rates for its attention controls; several will not even publish their default values (Slack's 8am–10pm schedule, Gmail's snooze presets, Calendar's 10-minute lead are documented only by third parties reading the UI). Meanwhile the defaults themselves are opinionated and uneditable in places — Gmail's snooze menu cannot be customised, Slack's reminder presets were hard-coded to 9am anchors until 2020. The tempo is the vendor's, not the actor's, more than the settings surfaces suggest. Circumstantial evidence points the same way for tuned controls generally: Android's notification snooze ships disabled and third-party coverage calls it a forgotten feature.
- The layers fight. Teams priority access — the designed breakthrough — is defeated by Windows OS-level DND, on Microsoft's own Q&A. Stacked budgets (OS mode over app schedule over channel setting) do not compose cleanly, and the actor sees only that the urgent message never arrived.
- The alert layer is the part that fails silently. Codex CLI shipped an agent with no completion notification at all; Codex mobile pushes and Cursor Slack notifications both have open not-delivered bug reports; Codex's notify hook ignores errors from the command it runs. In the agent-era products, notification is the least tested path — the opposite of what a sequence built around announce-on-completion would hope.
- Deliberate de-escalation is not niche. Duolingo — the canonical pushy notifier — ships an algorithmic backoff and announces it to the actor, and the stop-notice reportedly outperforms the reminders it replaces. Apple argues restraint from revocation risk in its own developer guidance. The threat model shipping vendors design against is not missed messages; it is the actor turning everything off.
- Badge fatigue is folklore, grading is fact. The quantified badge-engagement claims do not survive sourcing, and no major product has removed badges with an announcement. What actually ships is a graded mark (dot vs count) and per-app kill switches — evidence for designing the mark's grammar, not for or against the mark.
