## What the lineage shows

Three of Alexander's books sit under four of the five papers, which is what a
pattern-sequence literature should look like and confirms the note's reading
that "the plan's move is precedented on both flanks". The flanks are visible in
the ancestors: the urban-design papers reach *A New Theory of Urban Design* and
*The Oregon Experiment*, the software papers reach the PLoPD volumes and the
Gang of Four, and only Alexander himself is common to both. *The Nature of
Order* appears under two, which is a small but real signal that the later
Alexander is already in this conversation rather than waiting outside it.

Two descendants are worth reading before the sequence work settles. *The
Mathematical Structure of Alexander's A Pattern Language* analyses which
patterns are invariant across the language — a structural question adjacent to
what a sequence is for. And *Donor Patterns: A Modular Structure for Sharing
Knowledge* proposes a modular unit for passing patterns between languages,
which is the composition problem the plan is working on, named differently.

The critical-response survey (*Analysing, Mapping and Classifying the Critical
Response*) is the other useful find: the note's own *What this challenges*
section works from the papers' internal arguments, and that survey is a
catalogue of the objections from outside.
