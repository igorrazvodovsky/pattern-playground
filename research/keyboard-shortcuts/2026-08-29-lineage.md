## What this settles

Two of the note's three named expectations hold. *From Sufficient to Efficient
Usage* (Bhavnani & John 1997) is confirmed as a convergent ancestor, exactly as
the note predicted. The lineage root is confirmed too, but it is Card, Moran &
Newell rather than the note's guess of Nielsen's *Usability Engineering* — the
accelerator guidance in Nielsen is downstream of this line, not its source.
Carroll & Rosson's paradox of the active user does not appear in either
bibliography, so the satisficing framing the note leans on is not what these two
papers cite for it.

The descendant side answers a loose end the note left open. It listed a Radboud
University repository item, *Satisficing and the Use of Keyboard Shortcuts*,
with "authorship unconfirmed" and a hedged date. It has an OpenAlex record,
dated 2013, and it cites both of the run's anchor papers — so it belongs in the
line rather than beside it, and it can be cited properly now.

*Supporting Novice to Expert Transitions in User Interfaces* is the heaviest
descendant, and it also turns up as a convergent ancestor in the
[editing-in-place](../editing-in-place/) lineage. The novice-to-expert
transition is a shared concern of the two folders.
