## Convergent findings

### A. Incremental formalisation is a move with a stable, citable shape — and the schema itself has a lifecycle

Three papers converge on the same division of labour: the actor enters content informally; the system recognises implicit structure (spatial, textual, repetitional) and *offers* formalisation; the actor accepts, adapts, or defers, keeping the informal form legitimate throughout. Marshall & Shipman supply the origin (VIKI's pattern recognition over spatial layouts), Formality Considered Harmful the rationale and failure modes (premature structure, cognitive overhead, tacit knowledge), and How Notations Evolve the extension the library's formality page doesn't yet have: the *schema being formalised into* is itself invented, diverges, and hardens over time — the Notion page-that-becomes-a-database is a notation moving from incubation toward institutionalisation. Disagreement: none on the shape; the open edge is how much recognition to automate (Formality Considered Harmful warns inferred structure must stay cheap to reject — which `formality.mdx` already records as "transient and lightweight").

Papers: Marshall & Shipman 1995; Shipman & Marshall 1999; How Notations Evolve 2026.

*Draft project takeaway*: incremental formalisation earns a pattern node as the *move* enacting the formality quality — informal entry, recognised structure, offered promotion — with type-switching-over-persistent-data as its block-editor instance and schema lifecycle as a named force; the quality page keeps the lens, the move page owns the interaction.

### B. The workspace-as-document paradigm has an academic name: computational media / substrates

Webstrates names precisely the thing the Notion/Obsidian experience instantiates: a *substrate* — an artefact that "acts as application or document depending upon use", malleable, shareable, distributable, in the lineage of Kay's dynamic media and Boxer-style reconstructible media. The Gradual GenUI paper (and, abstract-only, the GenUI technical-debt paper's warning about "lack of adaptable data formats") shows the same paradigm being renegotiated in AI-era terms. This answers Q3's fork unexpectedly: the paradigm is *real and named*, but the name describes a *design material* more than an activity — which in this library's terms suggests a **foundation** (like prose, data) rather than, or in addition to, an activity-level pattern. The library's own rule (foundation = design material/substrate) fits almost verbatim.

Papers: Webstrates 2015; Gradual Generation of UIs 2026 (supporting, abstract-only: Hidden Technical Debt in GenUI 2026).

*Draft project takeaway*: the Notion/Obsidian distinctiveness enters the library as a substrate foundation (working name: *computational medium* or *substrate*) that block composition, incremental formalisation, and transclusion instantiate — not as a single activity-level pattern; whether a thin activity node sits above it is a separate, deferrable call.

### C. Transclusion is one vision, several practices — and its cost lands on provenance

Nelson's primitive (same content knowably in multiple places) fans out in practice into embed, synced-edit, and query-materialised views, and the one quantitative study retrieved finds real content-affecting transclusion far rarer than assumed (5% vs 61%) with the burden falling on people verifying where content came from. The move is real but its failure mode is *provenance opacity*: the reader can't tell transcluded from local, the editor can't tell what an edit will ripple into. Nelson's "knowably" is the load-bearing word modern implementations drop.

Papers: Nelson 1995; Wikidata Transclusion 2020.

*Draft project takeaway*: block reference / transclusion is a seed-grade move whose stated centre should be Nelson's *knowable* multiplicity — visible provenance and ripple-scope — not the syncing mechanics; evidence of actual usage patterns is thin and worth watching.

### D. Vocabulary hazard: "block-based" is an occupied term

A 152-paper systematic review defines "block-based environments" as Scratch-style visual programming. The library's page name collides with an established, unrelated research corpus — a designer searching the literature from this page lands in the wrong field. This independently supports renaming the pattern by its move (the composition of documents from typed, rearrangeable units) and keeping "block-based editor" as the component/mechanism name only.

Papers: Block-based environments SLR 2021.

*Draft project takeaway*: keep "block-based editor" as the Storybook mechanism name; name the language-side move without "block-based" as its head term to avoid colliding with the visual-programming corpus.
