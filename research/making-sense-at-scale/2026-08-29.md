## Context

An actor faces material that has accumulated past any single framing. They view it at different levels, externalise judgements onto it (tags, annotations, notes), and curate views that carry a framing for later work or other people. The corpus fragments: a lens group with no ordering edges (focus-and-context, semantic-zoom, text-lense), an externalising fragment (annotation → commenting, tag → grouping), and a curation chain (data-view → purpose-keyed-view → problem-curated-view). Generic mechanisms shared by most tools, not specialised analyst tooling.

Questions:

1. Are foraging and sensemaking separable activities with an order between them, or one interleaved loop? (Decides one sequence or two.)
2. Is the lens choice (overview+detail, zooming, focus+context, generated summary) a chain of steps or alternatives at one choice point?
3. Does externalising (tagging, annotating) precede or follow viewing at scale?
4. Do generated summaries add a step or compress the foraging loop into a delegated pass?

## Canon reads (named-and-grounded; question 1–3 literature)

- *The sensemaking process and leverage points for analyst technology* (Pirolli & Card, Intl. Conf. on Intelligence Analysis 2005) — [S2 record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6b54379043d0ee28bea45555f481af1a693c16c). Provenance: slide-deck + secondary-source grounded, not a full-text read. The model: a chain of increasingly structured representations (external sources → shoebox → evidence file → schema → hypotheses → presentation) organised into two loops — a *foraging loop* (seek, search and filter, read and extract) and a *sensemaking loop* (iterative development of a mental model from the schema that fits the evidence). The chain is drawn with arrows in both directions at every link (bottom-up building, top-down re-search for evidence and relations), and the two loops overlap on the middle representations: extraction feeds schemas, schemas redirect extraction. Speaks to Q1. Transfer: partial — intelligence analysis is the source domain, but the model is explicitly offered as general sensemaking; the corpus should take the loop structure, not the analyst artifacts.
- *The cost structure of sensemaking* (Russell, Stefik, Pirolli & Card, CHI '93) — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/169059.169209). Abstract-grounded. Defines sensemaking as "the process of searching for a representation and encoding data in that representation to answer task-specific questions". The learning loop: search for a good representation, encode data in it, identify what does not fit ("residue"), and use the residue to re-derive the representation. Speaks to Q1 and Q3: encoding (externalising into a schema) is inside the loop from the start, and the schema is revised by what resists it — neither viewing-then-coding nor coding-then-viewing.
- *Making sense of sensemaking 2: a macrocognitive model* (Klein, Moon & Hoffman, IEEE Intelligent Systems 2006) — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/MIS.2006.100). Abstract/secondary-grounded. The data-frame model: data and frame determine each other; sensemaking proceeds by elaborating a frame, questioning it, and reframing when anomalies accumulate. Speaks to Q1: a second, independently derived model that refuses a phase order between gathering and framing.
- *A review of overview+detail, zooming, and focus+context interfaces* (Cockburn, Karlson & Bederson, ACM Computing Surveys 2008) — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1456650.1456652). *Full-text read* (introduction, §8 Summary). Categorises the lens schemes "according to the interface mechanisms used to separate and blend views": overview+detail "uses a spatial separation between focused and contextual views; zooming, which uses a temporal separation; focus+context, which minimizes the seam between views by displaying the focus within the context; and cue-based techniques which selectively highlight or suppress items". The summary's verdicts: "None of these approaches is ideal" — each buys its capability with a distinct cost (split-attention assimilation, pre/post-zoom reorientation, distortion). "The question then becomes which style of interface, or which combination of styles, offers the greatest performance advantages, for what tasks, and under what conditions? The current state of research fails to provide clear guidelines"; efficiency "is dependent on many factors, particularly the nature of the users' task"; "Many systems support combinations of these techniques"; "it is important for designers to use their own judgment as to which approach to pursue". Speaks to Q2 decisively. Transfer: strong — the review is deliberately tool-generic.
- *The eyes have it: a task by data type taxonomy for information visualizations* (Shneiderman, IEEE VL 1996) — [open PDF](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/shneiderman96eyes.pdf). Abstract/front-matter grounded. The visual information seeking mantra — "overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand" — is an ordering claim, but over the *actor's walk within one surface*, not over which lens mechanism to build; later evaluation (Craft & Cairns 2005, [record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4175429_Beyond_guidelines_What_can_we_learn_from_the_Visual_Information_Seeking_Mantra)) found its status as a design guideline asserted more than validated. Speaks to Q2: the ordering the mantra carries belongs in a step's gloss (material at scale opens with an overview), not in edges between the lens patterns.
- *Formality considered harmful* (Shipman & Marshall, CSCW journal 1999) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1008716330212). Abstract-grounded. Users are unwilling or unable to make structure explicit up front; the named problems are cognitive overhead, tacit knowledge, *premature structure*, and situational structure; the proposed remedies are incremental formalisation and recognising undeclared structure. Speaks to Q3: a schema demanded before the material is encountered is a documented failure mode; the corpus already carries this as incremental-formalisation, and the sequence should inherit it as the marking region's principle.
- *Exploring the relationship between personal and public annotations* (Marshall & Brush, JCDL 2004) — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/exploring-the-relationship-between-personal-and-public-annotations/). Abstract-grounded. "Only a small fraction of personal annotations are used in initiating and responding to related on-line discussions", and the ones shared "tended to correspond to explicit marginalia; much effort is still put into rendering both the content and anchors of these annotations intelligible to others". Speaks to Q3 and the annotation → commenting edge: the private mark and the audience-directed note are different artifacts, and the move between them is real, effortful work — a step, not a restyling.
- *Personal information management: from information consumption to curation* (Whittaker, ARIST 2011) — [Wiley](https://asistdl.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/aris.2011.1440450108). Abstract-grounded. Reframes PIM as curation: information as "a personal resource that we keep, manage, and (sometimes repeatedly) exploit". Keeping decisions happen at encounter; management is deferred and minimised; exploitation is where the kept material pays. Speaks to Q3: lightweight keeping at the moment of encounter, deliberate organising later, matches the incremental-formalisation reading.
- *Distributed sensemaking* (Fisher, Counts & Kittur, CHI 2012) — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2207676.2207711), [open PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/DistributedSensemaking_CHI2012.pdf). Abstract-grounded. Users given previous users' knowledge maps made sense faster and better; "the organization of content was more useful to subsequent users than the content itself"; schemas iterate across people in a "virtuous cycle". Speaks to the curation region's purpose: a curated framing is a hand-off artifact, and the framing outvalues the material it frames.

## Retrieved papers (arxiv; question 4 mostly)

- *Sensecape: enabling multilevel exploration and sensemaking with large language models* (UIST 2023) — [arxiv 2305.11483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11483). Designed so users can "manage the complexity of information through multilevel abstraction" and "seamlessly switch between foraging and sensemaking"; the study found users "structure their knowledge hierarchically, thanks to the externalization of levels of abstraction". Speaks to Q1, Q4. Transfer: strong — the design target is the switch cost between the two loops, treating them as modes of one activity.
- *Fuse: in-situ sensemaking support in the browser* (UIST 2022) — [arxiv 2208.14861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14861). "Humans are able to fluidly iterate on collecting and organizing information in their minds, existing tools and approaches introduce significant friction"; couples "low-cost collection with lightweight organization" in one always-available surface; 22-month deployment. Speaks to Q3. Transfer: strong — browser-generic.
- *Wigglite: low-cost information collection and triage* (UIST 2022) — [arxiv 2208.00496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00496). One gesture collects an item *and* encodes a judgement on it (urgency, pro/con) without leaving the reading flow; externalised mental context "helps scaffold subsequent deeper exploration". Speaks to Q3: marking rides the encounter when its cost approaches zero. Transfer: strong.
- *Augmenting expert cognition in the age of generative AI* (2025) — [arxiv 2503.24334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24334). Across two document-work studies, "experts welcome AI assistance with repetitive information foraging tasks" but "prefer to retain control over complex synthesis and interpretation"; calls for "verification mechanisms that help users calibrate their reliance". Speaks to Q4. Transfer: strong.
- *Orca: browsing at scale through user-driven and AI-facilitated orchestration* (2025) — [arxiv 2505.22831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22831). Fully automated browsing "diminish[es] user agency and hinder[s] contextual understanding"; AI-augmented foraging instead produced "an increased 'appetite' for information foraging" and "more flexible sensemaking". Speaks to Q4: delegating the gather widens the intake; the loop's shape survives. Transfer: partial (research prototype).
- *Supporting sensemaking of large language model outputs at scale* (CHI 2024) — [arxiv 2401.13726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13726). Presenting many LLM responses at once with computed similarities and differences "make[s] tasks previously considered to be too difficult... now tractable". Speaks to Q4's reflexive case: generated output is itself accumulated material, and the same walk re-enters over it. Transfer: partial.
- *The design space of recent AI-assisted research tools for ideation, sensemaking, and scientific creativity* (2025) — [arxiv 2502.16291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16291). Thirteen-tool survey; recommendations are "user agency and control, differentiating divergent/convergent thinking support, ensuring adaptability, and prioritizing transparency/accuracy". Speaks to Q4 as a cross-check on the same seams. Transfer: partial (research-tool corpus).

## Convergent findings

### 1. Foraging and sensemaking are one interleaved loop; the split is analytic, not sequential

Three independently derived models — the learning loop (Russell et al.), the two-loop notional model (Pirolli & Card), and the data-frame model (Klein et al.) — all name two activities and all refuse an order between them: representations are chosen early and revised by residue, the loop arrows run both directions, data and frame co-determine. The LLM-era work builds on the same premise from the design side: Sensecape's contribution is making the *switch* between foraging and sensemaking cheap, which only matters if actors switch constantly. For the map's open question this is the same answer attention and tempo got: one sequence, because it is one loop. The decision order a sequence records is which capabilities to provide (a lens, a marking surface, a kept framing) — the actor's runtime walk through them loops freely, as it does in every authored sequence.
Papers: Russell et al. 1993; Pirolli & Card 2005; Klein et al. 2006; Sensecape.

### 2. The lens is a choice point per surface, not a chain of steps

Cockburn et al. is unambiguous: the four schemes are competing mechanisms for one problem (working at focus and context together), distinguished by *how they separate or blend views*; none is ideal, the evidence hangs on the task, combinations are common, and the field declines to give an ordering. This is the same shape as structuring the space's navigation-model choice point: a decision taken per surface, arms non-exclusive, task-keyed. The lens group's lack of `precedes` edges in the corpus was the graph telling the truth. What ordering exists in the literature — Shneiderman's mantra — orders the *actor's first minutes on one surface* (overview before detail), which belongs in the choice point's gloss as an obligation any chosen lens must discharge, not in edges among the lens patterns.
Papers: Cockburn et al. 2008; Shneiderman 1996 (with Craft & Cairns 2005 on its evidential status).

### 3. Marking rides the encounter; schematising is deliberately late

The convergence runs from 1999 to 2022: demanding structure up front fails (premature structure, cognitive overhead), keeping decisions happen at the moment of encounter and organising is deferred (Whittaker), and the successful designs collapse capture-plus-judgement into one gesture inside the reading flow (Wigglite, Fuse). So the externalising region opens with lightweight, one-gesture marks (a tag applied, a passage highlighted, a working note) as near-constituents of encountering material, and the deliberate act — building the vocabulary, grouping the marks, promoting structure — is a *later* step governed by incremental formalisation. Answer to Q3: neither code-first nor view-first; mark cheaply throughout, schematise deliberately afterwards.
Papers: Shipman & Marshall 1999; Whittaker 2011; Wigglite; Fuse; Russell et al. 1993.

### 4. The audience-directed note is a separate step from the private mark

Marshall & Brush's finding — few personal annotations get shared, and sharing takes real transformation work — grounds the corpus's `annotation precedes commenting` edge as a genuine decision boundary: the mark made for oneself and the note directed at an audience differ in content and anchor, and the move between them is authored work. Distributed sensemaking extends the same boundary to framings: what transfers to the next actor is the *organisation*, more than the content, so the curated view directed at others (problem-curated-view's territory) is the region's exit, facing working together.
Papers: Marshall & Brush 2004; Fisher, Counts & Kittur 2012.

### 5. Generation compresses the foraging leg; it does not add a step

Experts delegate the repetitive gather and keep synthesis; AI-facilitated foraging widens the intake without changing the loop's shape; a generated overview is a lens face (the summary as a way of viewing the pile), and its debts are the familiar ones — agency, verification, transparency — which are calls into delegating work and calibrating reliance, not new steps here. The one genuinely new fact is reflexive: generated output accumulates into material the same walk re-enters over.
Papers: Augmenting Expert Cognition; Orca; Sensecape; Supporting Sensemaking of LLM Outputs at Scale; design-space survey.

## What this challenges

- The map entry's guess that the evaluation group "probably joins here as the lens steps" is half wrong: the lenses join as *one choice point*, not steps. Finding 2 is direct on this.
- The map's open question — one sequence or a split into sense-making and understanding-at-scale — is answered *one sequence* by finding 1. Understanding-at-scale (the lens work) is a capability choice inside the loop, not a separable activity that could head its own walk.
- The `tag → grouping` fragment reads in the entry as internal spine, but grouping is already a step of composing views' framing cluster. Finding 3 suggests the marking region's product (the tag vocabulary) is *spent* by composing views — a hands-off between sequences, not an internal edge. To classify at the sitting.
