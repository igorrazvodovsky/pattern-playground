# Quarry read: Working Through Screens — making sense at scale — 2026-08-29

Companion to the same-day literature note and practice survey. *Working Through Screens* (Tabor, 2009) read as the sequence-map plan uses it: a quarry for territory this corpus is missing, not an ordering source — the book organises its 100 considerations for random access on purpose. Read from the chapter notes in the author's resource folder (Dropbox/PARA/3 Resources/Working through screens/), full core texts of the considerations named below.

The considerations that bear on this territory sit in bands F (information representation) and I (volumes of information), with the annotation pair in H and J — not the D/E/H bands the map flags for other missing territories.

## What each contributes

- *F4 Support for visualization at different levels.* The territory's framing consideration: alongside "ground level" item views, "high level 'views from the clouds' looking meaningfully down at different aggregations". Two facts for the sequence. Levels are *interconnected series* — the envisioning questions ask how strata are linked and what transitions clarify their relationships, which is the lens choice's obligation whatever arm is taken. And the skip condition is stated: where "knowledge work revolves around entirely discrete items in clearly articulated processes", higher-level views may not be valued — the condition under which a product skips the lens region entirely.
- *F8 Representational transformations.* Reclassifying and reformatting a display against differing goals, explicitly tied to "specific information seeking... and sense making goals". Its sharpest question marks a boundary the sitting must draw: "at what point might a transformed information representation become an entirely different view of application content?" — the line between composing views' framing cluster (sort, group, filter on one view) and this sequence's kept, purpose-shaped views. It also warns that "transformation of shared representations [can] lead to breakdowns in common ground" — a working-together seam on the curation region.
- *F5 Comparative representations.* Comparison is named as its own representational act: workers compare "manually, without specialized representations for the task, by placing multiple printouts... or other artifacts within their visual field", and tools can automate the comparison and "call out meaningful distinctions". The corpus has no comparison pattern; F5 says the absence is a dimension, not an accident. Candidate finding for the sitting.
- *F9 Simultaneous or sequential use of representations.* Parallel and switched views over the same content. This is composing views' splitting/coupling territory (overview-detail, coordinated-views); it contributes a seam, not a step — the lens choice's overview+detail arm dissolves toward that sequence exactly as the navigation-model's arm already does.
- *I1 Flexible information organization.* The marking region's consideration, and it names both directions the literature note's finding 3 needs: "a starting point of information structure... that users might then customize" (top-down, deferred) and "bottom up organization methods... allowing users to apply meaningful categorization 'along the way' to accomplishing their day to day goals". Organisation is also credited with "promoting implicit communication" — marks in shared material signal without messages, the working-together seam from the marking side.
- *H4 Working annotations.* The private mark: "lightweight, often private annotations... 'work about the work'" tied to specific artifacts, offloading memory, explicitly including marking one's place when interrupted — which is memory and continuity's parked-place fact seen from inside the material. "The shorthand of working annotations may not contain fully formed ideas that are intended for outside interpretation": the private face of the annotation → commenting boundary, agreeing with Marshall & Brush from the design side.
- *J5 Public annotation.* The audience-directed mark: annotations placed "to be viewed by an anticipated 'public'", some valuable "for a set duration", others becoming "essential elements of work artifacts, providing persistent, historical value in organizational memory". Also the attention argument: "real time communication used in place of public annotations may demand receivers' attentions at inappropriate times" — the annotation channel as the non-interrupting alternative, the attention-and-tempo seam stated in 2009.
- *I7 Archived information.* What leaves the view: "workers may come to value the ability to exclude selected older content from their day to day onscreen views", with pathways in, management, search within, and restoration out. For this corpus the archive belongs to memory and continuity's keeping/forgetting territory; the contribution here is the trigger — accumulation degrading the day-to-day view is what makes the actor reach for the archive, so the sequence hands off rather than owning a step.
- *G5 Impromptu tangents and juxtapositions.* Sensemaking work is "improvised, multithreaded"; tools that force "serial paths of action" get worked around. Supports reading the territory as one interleaved loop whose *provision* is sequenced, never its use.

## What the quarry does not give

No ordering — by design. And no generated-material considerations: the book predates the generated overview, the machine-curated view, and the reflexive case of making sense of generated output; those enter only through the same-day literature note and practice survey.
