# Bibliography leads: Maru — making sense at scale — 2026-08-29

Candidate follow-up reads mined from the reference list of *Maru: Information Architecture as a Shared Language for Generating Aligned and Persistent User Interfaces* (Kim, Choi, Min, Yi, Jiang & Kim, UIST '26, [arxiv 2608.25565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25565)). The paper's own topic — persisting user-established structure across generative-UI generations — is a different territory from this sequence, so it is not read here. Its bibliography is used as a 2026-vintage survey of the sensemaking-and-interactive-systems literature, which is the view the same-day literature note could not get: Semantic Scholar was rate-limited, so 1-hop lineage was omitted and the descendant question — where the two-loop model went after 2012 — went unanswered.

> *Extraction note.* A first pass over this bibliography stopped at [103]; the list runs to [111], and the missed tail held one first-rank lead. The entries below are checked against the complete list.

> *Grounding tier.* Everything below is *title, venue and year only*, read off someone else's reference list. This is weaker than the abstract-grounded entries in the same-day literature note and much weaker than its one full-text read. Nothing here is a finding, and nothing here should be cited in pattern or sequence prose until it has been read. The list is a queue.

## Already grounded — cited here as convergence

Five of the same-day note's sources appear in this 2026 bibliography: Russell, Stefik, Pirolli & Card 1993 [84]; Pirolli & Card 2005 [76]; Fuse [45]; Wigglite [53]; Sensecape [92]. Their survival into a current UIST reference list corroborates findings 1, 3 and 5 — the two-loop model, cheap marking at the point of encounter, and generation compressing the foraging leg are the live frame, not a historical one.

One near-miss to keep straight: Maru cites Klein, Moon & Hoffman's *Making sense of sensemaking 1: Alternative perspectives* [43]. The literature note grounds *…sensemaking 2: a macrocognitive model* — same authors, year and venue, different paper. Paper 1 is a lead in its own right, not a duplicate.

## Leads that move an open question

Ranked by which unresolved item they bear on, not by topical proximity.

1. Zhu, Haisfield, Langen & Chan (2024), *Patterns of Hypertext-Augmented Sensemaking*, UIST [111]. A pattern-shaped account of sensemaking, coded by Maru under sensemaking theory rather than systems. Two reasons it leads: a pattern language for this territory written from the hypertext side is a direct comparison for the sequence's own form, and it is the most likely place to find whether the linked representation of a collection — which this corpus has no pattern for — is a mechanism or an artifact of one tool tradition.
2. Hearst & Degler (2013), *Sewing the seams of sensemaking: a practical interface for tagging and organizing saved search results*, HCIR [33]. The note leaves the `tag → grouping` fragment open ("to classify at the sitting"), and the sequence draws it as a hands-off from `marking/1` to composing views via `filtering`. This paper is titled as exactly that seam — tagging and the kept saved search in one interface — so it either grounds the hands-off or complicates it.
3. Sen, Lam, Rashid, Cosley, Frankowski, Osterhouse, Harper & Riedl (2006), *Tagging, communities, vocabulary, evolution*, CSCW [88]. The marking cluster asserts that the vocabulary is flat and grown along the way, and `incremental-formalisation` promotes it late. Nothing in the current notes grounds vocabulary *evolution* empirically — how a grown vocabulary converges or fails to, and what community exposure does to it. This bears on the promotion step's timing.
4. Chang, Hahn & Kittur (2020), *Mesh: Scaffolding comparison tables for online decision making*, UIST [14]. The `comparing` step was authored on a taxonomy (Gleicher et al.) plus a Working Through Screens consideration; the quarry read recorded that the corpus had no comparison pattern and little ground for one. Mesh is comparison at collection scale during a decision, which is the step's situation rather than the visualisation-taxonomy framing it currently carries.
5. Kuznetsov, Liu & Kittur (2024), *Tasks, Time, and Tools: Quantifying Online Sensemaking Efforts Through a Survey-based Study*, [arxiv 2411.07206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07206) [46]. The sequence claims a territory without saying how much of anyone's work it occupies. A quantified base rate would tell whether the presupposition ("material that has outgrown any single framing") is a common situation or a specialist one.
6. Kittur, Peters, Diriye, Telang & Bove (2013), *Costs and benefits of structured information foraging*, CHI [42]. Directly on the cost side of the marking region's premise that a mark deferred is mostly a mark lost — what structuring during foraging actually costs and returns.

## Leads by region

*Lens choice.* Min, Chen, Cao & Xia (2025), *Malleable overview-detail interfaces*, CHI [61], and Min & Xia (2025), *Meridian: A Design Framework for Malleable Overview-Detail Interfaces*, UIST [63] — a current design framework for the arm that dissolves into composing views, which may say something about whether the pairing stays fixed or is itself chosen at runtime. Marchionini (2006), *Exploratory search: from finding to understanding*, CACM [57], and Hoeber (2025), *Design principles for exploratory search interfaces*, CHIIR [35] — the presupposition's "not yet settled enough to be a query" is exploratory search's founding claim, and the 2025 paper is a recent principles synthesis. Pirolli & Card (1995), *Information foraging in information access environments*, CHI [75] — the foraging half of the two-loop model in its own right.

*Generated tellings.* Liu, Wu, Chen, Li, Kittur & Myers (2024), *Selenite: Scaffolding online sensemaking with comprehensive overviews elicited from large language models*, CHI [54] — the generated overview of a whole pile, which is precisely the `text-lense` arm at collection scale. Fok, Lipka, Sun & Siu (2024), *Marco: Supporting business document workflows via collection-centric information foraging with large language models*, CHI [23] — collection-centric rather than document-centric foraging. Fok, Chang, Radensky, Siangliulue, Bragg, Zhang & Weld (2025), *Facets, taxonomies, and syntheses: Navigating structured representations in LLM-assisted literature review*, [arxiv 2504.18496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18496) [22] — generated structure over a collection, which is the marking region's promotion step performed by a model rather than an actor. Kang, Wu, Chang & Kittur (2023), *Synergi: A mixed-initiative system for scholarly synthesis and sensemaking*, UIST [37]. Yun, Feng, Chen, Nikzad & Salehi (2025), *Generative AI in knowledge work: Design implications for data navigation and decision-making*, CHI [104] — design implications rather than a prototype, which makes it the readiest cross-check on finding 5. Zheng, Zhang, Huang, Shi, Xu & Ma (2024), *Disciplink: Unfolding interdisciplinary information seeking process via human-AI co-exploration*, UIST [110]. Dontcheva, Drucker, Wade, Salesin & Cohen (2006), *Summarizing personal web browsing sessions*, UIST [20] — the generated overview of accumulated material two decades before the LLM, which would give `text-lense`'s arm a pre-generative history.

*Theory side.* Rieh, Collins-Thompson, Hansen & Lee (2016), *Towards searching as a learning process: A review of current perspectives and future directions*, Journal of Information Science [78] — the closest thing in this bibliography to a theory-side review of the territory, and it bears on two live items: the presupposition's "not yet settled enough to be a query", and finding 1's claim that gathering and framing are one interleaved loop rather than two stages. Piorkowski, Fleming, Kwan, Burnett, Scaffidi, Bellamy & Jordahl (2013), *The whats and hows of programmers' foraging diets*, CHI [74] — foraging theory carried into one concrete domain, which is the transfer question these notes keep asking.

*Marking.* Roy, Valle Torre, Gadiraju, Maxwell & Hauff (2021), *Note the highlight: incorporating active reading tools in a search as learning environment*, CHIIR [82] — highlighting inside search rather than inside reading, which tests whether the marking region's "while doing something else" holds outside a document. Subramonyam, Seifert, Shah & Adar (2020), *TexSketch: Active diagramming through pen-and-ink annotations*, CHI [91] — annotation that produces structure, sitting between the two marking forms and the promotion step.

*Curation.* Chang, Hahn, Perer & Kittur (2019), *SearchLens: composing and capturing complex user interests for exploratory search*, IUI [15] — a captured, composable interest is the kept framing under another name. Ward & Capra (2021), *OrgBox: Supporting cognitive and metacognitive activities during exploratory search*, SIGIR [100]. Ros, Takwane, Patil, Jayaprakash & Zhai (2024), *TextData: Save What You Know and Find What You Don't*, SIGIR [80]. Zhang, Qu, Giles & Song (2008), *CiteSense: supporting sensemaking of research literature*, CHI [107]. Nguyen, Xu, Bardill, Salman, Herd & Wong (2016), *SenseMap: Supporting browser-based online sensemaking through analytic provenance*, VAST [65] — provenance as a kept artifact, adjacent to the curation region and to memory and continuity.

*Accumulation as the trigger.* Chang, Hahn, Kim, Coupland, Breneisen, Kim, Hwong & Kittur (2021), *When the tab comes due: challenges in the cost structure of browser tab usage*, CHI [13], and Ma, Lassila, Nurgalieva & Lindqvist (2023), *When browsing gets cluttered*, CHI [56]. Both are on what accumulation costs before anyone reaches for a lens — the sequence's presupposition, evidenced.

That table is read for corpus gaps in the companion note [`2026-08-29-maru-need-mapping.md`](2026-08-29-maru-need-mapping.md).
