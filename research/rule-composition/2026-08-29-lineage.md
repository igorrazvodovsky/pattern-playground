## The negative finding is confirmed

The note recorded a lineage-by-inspection claim: that the trigger-action
literature and the Boolean-query-interface literature descend from separate
programmes and do not cite each other. With eight of nine papers testable, the
graph confirms it.

The ancestor half was checked exhaustively rather than from the ranked list
above, which the script truncates at twenty-five. Taking every reference of the
five trigger-action papers (131 distinct works) against every reference of the
three Boolean papers (45), the intersection is empty. Neither side cites a paper
from the other side's input set either. The two sides are not merely separate —
each is internally dense, which rules out the alternative explanation that
either is too thin to show connections.

The descendant half is weaker evidence, because it is computed from each paper's
top fifty citing works: a lightly-cited paper citing across the divide would not
appear. Nothing in the retrieved descendants spans both sides.

The two sides also part company on what the descendants are about. Everything
downstream of the trigger-action papers is smart homes, IoT platforms, and
security; everything downstream of the Boolean papers is information
visualisation. Neither line produced a study of rule composition as a general
authoring move. That gap is the note's own territory.

One correction to the note's reading of the trigger-action line. The note has
Ur and Littman's group at the centre, which is right for the papers, but the
convergent ancestor under the most of them is *iCAP* — Dey's context-aware
prototyping tool — not an end-user-programming study. The forces this
literature inherited were first stated as prototyping problems for
context-aware applications, and the rule-authoring framing came later.
