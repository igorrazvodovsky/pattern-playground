## What the lineage shows

The corpus is two literatures with one bridge. The NLP half measures whether a
citation supports its claim, and descends from question-answering benchmarks
and faithfulness metrics. The HCI half measures whether a person acts
differently when a citation is present, and descends from the reliance and
trust-calibration line — the same ancestors that turn up in the
[plan-preview](../plan-preview/) lineage, *To Trust or to Think* among them.
The two halves share no ancestor and no descendant.

The bridge is *Believe It or Not: Factors Influencing Credibility on the Web*
(2001), which sits under both the reliance study and the lateral-reading canon
read. That is the only work connecting the AI-reliance literature to the media-
literacy tradition the note reached for in its addendum, and it predates both
by twenty years. If the pattern wants one grounding source for why a citation
changes behaviour rather than accuracy, that is where the two lines meet.

One of the note's five named expectations is confirmed. Buçinca et al. 2021 —
*To Trust or to Think* — is a convergent ancestor of the reliance half, as
predicted. Rashkin et al.'s attribution work, Pirolli & Card's information
foraging, Vasconcelos et al. 2023, Wineburg & McGrew 2019 and Sundar 2008 do
not reach the two-paper threshold in the six bibliographies available.

The descendants say where the NLP half went: automated frameworks for scoring
citation quality, and the beginnings of a distinction between correct and
faithful attribution. Nothing on the descendant side is an interface study. The
design question the note asks — what a citation should look like to an actor —
has no citation record of its own yet.
