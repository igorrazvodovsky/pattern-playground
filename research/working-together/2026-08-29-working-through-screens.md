# Quarry read: Working Through Screens — working together — 2026-08-29

Companion to the same-day literature note and practice survey. *Working Through Screens* (Tabor, 2009) read as the sequence-map plan uses it: a quarry for territory this corpus is missing, not an ordering source. Read from the chapter notes in the author's resource folder (Dropbox/PARA/3 Resources/Working through screens/), full core texts of the considerations named below.

The considerations that bear on this territory are the J band (facilitating communication) nearly whole — J1–J6; J7, pervasive printing, contributes nothing here — plus the awareness pair C7/G4, the access pair C5/B7, the private→public crossing G7, and the presuppositional pair A2/A7. This is the densest single-band claim any sequence has made on the quarry so far, which supports picking the territory now.

## What each contributes

- *A2 Workers' interrelations and relationships / A7 Collaboration scenarios and variations.* The presupposition territory, stated from the process side: model the roles, power structures, and divisions of labour before sketching functionality, because "even apparently individualistic knowledge work practices can have key collaborative, or at least cooperative, scenarios" — people "cooperatively completing their own parts of a larger process" without feeling they collaborate. Two facts for the head. The map's presupposition ("the working relationships mapped — who shares what with whom, in which roles") is exactly A2's deliverable. And A7 warns the walk must cover cooperation, not only felt collaboration — the sequence's situation should not presuppose that the actors think of themselves as collaborating.
- *C5 Permissions and views tailored to workers' identities.* The grant step's consideration: permissions "shape each user's ability to see and make use of certain data and interaction options", mapped to role segmentation. Its converse is the seam the sitting must respect: permissions applied "without sufficiently considering potential impacts on collaboration between roles" make it "more difficult to establish common ground" — the grant can starve the shared-view step downstream.
- *B7 Object ownership and availability rules.* The conflict question answered at 2009's substrate: "clear and consistent rules governing who can perform actions on or with them at a given time" — check-out, locks, an offered "alternate version… until she later merges it back". Without such rules, "frustrating conflicts and versioning problems". The book's mechanism is locking, which practice has since retired at the live surface (the practice survey's §4); what survives is the claim beneath it — availability must be legible at the object, whatever machinery enforces it.
- *C7 Structural support of workspace awareness / G4 Workspace awareness embedded in interactions.* Awareness at two levels, named as separate considerations: application-level cues (a pane of who is working where; a login-time summary of "key changes… since the last time she accessed the tool" — memory and continuity's catch-up seen from this territory) and per-interaction cues (the trade form showing colleagues entering the same security; portions of the model that "flash" as colleagues save). G4 names the cost of absence in the CHI 2024 paper's own words fifteen years early: users "constantly 'stepping on each others toes'". Both carry the same converse, twice-stated: "too much visibility into the actions of others can be distracting… and can potentially lead to unwanted surveillance effects" — the attention-and-tempo seam and the translucence tension, from the design side.
- *G7 Transitioning work from private to public view.* The private→public crossing as its own consideration: workers "want to explicitly 'move' or 'send' their outputs into the view of others", the boundary ranging "from entirely blurred… to highly distinct" per practice; and the surveillance cost is generative, not just unpleasant — "unwanted surveillance may limit or otherwise influence the character of their explorations", driving workers to other tools "to preserve privacy and gain the freedom to make 'mistakes'". Grounds the step's skip condition (some practices value the blur) and its force (visibility vs the freedom to explore).
- *J4 Authorship awareness, presence, and contact facilitation.* The territory's "who" consideration, and it unifies what the corpus might otherwise split: the historical cue (who has worked here) and the live cue (who is working here) are one consideration, with contact as the mark's use — "seeing that he is currently online, she clicks on his name". Offline precedent stated: "workers can often trace the author of a change through handwriting". The same surveillance converse as C7/G4.
- *J2 Representational common ground.* The shared-view consideration: sharing "her view of a 3D display" with a pointing cursor; the failure mode is having to "retrace and verbalize the steps needed to recreate their current displays" — which is the addressing seam: a shareable view is a deep link plus the arrangement on it, structuring the space's and composing views' products spent here. Follow-mode and spotlight (the practice survey's directed presence) are this consideration shipped.
- *J3 Explicit work handoffs.* The handoff consideration, and it gives the step its shape: artifacts *and responsibility* passed, at defined workflow points or improvised contextually; and the sender's reciprocal need — "visibility into whether handed off items have been received, reviewed, and acted on" — which is what practice ships as typed review states and travelling subscriptions. Also the skip condition: "extra functionality for explicit work handoffs may not be necessary" where organisational norms carry the process outside the tool.
- *J5 Public annotation.* The audience-directed mark (read for making sense at scale already; its working-together face is primary): annotations placed "to be viewed by an anticipated 'public'", and the attention argument — "real time communication used in place of public annotations may demand receivers' attentions at inappropriate times" — the annotation channel as the non-interrupting alternative, grounding why the contribution region's floor is a mark in the material rather than a message.
- *J6 Streamlined standard communications.* The status-feedback consideration: "established, commonly shared genres of communication" (the session-end status report to selected recipients in J1's example is one), where the tool offloads composing, distributing, and interpreting. Its converse bounds the step: standardisation "may force the inclusion of some types of information, exclude needed categories, and constrain representation".
- *J1 Integral communication pathways.* The band's frame: communication options placed "within the pathways of their onscreen actions" rather than as a separate surface, because end users "may not make these types of distinctions" between working and communicating. For the sequence this is a principle over the whole contribution region, not a step.

## What the quarry does not give

No ordering — by design. No merge model beyond locking: the book predates the silent-merge convergence the practice survey documents, so B7's machinery is dated while its legibility claim stands. And no generated participant: the AI teammate whose contributions arrive in shared material — the seam delegating work and calibrating reliance already hold open toward this sequence — enters only through the same-day literature note and practice survey.
