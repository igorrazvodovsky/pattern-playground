## Context

A pattern library whose graph reads patterns as generative moves (suggestion-grade edges, node-side situations; trees and situation blocks are the two judgement homes). Eleven decision trees exist in three states: wired (leaf maps emitting `recommends`), deferred with recorded reasons, and never wired. The strawman under test sorts trees by leaf type:

- (a) move-leaf trees stay authored trees emitting `recommends` edges;
- (b) component/technique-leaf trees are realisation guidance — beside the component dataset or dissolved into dimension tables on the owning move's page, never graph edges;
- (c) internal-anatomy trees (help placement, localization layers, modality gradient) dissolve into body structure and situation constructs;
- (d) suitability-note rows become a second tree over the same targets from a different lens.

Questions: does dissolving an explicit selection tree harm generativity (selection vs. unfolding)? Where does prior art put component-chooser trees? Do explicit choosers do work situated prose does not? Do parallel lenses over one target set merge or stay separate?

## Convergent findings

### Generativity has two recognised species, and a decision tree is a legitimate form of the second

Dearden & Finlay §5.3 distinguishes them explicitly. First, sequencing: "the idea of generating designs by implicit sequencing of decisions, derived by traversing the network of links between the individual patterns"; Beck & Cunningham's gloss is that a language "helps designers to ask and answer the right question at the right time, i.e. the language can be used to sequence design decisions." Second, option-space generation: "Another concept of 'generative' discussed in HCI, is the idea of generating an option space of alternative designs from which the design team should select … Some pattern collections offer the reader a choice of alternative (incompatible) solutions to a design problem, from which one must be selected, based on specified attributes of the domain."

That second sentence is a decision tree described from the literature's side. Selection-among-alternatives-by-domain-attributes is not the anti-generative degenerate case of a pattern language — it is one of the two things "generative" has meant in HCI all along. The strawman's premise ("selection-from-a-menu is the anti-generative form") is therefore *wrong as stated*; what the epistemic stance actually polices is rule-grade *matching*, not choice-shaped judgement.

Papers: Dearden & Finlay (carrying Lane 1990, MacLean et al. 1991, Dearden & Harrison 1997, Tidwell's alternative-pattern pairs).
*Draft project takeaway*: a decision tree is the option-space species of generativity — dissolving one is justified by mis-homed content (anatomy, realisation), never by treating choice itself as anti-generative.

### What a tree contributes that situated prose does not: asking the right question at the right time

Beck & Cunningham's formulation locates the value in the *questions*, not the routing. Fincher & Windsor's four requirements for an organising principle (via D&F) include both "allow the user to evaluate the problem from different standpoints" and "be generative, allowing users to develop new solutions" — findability and question-posing are separate obligations. Friedman's survey makes the same point from practice: trees "resolve never-ending discussions" and serve as *documentation of a settled debate*, i.e. their value survives even when the reader doesn't follow the branches mechanically.

Consequence for strawman (c): where an internal-anatomy tree dissolves, its *questions* must stay legible — as a dimension table (the bounded-choice precedent) or a named "the useful question is…" sentence (the Form-story precedent) — or the dissolution loses the cognitive-forcing content while keeping only the anatomy.

Papers: Dearden & Finlay; Friedman.
*Draft project takeaway*: when a tree dissolves, its questions are the part to keep — the branch routing can go, the ask-this-at-this-moment content cannot.

### Component-chooser trees are design-system artefacts and live beside the components

Every surveyed chooser of this species — Workday Canvas (notifications, loading, truncation, overflow), Lyft (radio/checkbox/dropdown), Doctolib (forms, navigation, errors), Carbon (inline/toast/actionable/modal) — lives in design-system documentation next to the components it chooses among. None of the surveyed systems hoists the chooser into an abstraction layer above its own component vocabulary. Carbon's page is labelled "pattern", but its leaves are Carbon components — the label names the page genre, not a language layer; placement, not labelling, is the evidence.

This supports strawman (b): a tree whose leaves are realisations belongs with the realisation dataset (component stories), or dissolves into a dimension table on the owning move's page. It also reframes the library's notification tree: it descends from Carbon's (which notification.mdx cites), and its component leaves are inherited, not authored drift.

Papers: Friedman; Carbon (snippet-grounded).
*Draft project takeaway*: chooser trees with component leaves are realisation guidance — prior art keeps them beside the components; only leaves that name moves belong in the language graph.

### Plural lenses over one target set stay separate structures

Pattern Views' mechanism is plural, problem-context-specific views over the same pattern corpus, with relations captured per view rather than merged into one canonical structure ("patterns and their relations across pattern languages for a particular problem context"). Fincher & Windsor's "evaluate the problem from different standpoints" requirement says the same thing as an obligation on organising principles. Neither source supports collapsing two discriminating structures into one bigger tree.

This supports strawman (d): a second tree recommending the same members from a behavioural-intent lens, beside the existing topology tree, is prior-arted practice — two views, two judgement homes, each emitting its own hinted edges.

Papers: Weigold et al.; Dearden & Finlay (carrying Fincher & Windsor).
*Draft project takeaway*: two trees over the same targets from different lenses are two views, not duplication — merge pressure is the thing to resist.
