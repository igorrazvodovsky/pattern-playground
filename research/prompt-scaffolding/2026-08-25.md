# Research: prompt scaffolding — 2026-08-25

Query: whether structuring an open request measurably helps, whether the structure narrows expression, and whether the need has moved as models improved. Run to decide whether the `prompt-scaffolding` seed is promoted, split, merged, or discarded.

> *Retrieval provenance*. Generated by the `deep-research` workflow, not `/research-gate`: 111 agents, six search angles, 28 sources fetched, 136 claims extracted, 25 put through three-vote adversarial verification, 15 confirmed, 10 killed. Verification budget ran out after the first two angles, so **the claims below carry two different weights and the note marks which is which**. Verified claims were checked against primary text by three independent agents. Extracted claims were pulled from primary sources by a single agent and never adversarially checked; for those, existence, authorship, date and topic of every source were confirmed by hand (HTTP fetch of the abstract or page) on 2026-08-25, but the reported figures were not re-derived.
>
> One correction to the workflow's own output: it concluded that arXiv 2605.20149 was a fabricated citation after two claims from it were refuted 0-3. The paper is real — Ghosh, Polach & Sow, *Less Back-and-Forth: A Comparative Study of Structured Prompting*, submitted 19 May 2026, cs.CL. The verifiers could not reach the `/html/` mirror and read that as fabrication. The paper's weakness is methodological, not existential, and it is recorded as such below.

## Context

*Topic*: giving an open prompt structure so a request can be composed completely.

*Interaction*: an actor who can't yet specify what they want is offered structure instead of an empty box — labelled fields, slots, cloze passages, paginated composition, tone and persona controls, or pre-submission feedback.

*Setting*: conversational and generative surfaces where the request is the whole interface. The seed was carried over from a cross-catalogue triage of six AI pattern libraries and had no page of its own.

## The finding that reframes the question

*No study anywhere in the retrieved set puts the seed's own forms on a bench.* Not one of the papers tests a labelled-field, slot, cloze, or paginated prompt-composition interface against an open box with real actors. The pattern's strongest intellectual footing — Subramonyam's gulf of envisioning and Tankelevitch's metacognitive demands, both CHI 2024 — comes from two papers that report no original empirical study and evaluate no scaffold. They establish that the problem is real and nameable. They cannot be cited as evidence that a scaffold fixes it.

What the literature does supply, repeatedly, is a *different* form for the same problem: the system detects what the request is missing and asks for it. Five independent sources converge on this. It is not what the seed lists under Forms.

## Verified (three-vote adversarial confirmation)

Votes are marked per item. A 3-0 item was confirmed by three independent verifiers against primary text; a 2-1 item survived with one dissent and should be leaned on less.

1. **3-0.** *Bridging the Gulf of Envisioning* (Subramonyam, Pea, Pondoc, Agrawala, Seifert — CHI 2024). [arXiv:2309.14459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14459)
   - Speaks to: Q1, Q4. Decomposes the difficulty into a capability gap (can't formulate how-to procedures), an instruction gap ("the user's challenges in clearly and effectively expressing their intentions in the interface as text prompts", grounded in "even slight changes in words can lead LLMs to produce significantly different outputs"), and an intentionality gap (no internal benchmark for judging output, because planning was skipped).
   - The remedy the paper actually proposes is a *prompt prompter*: "identifying expected elements of task functions that are missing in a user prompt and requesting needed information (e.g., 'who is your audience?')", which "can change dynamically within an LLM session to better reflect the intentions as the user develops them". The form is dynamic and interrogative rather than a fixed field set.
   - Transfer: *strong* for the problem, *none* for the solution. Conceptual paper, no methods or participants section.

2. **3-0.** *The Metacognitive Demands and Opportunities of Generative AI* (Tankelevitch, Kewenig, Simkute, Scott, Sarkar, Rintel, Kalliamvakou — CHI 2024 Best Paper). [DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642902](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642902)
   - Speaks to: Q1, Q4. Locates the demand in the open-endedness of the box itself: an unstructured prompt requires self-awareness of task goals and the ability to decompose the task before anything can be verbalised. This is the seed's premise stated by a peer-reviewed source.
   - Also endorses feedforward — telling an actor a vague prompt is unlikely to work before they submit — but presents the balance as unresolved rather than settled.
   - Transfer: *strong* for warrant. Position paper; evaluates nothing.

3. **3-0.** *Partnering with Generative AI* (Maier, Schneider & Feuerriegel, LMU Munich / MCML — CHI 2026). [arXiv:2510.23324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23324)
   - Speaks to: Q1, Q2, Q3. The single most decision-relevant measured result in the set. Pre-registered RCT, N=486 on GPT-4.1, plus a second pre-registered study in another domain, N=640.
   - Pre-articulated content suggestions produced *no* quality gain over unassisted control (d=0.13, p=.366; replication z=0.41, p=.684, d=0.04) and *reduced* idea diversity (Δ=−0.03, p=.006). Model-asked open questions produced both a quality gain (d=0.36; d=0.49) and a diversity gain (Δ=+0.07, p<.001).
   - Qualitative coding of 80 conversations (two raters, weighted κ=0.628): 25% of suggestion-mode participants did not elaborate at all and 55% only minimally, against 60% substantial elaboration and 40% adding a new idea in question-mode.
   - Two honest limits: the replication is by the same authors, and question-mode's study-1 effect does not survive Holm correction (p_adj=.090). The *suggestion-mode null is robust* across both studies and both test families.
   - Transfer: *partial but pointed*. None of the five conditions was a field-and-slot composition UI, so this discriminates who articulates the request, not whether the box has fields. It is nonetheless the closest thing to a head-to-head between the seed's territory and its neighbour, and the neighbour wins.

4. **3-0** on the homogenisation result; **2-1** on the prompting-strategies null. *Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation* (Anderson, Shah & Kreminski — C&C 2024). 36 participants, 1271 ideas. [PDF](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Homogenization_C%26C2024.pdf)
   - Speaks to: Q2. ChatGPT-assisted ideas were significantly more homogeneous at the *group* level (M=.24 vs .28, t(32)=2.154, p=0.038, d=.47) with no individual-level difference (M=.65 vs .66, p=0.352). The authors' own reading: the effect "seems to stem largely from the LLM generating similar responses to different users' queries in the same creative context."
   - Teaching better prompting did not fix it (2-1; one verifier dissented, and the finding is evidence against prompt *education*, not against design-level scaffolding): role-based prompts and creative constraints showed no reliable difference from pasting the task verbatim (ideas M=9.18 vs 8.01, p=0.317; homogeneity M=.99 vs .99, p=0.927). This is an underpowered post-hoc subgroup split at a measurement ceiling — absence of evidence more than evidence of absence — and the authors qualify it with "without additional interventions at the level of CST design or model development".
   - Transfer: *analogical*. No prompt UI is tested. The narrowing it measures is across actors, not within one.

5. **3-0** on the anchoring result; **low confidence** on the DAT-dependence bullet. *Generative AI Enhances Individual Creativity but Reduces the Collective Diversity of Novel Content* (Doshi & Hauser — Science Advances, 12 July 2024). Pre-registered, N=293 writers / N=600 evaluators, GPT-4. [DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adn5290](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)
   - Speaks to: Q2. Stories were 5.2% and 5.0% more similar to the AI idea shown; within-condition similarity rose by 10.7% and 8.9% of the human-only range. "Writers in the two GenAI conditions are anchored to some extent on the GenAI idea presented to them."
   - The benefit is population-dependent — the workflow flagged this bullet as low confidence and warned against reading it as confirming a scaffolding prediction: "Among the most inherently creative writers (i.e., high-DAT writers), there is little effect of having access to generative AI ideas." Low-DAT gains were +6.3%/+10.7% novelty. Hold this loosely — standard errors are clustered at the evaluator level while treatment is assigned at the writer level, and adj. R² is 0.012.
   - Transfer: *analogical, direction inverted*. The intervention supplies finished content, which is closer to suggestion or regeneration than to structuring a request.

6. **3-0.** *The Effects of Generative AI on Design Fixation and Divergent Thinking* (Wadinambiarachchi, Kelly, Pareek, Zhou & Velloso — CHI 2024). N=60, Midjourney V4. [DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642919](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642919)
   - Speaks to: Q2. Higher fixation on an initial example (+0.32, 89% CI [0.11, 0.54], BF 124) and lower originality (−0.03, BF 35) than a no-AI baseline. 44% of prompts reused a word from the brief; 57% contained "robot", "chatbot" or "chatbox". §4.6.1 is titled, verbatim, "I just took the words from the brief".
   - Three limits worth carrying: fluency and variety decrements are weak (BF 3.20 and 5.93, CIs crossing zero); the Image Search arm showed *larger* decrements than the AI arm, so the mechanism is exposure to external examples generally rather than generativity; and there is no scaffold in the study — a plain free-text box, a brief, and an example image.
   - Notably, the paper's own §5.1 remedy is "a predetermined prompt structure or template". A study often cited for the narrowing tension prescribes scaffolding as the fix.

7. **2-1.** *Why Johnny Can't Prompt* (Zamfirescu-Pereira, Wong, Hartmann & Yang — CHI 2023). n=10, GPT-3 davinci. [DOI 10.1145/3544548.3581388](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581388)
   - Speaks to: Q1. The Mad Libs recommendation the catalogues inherited rests on a computers-are-social-actors argument, not on a completeness argument: people refuse to perform socially violating moves that work, so the system should perform them hidden — "using a template that explicitly repeats without giving the user agency, in the style of Mad Libs."
   - "Mad Lib" appears exactly once in the paper, as unvalidated design speculation the authors hedge throughout. The underlying technique (hidden repetition) is GPT-3-era.
   - Transfer: *weak and dated*. If the mechanism is dead, one of the pattern's two inherited rationales goes with it.

## Extracted, source verified by hand, claim not adversarially checked

Everything in this section answers Q3, Q4 and Q5 — the questions the workflow's verification budget never reached. Treat the figures as leads, not as established.

*On capability drift (Q3)*

8. *The Decreasing Value of Chain of Thought in Prompting* (Wharton Generative AI Labs technical report, 2025-12-07). [Report](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/tech-report-chain-of-thought/) — GPQA Diamond and MMLU-Pro, 25 trials per question per condition. On reasoning models, adding CoT produced marginal or negative change (o3-mini +2.9%, o4-mini +3.1%, Gemini Flash 2.5 −3.3%) while adding 20–80% latency. The scaffold has been absorbed into default model behaviour. On non-reasoning models it helps on average but adds variance that breaks questions the model would otherwise get right.

9. *Playing Pretend: Expert Personas Don't Improve Factual Accuracy* (Wharton Generative AI Labs, 2025-12-07). [Report](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/playing-pretend-expert-personas/) — across GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, o3-mini, o4-mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash and Gemini 2.5 Flash, domain-expert personas produced no accuracy improvement even when matched to the question domain. Low-knowledge personas *degraded* accuracy on o4-mini. The authors scope the null to factual accuracy and leave open a residual value for tone and framing. This bears directly on the Persona Selector and Tone Slider sub-forms.

10. *You Don't Need Prompt Engineering Anymore: The Prompting Inversion* (Khan — [arXiv:2510.22251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22251), 25 Oct 2025). Heavy constraint-based prompting helped gpt-4o (97% vs 93%) and hurt gpt-5 (94.00% vs 96.36% for plain CoT) on GSM8K. The proposed mechanism is a guardrail-to-handcuff transition: constraints that prevent common-sense errors in weaker models induce hyper-literalism in stronger ones. *Weigh this lightly* — single independent author, unrefereed, one benchmark, one model family, and the paper's own data shows light CoT still beating zero-shot, so what inverted was heavy constraint, not structure as such.

11. *What Prompts Don't Say: Understanding and Managing Underspecification in LLM Prompts* (arXiv:2505.13360, May 2025, rev. April 2026). [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13360) — models infer unstated requirements by default in only 41.1% of cases, so the gap the pattern targets is measurable and has not closed. Underspecified prompts are twice as likely to regress across model upgrades, sometimes by more than 20 points — a governance argument for making requirements explicit. But maximalist scaffolding backfires: accuracy falls as requirement count rises, because instruction-following capacity is finite and stated requirements conflict. Generic prompt improvement yields little; only requirement-aware optimisers help, and only by +4.8%.

12. *Less Back-and-Forth: A Comparative Study of Structured Prompting* (Ghosh, Polach & Sow — [arXiv:2605.20149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20149), 19 May 2026). Checklist-structured prompts scored 7.50/8 against 5.67 raw and 6.67 clarifying-question, across four task types and ChatGPT, Claude and Grok, and used the fewest tokens (683 vs 962 raw, 937 clarifying). The one retrieved source that argues front-loaded structure still beats asking back on a 2026 model. *Weigh it lightly*: seven-page unrefereed preprint, authors scored their own outputs, no blind raters, no inter-rater reliability, no human participants — it tests prompt *text*, not an interface.

*On what products did (Q3, Q5)*

13. ChatGPT's automatic DALL·E 3 prompt rewriting — the shipped prompt-enhancer form — measurably *degraded* output. A UC Berkeley experiment (N=1,891, reported 2024-08-10) found the automatic revision layer erased roughly 58% of DALL·E 3's advantage over DALL·E 2. [Summary](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpts-automatic-prompt-rewriting-reduces-dall-e-3s-performance-study-finds/)
14. Microsoft discontinued the standalone Copilot Prompt Gallery app: new installations stopped June 2025, access ended 15 July 2025. The scaffolding was not deleted but relocated into the composer as prompt pills. [Support page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/changes-to-the-copilot-prompt-gallery-app)
15. GPT-5 launch, 7 August 2025. [Announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5/) — the reasoning-depth decision moved from the actor to a router; the advertised interaction is to type an unstructured question. OpenAI self-reports a shift toward the model asking back. Cutting the other way: OpenAI shipped four preset personalities (Cynic, Robot, Listener, Nerd) as configurable controls whose stated purpose is to remove the need to author prompt text, and attributes their feasibility to improved steerability. A frontier vendor *adding* persona configuration in 2025 as a consequence of capability gains.
16. ChatGPT deep research handles completeness with a system-side pipeline — the model asks clarifying questions, rewrites the prompt, then researches — plus an editable plan the actor reviews *after* submitting. The completeness checklist (question, desired outcome, constraints) ships as prose in help documentation, not as fields. [Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10500283-deep-research-in-chatgpt), [API guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/deep-research)
17. GitHub Spec Kit (2 Sept 2025) inverts the pattern: the actor supplies a short unstructured prompt and the agent generates the structured specification, which the actor then reviews. The slots are filled by the model. [Blog](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/generative-ai/spec-driven-development-with-ai-get-started-with-a-new-open-source-toolkit/)

*On boundaries and what survives (Q4, Q5)*

18. *Interaction Methods in Generative AI Image Tools* (Park et al. — CHI 2026, corpus of 37 tools, 2022–July 2025). [PDF](https://hal.science/hal-05550947v1) — the strongest available case for discarding. Prompt scaffolding is well supported in *academic prototypes* and largely absent from shipping commercial products. It co-occurs with early-stage ideation (r=.47, p<.01) and is *negatively* correlated with fine-grained control (parameter r=−.43, precision r=−.41): scaffolded input and precise steering are alternatives, not companions, and scaffolding disappears once work moves past ideation. Only 1 of 37 tools (Midjourney) offered strong parameter control; 14 exposed no model parameters at all. Free-text prompting remained dominant through July 2025. The authors explicitly frame the pattern's continued value as an open question against abandoning text-first interaction.
19. *OOPrompt: Reifying Intents into Structured Artifacts* (Xu, Ma & Chen — [arXiv:2604.19114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19114), 21 April 2026). The one recent system paper that builds the seed's form. Its validation study (n=8, all returning from the formative study, so preference is stated rather than measured) gives the pattern a *scope rather than a recommendation*: structured prompting pays only above thresholds of constraint density, hierarchical depth and repetitiveness — report writing, routine updates, decomposable planning — and below them the structure is net-negative. On argumentative and reasoning-shaped tasks participants could not map intent onto the slots and wrote long prose inside the structured interface. The authors attribute that to the structuring paradigm, not to prototype quality. They also found the object-centred flow conflicts with conversational repair: adding information through follow-up turns was easier to adapt to.
20. Microsoft's ISV guidance for generative AI applications (content dated 2024-09-16) explicitly recommends decomposing one prompt box into labelled fields, with a worked four-field example (title, details, images, tone), plus promptbooks justified by *repeatability over enterprise data* rather than by prompt-writing difficulty. [Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-cloud/dev/copilot/isv/ux-guidance) — note the section's stated premise is that long-form natural-language typing is unfamiliar to most people, which is exactly the premise 2024–2026 familiarity drift erodes. The section contains no quality feedback, no enhancer, and no system-initiated clarification.
21. Google PAIR's People + AI Guidebook (published 2019-05-08, last updated 2021-05-18) has 23 patterns, none touching prompt input. Its silence is a scope artefact of its date, not a considered rejection. Microsoft's HAX Toolkit is pitched at a deliberately UI-independent altitude and publishes patterns for only 8 of its 18 guidelines, so its silence is likewise not a rejection. *Neither institutional catalogue carves this territory* — it exists only in the newer practitioner catalogues the seed was triaged from.

## What this means for the seed

*The two tensions the seed records are not its own.* `template` already states both, almost word for word: "the template is now a default the actor may not realise they can leave" and "the templates keep proposing last year's shape until somebody revisits them". `suggestion` already states the anchoring one: "shown before they have formed their own idea, it narrows what they go on to consider". The seed inherits its consequences from its parents.

*The form the evidence favours already has a home.* `inquiry-agent` is the agent asking for a value it lacks, in slot vocabulary, and `open-request` composes it — "ask only for missing/ambiguous slots", "show a brief intent frame, e.g. goal, constraints, assumptions". That is Subramonyam's prompt prompter and Maier's question-mode, already written down. The seed does not link to either.

*The persistent-artefact material belongs elsewhere.* Spec Kit, the deep-research editable plan, and OOPrompt's revisable prompt object are `plan-preview` and `detached-artefact` territory — Spec Kit's own framing says the structured artefact outlives the request. `research/plan-preview/` is already open on this.

*The sub-forms have different fates.* Structured fields survive with a scope (high constraint density, repetitive, enterprise governance — Microsoft ISV, the underspecification regression argument, OOPrompt's own threshold), and fail on argumentative and reasoning-shaped work. Configurable controls survive for tone and framing and fail for accuracy (Wharton personas; Tankelevitch on added metacognitive demand; 14 of 37 image tools exposing no parameters). Prompt enhancers have a measured negative (DALL·E 3, −58% of the generational gain; requirement-blind optimisers near-zero). Madlibs and cloze have no evidence at all beyond a hedged GPT-3-era aside.

*One recorded edge is contradicted.* The seed lists `complements: suggestion` on the grounds that starters and example prompts seed the scaffold's slots. Maier's verified result measures exactly that arrangement — pre-articulated content handed to someone before they compose — and finds no quality gain plus reduced diversity, against a gain on both from asking instead. The edge should be rewritten as a tension rather than a complement, or dropped.

## Disposition

Not promote as it stands, and not discard. The seed is a bundle whose parts belong in four different places.

- *Structured fields, labelled slots, paginated composition* — keep, narrowed. This is the residue that stays on the page, scoped to work with high constraint density, hierarchical depth, or repetition, and to the enterprise repeatability argument. Record that it is net-negative below those thresholds and that it fails on argumentative and reasoning-shaped requests.
- *System detects what's missing and asks* — do not build here. It already exists as `inquiry-agent` and `open-request`. Add the edges from this page to them; that is where the only positive measured evidence points.
- *Persistent structured specification the actor reviews* — belongs to `plan-preview` and `detached-artefact`, where work is already open.
- *Prompt enhancer and automatic rewriting* — carries a measured negative. Record it as a caution or an anti-pattern rather than a form to offer.
- *Tone, persona and parameter controls* — keep only for tone, perspective and framing. The accuracy rationale does not hold.
- *Madlibs and cloze passages* — drop. The only warrant is a hedged GPT-3-era aside about hidden repetition.
- *The two recorded tensions* — already owned by `template` and `suggestion`. Cite rather than restate.

The page that survives is narrower than the seed and points outward more than inward.

## Open questions

- No study tests a field-and-slot prompt UI against an open box with non-expert actors on a current model. The pattern's central empirical question is unasked, not answered.
- Whether the seam is *who supplies the structure* (actor fills slots vs system detects and asks) or *when* (before submission vs during the exchange). Maier and OOPrompt disagree about which dimension does the work.
- Whether the open box's demanded explicitness is beneficial friction. Tankelevitch raises it and does not settle it; if it holds, scaffolding that removes the need to articulate removes a training effect the seed's `enacts: learnability` edge assumes.
- Whether "the actor is unfamiliar with typing long natural-language requests" — Microsoft's stated premise in 2024 — is still true in 2026. Every field-based recommendation retrieved rests on it.
