## Findings by question

### 1. The one-node carving survives; Tidwell's own selection criteria argue for it

- *Tidwell* (Designing Interfaces) splits the territory three ways, but her "when to use" clauses select by screen-space condition, not by user problem: Two-Panel Selector applies "when the display is large enough to show two separate panels at once"; One-Window Drilldown "when you are building for a device with tight space constraints" (search-extract grounding; designinginterfaces.com unreachable). Selection keyed on display constraints is selection among *layouts* — exactly the dimension Min et al.'s content analysis demotes to layout values (new-page / side-by-side / in-place …). Her split is real prior art but its own criteria are conditions on one underlying pattern.
- *Alternative Views* is a label mismatch, not a genuine third O-D form: it is representation switching over one collection — data-view's territory in this corpus — so the disagreement with the one-node O-D shape is two-thirds layout, one-third mis-sorted neighbour.
- *welie.com* carries a one-node precedent: "Overview by Detail" is a single pattern ("People like to work from overviews, and overviews should contain enough information to select objects for a particular purpose. The detailed view then shows the object with all the details that cannot be shown otherwise"), which he treats as *underlying* other patterns (Image Browser, List Builder, Panning Navigator). Caveat for our narrower scope: welie's one node is broader than the reshape's — his examples include continuous-space cases (Panning Navigator, minimap-style miniaturised document) that the plan deliberately routes to pan-and-zoom + coordinated-views. The precedent supports one node; it does not adjudicate the item-structured scope cut.
- *NN/g* has no dedicated master-detail node at all. The listing-page ↔ detail-page roundtrip lives in "The Anatomy of a List Entry", framed linearly (browse → click through → return), with *pogo-sticking* as the named failure mode: "a list entry without enough detail leaves too many questions unanswered, forcing users to pogo stick". Two consequences for the plan: (a) the claim that the new-page roundtrip "currently has no corpus home" holds in the wild too — NN/g files it under list-entry anatomy, not as a pattern; (b) pogo-sticking is independent practitioner evidence for the "overview's attribute subset is everyone's ceiling" force.
- *Verdict*: no counter-argument stronger than Tidwell's split was found, and her split keys on the dimension the reframe demotes. Proceed with the one-node shape.

### 2. "Needs-based view" is free to claim

No UI-pattern or product-design usage with a conflicting meaning surfaced. The phrase's nearest established neighbour is marketing's *needs-based segmentation* (grouping customers by underlying need rather than demographics) — a different domain, and consonant rather than conflicting: both name carving by need, not by attribute. Dashboard-design practitioner literature talks around the concept ("identify the user's needs", "split into multiple views") without owning the phrase. Free to claim.

### 3. "Coordinated views" aligns with, and should cite, CMV

"Coordinated multiple views" is the established community term — Baldonado et al. 2000 coined the guidelines frame, Roberts 2007 ("State of the Art: Coordinated & Multiple Views in Exploratory Visualization") named the survey and the CMV workshop series, and current tooling docs (e.g. Vitessce) still use the full phrase. The shortened "coordinated views" appears throughout that same literature as informal shorthand, not as a distinct concept — so the pattern name neither collides nor misrepresents; it reads as the same concept generalised beyond visualization to product UI. The page should name "coordinated multiple views (CMV)" as the community's term in its opening or resources.

- *Bonus retrieval*: arxiv 2204.09524, "An Empirical Study on the Relationship Between the Number of Coordinated Views and Visual Analysis" (cs.HC) — CMVs "simultaneously present multiple visualizations in separate but linked views"; the study design targets advantages (finding hidden relationships) against disadvantages (cognitive load) as view count grows. Abstract-only, but directly on the parsimony consequence the mint plans to carry. Flagged as a deepening read alongside Roberts 2007.

### 4. Writable views: no UI pattern language names it; the naming lives in three adjacent literatures

- *Database view-update problem* — the original home (Bancilhon & Spyratos, already queued in the plan's deferred list).
- *Bidirectional transformations / lenses* (cs.PL): the get/put pair with well-behavedness laws — "a forward direction (get) that takes a concrete input to an abstract output, and a backward direction (put) that … computes a new concrete state"; acceptability/consistency laws are the formal version of "which edits are legal through which representation". Industrial instance: OutSystems CHAMELEON (arxiv 2305.03361) applies live BX between abstraction levels in a low-code editor.
- *Projectional / structured editing* (MPS; Fowler's bliki): the closest UI-adjacent *named* concept — "keystrokes are translated into modifications of the underlying AST, which are then instantly re-projected". Same move, but the term is scoped to code/DSL editors, not product-UI collections; Lorgnette's "malleable code projections" (UIST '23) sits in this line.
- *Verdict*: no pattern language names editing-through-a-projection for product UI. Data-view's writable-views section is naming new ground; anchor its lineage to the view-update problem, lenses, and projectional editing without borrowing any of those names (each is scoped to its own substrate).

## Convergent findings

### Practitioner libraries split overview-detail by layout condition, never by problem

Tidwell selects among her three patterns by screen space; NN/g files the roundtrip under list-entry anatomy; welie holds one node whose examples over-include continuous space. Nobody carves by user problem and arrives at a different set of problems — the variation across libraries is all in how much of the layout dimension gets pattern-hood.
Papers/sources: Tidwell (Designing Interfaces), welie.com Overview by Detail, NN/g List Entries.
*Draft project takeaway*: the practitioner disagreement over master-detail is entirely about how many layout values deserve names — evidence that layout is a dimension of one pattern, not a pattern boundary.

### Editing-through-a-projection is named everywhere except product UI

Databases (view update), PL (lenses/BX), and language workbenches (projectional editing) each name the move for their substrate; product-UI pattern languages don't.
Papers/sources: Bancilhon & Spyratos lineage, Wikipedia/JFP lens literature, CHAMELEON (2305.03361), Fowler bliki, Lorgnette.
*Draft project takeaway*: writable views in data-view can claim the product-UI naming gap, citing lenses and projectional editing as substrate-specific ancestors.
