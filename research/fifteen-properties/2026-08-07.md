## Context

The project documents the fifteen as substrate-agnostic moves (operand +
operation), one file per property, with per-substrate translations that must
state presence and absence conditions. Questions:

1. What prior academic work applies the fifteen properties or
   structure-preserving transformations to screens, interfaces, code structure,
   or software process?
2. Have the properties been tested empirically outside architecture?
3. What critiques exist of their transferability to non-geometric or temporal
   media?

## Retrieved papers

- *The Fundamental Behavioral Properties* (Iba, Kimura, Akado, Honda; PURPLSOC
  2015) — **full-text read**. 24 properties of lively human activity, derived by
  mining the Iba Lab's own human-action pattern languages (Learning,
  Collaboration, Presentation Patterns) for "the action of centers behind the
  patterns", then validated against further languages (Fearless Change,
  Pedagogical Patterns, Creative Thinking). [web.sfc.keio.ac.jp/~iba/papers/PURPLSOC15_BehavioralProperties.pdf]
  - Speaks to: Q1, Q3 (the strongest single answer to both)
  - Transfer: strong — the method (mine pattern languages for how centres
    strengthen each other in this medium) transfers directly; the finding is
    that the *list* did not: for a non-spatial medium they re-derived a
    different, larger set rather than translating the fifteen.
- *Fundamental Behavioral Properties, Part I: Extending the Theory of Centers
  for Pattern Language 3.0* (Iba et al.; EuroPLoP 2015) — abstract-grounded
  companion. States the transfer problem plainly: the fifteen "are geometrical,
  so it remains unknown whether they will work for things hard to perceive only
  by shapes or spatial characteristics, such as human actions or society."
  [dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2855321.2855333]
  - Speaks to: Q3
  - Transfer: strong — this is the project's exact question, asked in 2015.
- *Understanding Christopher Alexander's Fifteen Properties via Visualization
  and Analysis* (Iba & Sakai; PURPLSOC 2014) — **full-text read (partial)**.
  Redraws each property as a centre-diagram and runs correspondence analysis
  over the ch. 5 interdependency matrix to map how the properties cluster;
  motivation stated as enabling application "to other domains".
  [web.sfc.keio.ac.jp/~iba/papers/PURPLSOC14_Properties.pdf]
  - Speaks to: Q1, Q2
  - Transfer: partial — analytic tooling over the canonical list, not a new
    substrate; useful precedent for treating the dependency matrix as data.
- *Symmetry and Symmetry Breaking in Software Patterns* (Coplien & Zhao; GCSE
  2000, LNCS 2177) — abstract-grounded. Formalises software patterns as
  symmetry breaking with group-theoretic foundations, arguing Alexander's
  underlying symmetry foundations "may be more suitable to software than the
  architectural metaphors themselves." Later restated as *Explaining software
  patterns by breaking the symmetry* (ACM, 2007).
  [link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-44815-2_4]
  - Speaks to: Q1
  - Transfer: partial — code structure, not interfaces; but it is the
    software-engineering community's canonical route from Alexander's geometry
    into a non-geometric medium, and it went through symmetry, not the fifteen.
- *Harmony-Seeking Computations: a Science of Non-Classical Dynamics based on
  the Progressive Evolution of the Larger Whole* (Alexander; Int. J.
  Unconventional Computing, 2009) — abstract/web-grounded, open PDF. Alexander's
  own statement toward computation: computation reframed as finding
  whole-extending configurations. The fifteen play a background role; the
  foreground is the step that extends the whole.
  [livingneighborhoods.org/library/harmony-seeking-computations.pdf]
  - Speaks to: Q1
  - Transfer: partial — programmatic, not operational; consistent with the
    move-set-over-checklist reading. A 2023 follow-up exists (*A
    Quanti-Qualitative Approach to Alexander's Harmony-Seeking Computations*,
    not yet read).
- *Notes on "Notes on the Synthesis of Form": Dawning Insights in Early
  Christopher Alexander* (Richard P. Gabriel; arXiv 2302.12087, cs.SE, 2023) —
  abstract-grounded. Gabriel (Patterns of Software, habitability) re-reading
  early Alexander's turn from formalised design thinking toward intuitive
  process, addressed to computer scientists.
  - Speaks to: Q1 (context and lineage rather than the fifteen directly)
  - Transfer: weak as method, strong as framing — the software community's most
    experienced Alexander reader, on why the formal apparatus keeps yielding to
    process.
- *Wholeness as a Hierarchical Graph to Capture the Nature of Space* (Jiang;
  arXiv 1502.03554, 2015) — abstract-grounded; head of the computational line
  that includes *Structural Beauty* (2104.11100, images), *Living Images*
  (2301.01814), and geographic-space applications. Defines a computable degree
  of wholeness/life over recursively defined sub-structures.
  - Speaks to: Q2
  - Transfer: partial — the measures operationalise mainly levels of scale +
    strong centres (recursive "far more smalls than larges"), not all fifteen
    equally; substrates are images and geographic space, not interaction.
- *Beautimeter: Harnessing GPT for Assessing Architectural and Urban Beauty
  based on the 15 Properties of Living Structure* (Jiang; arXiv 2411.19094,
  2024) — abstract-grounded. Prompts GPT with the fifteen as an assessment
  rubric for environments.
  - Speaks to: Q2
  - Transfer: partial — evidence the fifteen can run as a machine-usable
    *checklist*; note this is precisely the direction (symptom reading at best)
    the project treats as secondary.

Also grounded, not in the keep-set: Salingaros, *Unified Architectural Theory*
(2013) — companion text restating the properties for teaching; Remseth,
*Symmetry in Software Platforms as an Architectural Principle* (arXiv
2510.20389, 2025) — platforms as structure-preserving systems, the
Coplien–Zhao framing carried into software architecture; William Saunders'
critical review of The Nature of Order (Harvard Design Magazine, 2002) and
Alexander's reply — the sharpest published scepticism, aimed at the empirical
claims rather than transferability specifically.

## Convergent findings

### For non-geometric media, prior art re-derives properties rather than translating the fifteen

The only sustained academic attempt to carry the properties into a non-spatial
medium (human activity) explicitly flagged that the fifteen are geometrical and
might not survive the crossing — and then did not translate them: it re-ran
Alexander's *method* (examine lively instances, ask how centres strengthen each
other) on domain-native material and got 24 different properties. Alexander
himself produced a different count (eleven, partially overlapping) when he moved
from geometry to colour. The stable, transferable thing in both cases is the
method and the centre concept, not the roster.
Papers: Iba et al. (both behavioral-properties papers); Alexander's colour
properties as internal precedent.

### Software engineering's translation went through symmetry, not the fifteen

Where the properties did reach code, they arrived compressed into
symmetry/symmetry-breaking (Coplien & Zhao, restated 2007, echoed 2025), with
the explicit claim that this substrate fits software better than architectural
metaphor. Notably convergent with Book 2 ch. 17's own elevation of local
symmetries ("the structure of local symmetries may be nearly all there is").
Papers: Coplien & Zhao; Remseth; ch. 17 as internal corroboration.

### Empirical operationalisation exists but is narrow

The computational line measures livingness via recursive centre hierarchies —
in practice levels of scale and strong centres — applied to images, geographic
space, and (via GPT) environments. No paper was found that tests the fifteen as
*generative* operators; every operationalisation is a measurement of finished
structure. The move-set direction is untested territory in the academic
literature.
Papers: Jiang line; Beautimeter.

### Patterns are a viable mining substrate for properties

Iba's derivation route — pattern languages examined for the centre-action
behind each pattern — is a working precedent for the planned corpus experiment
(which patterns embed which transformations), and it cites Book 2's claim that
a pattern language is a way of defining generic centres as its licence.
Papers: Iba et al. (PURPLSOC 2015).

## What this challenges

The index at `docs/language/properties/index.md` says "the same move applies at
several scales of software development." The strongest prior art says something
weaker and more interesting: the same *method* applies at several substrates,
and each substrate may yield its own property set — fifteen for geometry,
eleven for colour, twenty-four for human activity. If the interface-composition
translations start fighting their names, the vocabulary should be allowed to
fork per substrate (parallel corpora, linked) rather than forcing all
substrates through the fifteen. The per-file structure already accommodates
this; the index's framing sentence may eventually need loosening.
