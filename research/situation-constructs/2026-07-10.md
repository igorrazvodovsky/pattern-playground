## Context

A pattern library whose graph edges are explicitly suggestion-grade ("what has been useful in
similar situations", never predicates to match) and read in Alexander's generative-moves
register (applying move A produces a centre move B acts on). Conditions currently hide in
free-text notes and unauthorable decision-tree hints. The strawman under test: edges gain an
optional typed *condition*; patterns gain *resulting-context* (maybe *initiating-situation*)
properties; `situationalHints` on decision-tree `recommends` edges becomes the general condition
mechanism; prose decision trees become authorable sources of conditioned edges.

Specimens any construct must survive: the deletion→undo bundle (one judgement spread across
`precedes`, `recommends`-with-hints, and `related`); the assistance escalation ladder and
flat-navigation→hub-and-spoke ("B takes over when…"); the two condition-bearing held notes
(good-defaults→autofill, live-presentation→generated-content) and searching→navigation-overview's
"fallback if search fails"; notification's two same-typed `recommends` distinguished only by hints.

Questions:

1. Do suggestion-grade conditions drift rule-grade once typed and structured? The epistemic
   stance (hints, not predicates) is the constraint to defend.
2. What does condition-bearing-edge prior art teach (DMN, situation calculus, statechart
   guards, Alexander's unfolding sequences) about where conditions live?
3. Does resulting-context belong on the node or on the edge — one authorable home?
4. Should the condition dimension vocabulary be controlled, given the doc's warning against
   premature tag normalisation?
5. Which register is primary — designer traversal or actor runtime — and what happens where
   they diverge?

## Retrieved sources

External, web-grounded (step-2b canon reads unless noted):

- *A Pattern Language for Pattern Writing*, Meszaros & Doble (PLoPD3, Addison-Wesley 1997;
  full text at [hillside.net](https://hillside.net/index.php/a-pattern-language-for-pattern-writing)) —
  the pattern-form canon on where context lives. Context is mandatory and "often described via
  a 'situation' rather than stated explicitly. Sometimes, the context is described in terms of
  the patterns that have already been applied." Resulting Context is an optional section: "The
  context that we find ourselves in after the pattern has been applied… This sets us up for
  applying more patterns, possibly the next pattern(s) in a language."
  - Speaks to: Q3, Q5. Transfer: *strong* — the form this library's pages descend from.
- *PLML / PLMLx* (Fincher et al., CHI 2003 workshop "Perspectives on HCI Patterns"; DTD
  documentation at [cs.kent.ac.uk](https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/people/staff/saf/patterns/diethelm/plmlx_doc/plml_doc.dtd.html)) —
  the HCI pattern interchange schema. Defines a node-side `resulting-context` element ("the
  context that we find ourselves in after the pattern has been applied. It can include one or
  more new problems to solve") and a `pattern-link` element whose attributes are `patternID`,
  `collection`, `type`, `label` — *no condition slot on links anywhere in the DTD*.
  - Speaks to: Q3. Transfer: *strong* — the closest ancestor of this graph's edge schema.
- *Statecharts: A Visual Formalism for Complex Systems*, Harel (Science of Computer
  Programming 8, 1987; [PDF](https://dubroy.com/refs/Statecharts_a_visual_formalism_for_complex_systems.pdf)) —
  guard conditions attach to transitions (edges); states carry structure. The formalism's whole
  apparatus (hierarchy, orthogonality) exists to manage the combinatorial explosion of
  condition-bearing transitions.
  - Speaks to: Q2. Transfer: *partial* — rule-grade runtime formalism; instructive as the
    fully-evaluated end of the spectrum, not as a model to adopt.
- *Decision Model and Notation* (OMG standard) with *From BPMN process models to DMN decision
  models*, Bazhenova et al. (Information Systems, 2019;
  [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306437918300231)) —
  the field's documented correction to condition-bearing edges. Encoding decisions "through
  process control flow structures" (gateway conditions scattered across sequence-flow edges)
  reduces model maintainability; DMN's answer was *separation of concerns* — extract decision
  logic into first-class decision nodes that the flow references.
  - Speaks to: Q2, Q3. Transfer: *strong on the architectural lesson*, weak on the notation
    itself (DMN tables are deliberately rule-grade — the register this library refuses).
- *Situation calculus* (McCarthy & Hayes 1969; Reiter's basic action theories — see the
  [KR Handbook ch. 16](https://dai.fmph.uniba.sk/~sefranek/kri/handbook/chapter16.pdf)) —
  conditions split into two homes: precondition axioms on actions (`Poss(A(x̄),s) ↔ Π_A(x̄,s)`)
  and successor-state axioms describing what holds after (`F(x̄, do(a,s)) ≡ Φ_F(x̄,a,s)`). And
  the register datum: situations are not world-states but *histories* — "situations are
  first-order terms which denote possible world histories", `do(a,s)` the history extended by
  one action.
  - Speaks to: Q2, Q5. Transfer: *partial* — a logic for machine reasoning, but the
    initiating-situation/resulting-context split and situations-as-histories both transfer.
- *The Nature of Order, Book 2: The Process of Creating Life*, Alexander (2002;
  [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv27ftw6c)) — generative sequences: "the secret lies
  in knowing, as nature does, what must happen in what order." Unfolding is stepwise
  differentiation in which "every center is shaped by the next step in the differentiation" —
  the condition for a step is the wholeness the *preceding steps* produced, judged by the
  builder in situ, never a stored predicate.
  - Speaks to: Q2, Q5. Transfer: *strong* — the register the vocabulary doc already commits to.
- *Adaptive hypermedia authoring* (De Bra et al., AHAM, Hypertext '99; Cristea & Calvi's LAG,

- *Hyper-relational knowledge graphs* — *Query Embedding on Hyper-relational Knowledge Graphs*

## Convergent findings

### 1. Rule-grade is a property of the consumer, not of the field (Q1)

Across every formalism examined, what makes a condition rule-grade is not that it is typed,
structured, or stored — it is that *a machine evaluates it*. Statechart guards gate transitions;
`Poss` axioms gate actions; DMN tables compute outputs; Wikidata qualifiers "reduce the answer
set". Adaptive hypermedia shows the authoring cost of crossing that line: once link conditions
became an evaluated rule language, hand-authoring was explicitly given up on (AHAM), and a
decade of tooling (LAG, MOT) tried to climb back up from "the assembly language of adaptation".
Schmidt gives the criterion for when evaluation is even legitimate: only where interdependencies
can be identified, analysed and modelled *in advance*. Design situations in a pattern library
are exactly Schmidt's unmodellable case — so the construct must stay a map, and the stance is
defensible, but the defence lives in a *consumer contract*, not in the data model: no pipeline
step may evaluate, match, or filter on a condition; conditions render as prose for judgement.
Structured is fine; evaluated is the drift.

Papers: Harel; sitcalc; DMN; hyper-relational KGs; AHAM/LAG; Schmidt (in-house); Shipman &
Marshall (in-house).

*Draft project takeaway*: the epistemic stance survives structuring only as an explicit
consumer contract — conditions may gain shape, but nothing in the pipeline may match on them;
the moment a query filters by condition, the library has minted rules.

### 2. Condition-bearing edges scatter judgement; the field's correction was a first-class decision home (Q2)

The one discipline that lived with conditions distributed across flow edges — BPM, with
condition-bearing gateways — documented the failure (encoding decisions "through process
control flow structures… reducing process model maintainability") and corrected it by
*extracting* decision logic into first-class decision nodes (DMN) that the flow references.
Statecharts tell the same story from the other end: the formalism's machinery exists to manage
transition/guard explosion. Mapped home: this library's prose decision trees are already
DMN-shaped — a tree is a decision node; its `recommends` edges are its outputs; the hints are
its rows. The specimens confirm the scatter cost: the deletion→undo bundle is one judgement
smeared across three carriers, and notification's twin `recommends` are two rows of one
decision distinguishable only by their hints. Prior art says: give the judgement one home and
let edges reference it, rather than making every edge independently condition-bearing.

Papers: DMN/Bazhenova; Harel; specimens (in-graph).

*Draft project takeaway*: the decision tree, not the edge, is the condition's natural home —
formalising "edges gain conditions" without a single referenced judgement home reproduces
pre-DMN BPMN.

### 3. Resulting context is node-side in every pattern-form ancestor (Q3)

The pattern-writing canon (Meszaros & Doble), the HCI interchange schema (PLML), the
Alexandrian form (each pattern's closing links to the patterns it sets up), and the project's
own format-survey canon all place resulting context *on the pattern*. PLML is decisive on the
edge side: `pattern-link` carries `type` and `label` only — the lineage never gave links a
condition slot. The one live edge-side practice (Wikidata qualifiers) comes with exactly the
filter semantics the stance forbids. And Meszaros & Doble put *initiating* context on the node
too: context "described via a 'situation'… sometimes in terms of the patterns that have
already been applied." So the single authorable home is the node — resulting-context and
initiating-situation as pattern properties — and a conditional edge is a *compression of the
join* (the plan already states this: a conditional `precedes` decomposes into A's resulting
context meeting B's initiating situation). Edge-side condition text, if rendered, should be
derived from or reference that home, never be a second place to author the same fact.

Papers: Meszaros & Doble; PLML; Alexander; report-pattern-language-formats (in-house);
hyper-relational KGs (counterpoint).

*Draft project takeaway*: author situations on nodes, render them on edges — the edge is where
the join *shows*, the node is where it is *said*.

### 4. Control the dimensions per judgement, never the values, never corpus-wide (Q4)

The proliferation corpus (Trigg's 75 link types; Shipman & Marshall's formalization costs;
LAG as the tooling debt of a condition *language*) warns against a corpus-wide controlled
vocabulary of conditions — it demands exactly the advance modelling Schmidt rules out, and the
vocabulary doc's own `recommends` rationale ("the library is not mature enough for a controlled
vocabulary of conditions") already encodes the same verdict. But the existing hint shape shows
the workable middle: `{question, branch}` — the *dimension* is a stable authored question
local to one judgement (action-consequences' time-to-recover / scope / cascade), the *value*
stays in the author's words. That is controlled-dimensions-free-values, scoped to the tree that
owns them. Convergence across trees is something to observe post hoc and normalise through the
changelog (the Bowker & Star living-classification stance the doc already cites), not to
pre-legislate.

Papers: Trigg, Shipman & Marshall (in-house); AHAM/LAG; Schmidt (in-house); Meszaros & Doble
("context described via a situation").

*Draft project takeaway*: each decision tree owns its dimensions as authored questions; values
stay prose; no corpus-wide condition vocabulary until repeated convergence is *observed*.

### 5. The primary register is designer traversal; divergence marks a compressed conditional join (Q5)

Three independent traditions define a situation as *the history of moves so far*, not as
runtime state: situation calculus formally ("situations… denote possible world histories",
`do(a,s)`); Meszaros & Doble's context "described in terms of the patterns that have already
been applied"; Alexander's unfolding, where each step's condition is the wholeness the
preceding steps produced. So the vocabulary's `precedes` reads primarily as the designer's
traversal — which is what the doc's generative-moves commitment already says. The runtime
sequence is the *evidence* for the design-time claim (Schmidt's routine-conditions point: the
enactment is where the precomputed claim is tested), and A′ found the two coincide on nearly
every kept edge. Where they diverge — the escalation ladder, flat-navigation→hub-and-spoke,
where B *replaces* A under a condition rather than acting on A's product — the plain generative
claim is false as stated ("applying good-defaults produces a centre AI completion acts on" is
not what the author meant) and true only through the join: *A's resulting context under a
condition is B's initiating situation*. Register divergence is therefore a detector, not a
dilemma: an edge the two registers read differently is a conditional join wearing a plain type,
and wants decomposing through the node-side constructs rather than re-typing.

Papers: sitcalc; Meszaros & Doble; Alexander; Schmidt (in-house); A′ sweep record (in-house).

*Draft project takeaway*: `precedes` speaks in the designer's register; when the actor-runtime
reading disagrees, the edge is a compressed conditional join — decompose it through
resulting-context/initiating-situation instead of arguing about its type.

### 6. Rider — hosting is spatial inclusion, which the meronymy canon explicitly excludes (workstream C input)

Winston, Chaffin & Herrmann distinguish meronymic relations from *spatial inclusion* (and from
attachment and ownership): "the wine in the cooler" is contained, not part. The hosting claim —
*where a move's trigger is surfaced* — is locative, not compositional, so every A′ shunt of a
hosting note into `enables` (component-integral) mis-stated the authored claim in the doc's own
theoretical terms; that is *why* each shunt lost data. Prior art thus confirms hosting as a
distinct relational family. Whether it lands as a `hosts` edge type or as a clause of the
hosting composition's resulting context stays the schema sitting's choice — but "keep absorbing
it into `enables`" is now evidenced against, from the same source the part–whole audit already
stands on.

Papers: Winston, Chaffin & Herrmann; A′ sweep record (in-house).

*Draft project takeaway*: hosting is inclusion, not parthood — stop shunting it into `enables`;
give it either its own relation or a resulting-context clause when the situation constructs land.

## Breakage-question verdicts

1. *Drift* — answered. Not inevitable: drift happens at evaluation, not at structuring. Defence
   is a stated consumer contract (render, never match) plus values kept in the author's words.
2. *Condition-bearing-edge prior art* — answered. Conditions on edges are the pre-correction
   state of every discipline that tried them at scale; the correction is a first-class decision
   home the edges reference. The library's decision trees already are that home.
3. *Node vs edge* — answered. Node, unanimously across the pattern-form lineage; edge-side
   condition text is a rendering of the join, not an authorable field.
4. *Controlled dimension vocabulary* — answered. Per-tree controlled dimensions (questions),
   free values, no corpus-wide vocabulary; normalise post hoc through the changelog.
5. *Register* — answered. Designer traversal is primary (three traditions define situation as
   move-history); runtime is the evidence; divergence between the two is the signal that an
   edge is a compressed conditional join to be decomposed, not re-typed.
