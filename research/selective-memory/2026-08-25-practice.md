## What the products actually hold

The most consequential practice finding is that "memory" in one product is not one governable surface.

*ChatGPT* runs several parallel stores. The bio tool — "Saved memories" — is the itemised list exposed in Manage Memory. Alongside it sit system-injected sections (Assistant Response Preferences, Notable Past Conversation Topics, Helpful User Insights, Recent Conversation Content, User Interaction Metadata) that have no corresponding surface in the product. The Helpful User Insights block holds inferred attributes with explicit confidence scores. The only way to see any of it is to prompt the model to dump it verbatim. [embracethered.com](https://embracethered.com/blog/posts/2025/chatgpt-how-does-chat-history-memory-preferences-work/)

The chat-history tier does not retrieve per query. It maintains a rolling summary of prior conversations — roughly the 40 most recent — and injects that whole summary into every new chat. Saved memories are available on the free tier; the uninspectable rolling dossier is Plus and Pro only. Simon Willison, reading his own: "This is an *extraordinary* amount of detail for the model to have accumulated by me… Has there ever been a consumer product that's *this* capable of building up a human-readable profile of its users?" [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/21/chatgpt-new-memory/)

So the seed's premise — that what the system remembers is the actor's to inspect — is currently false in the flagship product. The part that is inspectable is the part the actor wrote.

Because memory is written by a tool the model can call, indirect prompt injection can commit persistent facts to an actor's memory without their consent, producing list entries they never authored.

## Named forms, and what each does

Four shipping shapes, differing in what the unit of curation is. What holds them together is not the control but the situation each answers: the actor gets a handle on state the system wrote without them. The editable-summary form is the one that tests this hardest, because it offers no per-item handle at all — but it is still a handle on system-written state, and its weakness is a consequence of choosing a coarse unit, not evidence of a different move.

Note that this inventory does not match the seed's `## Forms` line. Two of the seed's four forms are unsupported: nothing in the corpus documents *graph views of what has accumulated*, and per-message or per-thread scope does not ship — what ships is workspace scope and mode.

1. *Itemised snippet list with per-item edit and delete.* ChatGPT and Gemini. The unit is a "memory snippet" — a model-decided, rephrased extract of a user message. This is where the correction problem lives, because the item is an inference the actor never wrote in those words.
2. *Editable natural-language summary.* Claude keeps "a memory summary to capture all its memories in one place for you to view and edit", amended by chatting rather than by per-item controls. A distinct form with a distinct weakness for the wrong-fact-that-won't-die: there is no per-item handle.
3. *Scope set by where the actor works.* Claude gives each project its own separate memory store — "your product launch planning stays separate from client work". OpenAI shipped project-only memory in August 2025. Scope is a property of the workspace, not of the message.
4. *Mode.* ChatGPT's Temporary Chat suppresses both memory writes and training use. Claude's Incognito chat is "available to all Claude users" — including plans without memory at all, so the ephemerality control is decoupled from the feature it scopes.

Claude also treats held-forward state as portable actor-owned data, with documented import from another AI tool and export for backup or migration. [claude.com/blog/memory](https://claude.com/blog/memory)

## The defaults, and one counter-example

Claude's memory is off by default and opt-in — "fully optional, with granular user controls". That is the opposite default from ChatGPT's, whose documentation says it "may save those details as a memory without you needing to ask", and it is a live shipping counter-example to the assumption that this territory must be on-by-default.

Gemini's retention default sits in the middle of its own range: activity older than 18 months is auto-deleted unless the actor opens the control and picks 3 months, 36 months, or never. An actor who never curates gets 18 months by inaction. [support.google.com](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13278892)

## Named failures, documented by the vendors themselves

- *Ephemeral mode that still keeps things.* Gemini: "Even when Keep Activity is off, your conversations will be saved with your account for up to 72 hours." Those retained copies do not appear in the user-facing activity dashboard.
- *Deletion that starts a process rather than completing one.* Gemini describes removing the item from view and then beginning a separate storage-deletion process — what the actor sees disappear and what is gone are different events at different times.
- *Deleting the conversation does not delete what was learned from it.* ChatGPT's own dialogue admits it: "This will delete [chat]. To clear any memories from this chat, visit your settings."
- *Correct-but-unwanted continuity.* Willison needed the model naive for an experiment "and then realized that it could tell I was in Half Moon Bay from my previous chats, so I had to run the whole experiment again from scratch!" Nothing was wrong, so no correction or deletion affordance addresses it. The remedy he asked for was scope, which OpenAI later shipped. This is the clearest practice case that the seed's `initiating` situation — which names only a *wrong* remembered fact — is too narrow.
- *Opting out costs capability.* With Gemini's Keep Activity off, the assistant narrows to a named short list of surfaces. Non-retention is not a neutral toggle.

## Where the catalogues put the boundary

Shape of AI does not settle the split question — it does both things at once. Its Memory page models scope as a three-tier axis inside one pattern (global; scoped to workspace, project or conversation; ephemeral), naming a trade-off for the middle tier rather than treating scoping as pure gain: scoped memory "prevents irrelevant carryover but may reduce convenience". Ephemeral memory is described as retaining "context only for the current session, similar to incognito mode". And yet Incognito Mode is also maintained as a separate, separately-linked pattern.

The same page makes after-the-fact curation constitutive rather than optional, and ties it to visibility: "When users can see what the AI has captured, they should be able to curate it… Memory should never be a black box." Its remedy for accumulated error, though, is bulk rather than per-item — "provide a 'reset memory' option that clears remembered data in one step".

Two cautions on using it. Its failure modes are asserted without evidence, escalating to a claim that uncontrolled memory leads to "AI psychosis". And its product survey is already out of date: the Claude and Perplexity entries describe products that have since shipped full memory. [shapeof.ai/patterns/memory](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/memory)

## A boundary the products confirm

Gemini separates the two channels explicitly: with retention off, "any Gems you create will still be saved to your account. Any chats you create with Gems will not be saved." The behaviour-tuning artefact and the remembered-conversation artefact are governed by different controls in a shipping surface — which supports keeping `ai-tuning` as a `complements` neighbour rather than folding the two together.

## Takeaways for the pattern page

- Name the store-versus-surface gap as a force. What is inspectable is a subset of what is held, and in the flagship product the inspectable part is the part the actor wrote.
- Order the forms by unit of curation — item, summary, workspace, mode — rather than by product. They are not variants of one control and they fail differently; what makes them one pattern is the situation, not the mechanism.
- Correct the `## Forms` line. Graph views have no support anywhere in the corpus, and per-message scope does not ship; workspace scope and the editable summary do, and the seed names neither.
- Use Claude's off-by-default memory and Incognito-without-memory as the counter-examples that keep the defaults-govern force from reading as inevitable.
- The Half Moon Bay case earns a place in the situation: continuity that is accurate and still unwanted is the trigger correction cannot serve.
