## What this adds to the conceptual lineage

The ancestor result is narrow and expected: both papers reach back to the
NoteCards system report, which is the substrate Halasz is reflecting on and the
case Shipman & Marshall draw their evidence from.

The descendants are the useful half, and they were not in the note's reading.
Both are Buckingham Shum's line, and both are attempts to make typed links work
after the failure Halasz recorded — *ScholOnto* by giving the types a scholarly
ontology, *Facilitated Hypertext* by putting a human facilitator between the
author and the type. The note's conclusion was that typed-link authoring
imposes a formalisation cost users are justified in refusing. These two papers
accept that finding and propose two different ways around it: change what the
types mean, or change who applies them.

That second option is the one this project has not considered. The corpus
currently assumes the author types their own links. A facilitated or
system-proposed typing step is a design position with fifteen years of
hypertext work behind it, and it is worth a read before the vocabulary settles.
