# Shaping the pattern pages

## Intent

Bring every `role: pattern` page onto the shape settled on 2026-10-10, one page per sitting. The shape is Alexander's page form taken for what is useful here, not reproduced: an opening paragraph that is the initiating situation, a section mark, a bold problem essence, an account of the pattern, `Therefore,` and a bold instruction, the realisation copy after it, and a closing connections passage that is the resulting situation, with the typed index folded beneath it. Both situations are frontmatter prose; the typed edges are the part of each the vocabulary can carry.

The rule is written and the machinery is built, and the shape has held on four pages (wizard, hub and spoke, form, deep linking) and on twelve probe drafts. Each sitting is still a test of the shape as much as a migration: when a page shows the rule, the situations, the edge sentences or the plugin should move, they move, and the steps below are changed with them. This file states the procedure as it currently stands; the sittings record says only what happened on each page.

The work on each page is judgement: what the page currently says has to be re-told in the shape without losing what it knows. That is why this is a sitting per page and not a sweep.

## What the two ends are for

The shape takes the book's page layout. What the layout presupposes, and what each end does, is easy to lose under the mechanics, so it is stated here and the steps refer back to it.

- *The opening says what this pattern does for the larger ones, and why.* Most of the book's openings contain a sentence of the form "this pattern gives / takes / completes / helps form", often with the mechanism ("since every door, if glazed and near a window, creates a pool of light"). Naming the wholes is not enough; the opening says the contribution.
- *The opening is a moment, addressed to someone in the middle of making.* "Now it is time to fix the entrance." "Imagine that you are standing in the built-up frame of a partly constructed building." The initiating situation is the state of the thing being made and which patterns got it there, not the properties of a task described in the third person. A test: could the opening begin with "now"? It may also narrate a dynamic rather than a structure (a community growing under earlier patterns until stars form where the paths meet).
- *The closing is instruction, not description.* The book's closings begin with make, place, treat, give, use, build: the Therefore continued and delegated to the smaller patterns, sometimes as an ordered procedure. They grade the delegation (above all, perhaps, if possible, where appropriate). Their job, in the book's own words, is to make sure the maker's conception leaves room for the smaller patterns later. A resulting situation written as a state is the weaker form; prefer the imperative where the neighbour is a part or an onward move.
- *The headline is a claim that can be wrong.* "People will always gravitate to those rooms which have light on two sides." The essence states what people do and what fails without the pattern, so that it can be argued against; it is not a neutral description of a situation.
- *The two ends presuppose an order.* The book has a third kind of neighbour, the patterns of the same size that surround one, and no slot for it, because every pattern has a number: an earlier sibling goes in the opening, a later one in the closing, and a reader sees from the number which way a link points. This language has sequences, not a total order, and its links carry no direction cue. So a same-level partner that is used together with the pattern is named in the opening (Building Complex opens with "this pattern and the next one must be used together"), a lateral neighbour that works on what the pattern leaves behind gets its sentence in the passage, and every sentence says which way it points.
- *Reciprocity is not maintained.* A quarter of the book's closing links are answered by an opening on the far side. A hub's opening names the few larger patterns that give the reason it exists, not the ones that cite it. The index carries the rest; the opening does not try to.
- *The caution links upward.* "Don't let this pattern make your plans too wild, or you will destroy Positive Outdoor Space." A caution may name the larger patterns the instruction, overdone, would damage, and the later patterns its success depends on. Pattern-level tension has no edge; the caution is where it is said.
- *Variants that argue the invariant come before the instruction.* The book's body holds "the range of different ways the pattern can be manifested" because the invariant is what is common to them. Only recipe-like realisation goes after the passage. A measure that is a limit the pattern depends on (three minutes' walk, 16 inches) goes in the bold instruction.
- *Compression.* One element may realise two patterns at once ("you can kill two birds with one stone by using the walls as seats"). The situations have no slot for it, so a sitting that splits a page's parts out asks first whether the part is this pattern compressed with another.

Three read-aloud tests follow from this and are the finished-page check, ahead of the mechanical ones: does the opening say what this pattern does for the larger ones and where the maker now stands; does the passage tell the maker what to do next and what to leave room for, in the imperative; can the instruction be followed by someone who read only the two bold paragraphs.

What the book does not do, and this plan does on purpose: it never defines a pattern against its neighbours (no look-alike sentences), has no chooser pages and no alternative relation, lets a closing end in an open class ("and many others"), and tells the reader to write a missing pattern in themselves. The look-alike slot, the seeding tests and the chooser question are this language's own boundary work, and they are where sittings spend their time. That cost is accepted, not inherited.

The sittings are also where the language finds out what it is missing. A pattern is defined by what it connects to: what has to be in place before it is worth reaching for, and what acts on what it leaves behind. Writing both situations forces those connections to be named, and a connection that has no page to link is a gap in the language, not a sentence to leave vague or a link to force onto the nearest page. Each sitting therefore seeds the patterns its situations call for, on either side, and records them below. The same pressure can move the pattern itself: a situation that will not come out in one piece is a sign the page holds two moves, a resulting situation nothing acts on is a sign the move is a part of something larger, and a situation that reads the same as a neighbour's is a sign either that the two are one or that both are parts of a larger pattern the language does not yet have. The sitting weighs merging against seeding the parent and records which it chose. A sitting is allowed to redraw the page's boundary when the situations show it should, and the record says when it did.

Seeding lengthens the migration: the probes found three to seven gaps per round, and a page with a recovery or anatomy list may take two sessions. That is expected and not a cost to minimise; the project's goal is the graph, not the finish of the page set.

## Out of scope

Two kinds of page are not shaped under this plan:

- *Chooser pages.* A chooser is a page whose account argues a choice between other patterns rather than making a move of its own: status feedback (indication, validation, notification), and the choice over the ways to show an action that does not apply (unavailable actions, disabled state, explanation), which has no page yet. The shape fits them and that is the problem: a confident pattern page gets written for a judgement that belongs in one place. The arm pages of a choice wait too, since shaping an arm means arguing the choice in its account. A sitting that finds its page is a chooser records that in the sittings record and stops. Choosers are handled separately, together with whatever replaces decision trees. A shaped page may still link a chooser in its passage as a citation. The book's precedent for that separate work: the choice among House Cluster, Row Houses and Housing Hill is made in the parent's closing ("use this as it is up to 15 houses per acre; at higher densities modify it with Row Houses or Housing Hill") and in each arm's opening ("the detached homes of a House Cluster will not work here, because they are not dense enough"), and the arms layer rather than exclude. A chooser may therefore dissolve into its parent's resulting situation and its arms' openings, as the navigation models did; the bottleneck patterns (Building Complex, Structure Follows Social Spaces) show a routing page that keeps a problem and an instruction of its own.
- *Decision-tree machinery.* Decision trees are to be replaced; the replacement is not yet planned. A sitting on a page that owns a tree keeps the tree in the slot after Therefore, names component leaves in words, and removes a `decision-trees` entry whose diagram is gone. Nothing is invested in the tree extractor: its per-tree line is a count of paths, not edges, and component leaves are dropped at emit without a warning.

## A sitting

One page, one session. The work is reading first and writing second.

1. *Read the page as it is*, with its frontmatter, and the committed version if the working copy differs (`git show HEAD:<path>`, never a stash). List what the page knows: situations, clauses, notes on edges, variants, states, components, constraints, evidence, demos, to-dos. If the account turns out to argue a choice between other patterns rather than a move, the page is a chooser: record that and stop.
2. *Read the page's neighbours*: every pattern it links to or is linked from, the sequences it appears in (the folded index on a shaped page, the foot on an unshaped one; read the rendered Appears in, since sequences name a pattern by `rule` and by `constituents` slug and a search for `rule:` misses the second). The opening and the passage are written from these. Sort the incoming connections before writing, each into one of four: named in the opening (a whole, the predecessor whose product this acts on, a same-level partner used together with it, the one alternative, and a sequence only when it helps explain the context), named in the passage (a part, an onward pattern, a lateral neighbour that works on what this leaves behind, a look-alike), left to the index (a one-directional relation, which on a widely used pattern is most of them, and is normal: a hub's opening names the few larger patterns that give the reason it exists, not the ones that cite it), or wrong on the far side (the type or the sentence misreads the relation; note it on that page's to-do, do not edit it here). The sitting record names the fourth group; the counts are not recorded. A neighbour that wrote a sentence about this page outranks one that only authored an edge when the opening would otherwise run long. A sequence step that relies on the pattern without naming it gets an entry in [2026-08-sequence-map.md](2026-08-sequence-map.md) rather than a link in the opening. List the page's old to-do candidates for missing patterns too, and settle each one in step 7 or 8.
3. *Write the initiating situation* in frontmatter: where the maker now stands (what has been made and which larger patterns made it so), what this pattern does for the wholes it is part of and the predecessors whose product it acts on, with the mechanism where it is not obvious, a same-level partner only where the two are used together, at most one alternative, and a sequence only where naming it helps explain the context (never as a list of appearances, since the folded index carries those). Other lateral neighbours wait for the passage or the index. Address the maker at the moment of reaching for the pattern: the opening should be able to begin with "now". Conditions of applicability (when the pattern serves) are not the opening; they belong in the account. One to three sentences is the norm; a page with many incoming connections keeps the opening short and lets the index carry them. It starts with a typed `… ` and runs on lowercase; the renderer adds nothing. A larger pattern or a ruled-out alternative that has no page, or whose nearest page describes a different move, is seeded rather than linked to a poor fit; see *Seeding in a sitting* below.
4. *Write the problem essence*: one bold paragraph after the first `* * *`, a claim about what people do and what fails without the pattern, stated so that it could be argued against rather than as a neutral description of a situation.
5. *Write the account before Therefore*: when the pattern serves and when it constrains, what it costs, what is known and how far it is trusted, with contrary evidence stated as part of the account, and the range of ways the pattern shows up where that range is the argument for what the instruction holds constant. Headings are allowed where they help; bold run-in labels in lists are not (use italic). For an established pattern this is short. An old `## Behavioural position` becomes the account, with its links into foundation anchors kept.
6. *Write the instruction*: `Therefore,` on its own line, then one bold paragraph, including any measure that is a limit the pattern depends on. A caution may follow in plain prose; it may link the larger patterns the instruction, overdone, would damage, and the later patterns its success depends on.
7. *Sort the realisation, then place it*: after Therefore only the caution and one figure, a canonical demo or, on a page that owns one, the decision tree. Go through the old `## Variants`, `## Forms`, `## States`, `## Structural components`, `## Design constraints`, `## Metrics`, `## Guidance`, `## Repair affordances` and similar sections item by item: a variant that shows what the instruction holds constant across different realisations goes into the account before Therefore, a limit on the instruction goes into the caution, a restatement into the instruction, a part another pattern describes into the resulting situation with that pattern linked and a `composed-of` edge, or a `hosts` edge where the page is the surface a move lives on rather than a whole the move is part of (form hosts validation; wizard is composed of it) (a strained fit, or no fitting page, means seeding the missing pattern rather than forcing the link), component anatomy into `realised_by` or `## Related components`. What remains goes under `## In practice`, reworded, not dropped, with subheadings where it helps; measures from `## Metrics` go there as well. A part that comes before the move in time (the selection a deletion acts on) is told in the opening rather than the passage, with its `composed-of` edge as usual. On a page that owns a decision tree, the passage links the tree's leaves by where they sit in it and does not restate the conditions the tree carries. A leaf that is a component rather than a pattern (notification's dialog and callout, form's controls) is named in the passage as a word and cited with `<ComponentRef>` in `## In practice`; it has no edge and the leaf map does not resolve it.
8. *Write the resulting situation* in frontmatter: every clause of the old list said as prose, every part and onward pattern told as the next thing to do, in the imperative where it reads naturally, graded where the delegation is optional or conditional (above all, perhaps, if, where), the lateral neighbours that work on what the pattern leaves behind, the problems the pattern opens, the look-alikes it is not. The passage's job is to tell the maker what to leave room for. A sentence says which way its link points, since the link itself does not. Its last sentence ends with a typed `…` and no closing period; the renderer adds nothing. An onward pattern that acts on this one's product and has no page is seeded, as in step 7; a problem the pattern opens that an existing page should answer but does not is noted on that page's to-do. Each sentence that links an edge target is that edge's sentence, shown beside the connection here and quoted on the far page where that page has no sentence of its own; a sentence that will be quoted reads better without a dangling pronoun, and neighbours that share a sentence share one index line (the four moves a form hosts). Write the passage as prose first; a sentence may bundle several delegated moves, as the book's closings do. A situation may also link a pattern it has no edge to; the link is a citation and gets no index entry.
9. *Clean the relationships*: each `sets-up` target becomes an entry under `precedes`; notes whose content the prose now carries come off; a note that says something the prose does not is either written into the prose or kept. Never author a condition in a note. A pair joined by a directed type and a symmetric one keeps the directed type only. Before authoring a stronger type on a pair, look at the far page: the extractor's subsumption dedup drops a `related` edge the neighbour authored on the same pair, and its note with it, without a warning, so carry anything that note says into the prose first. Do not author the inverse of an edge the far page already authors. A note on the far page that the new prose makes redundant is recorded on that page's to-do for its own sitting, not edited here. "Takes over when this stops" is `complements` or `alternative`, not `precedes`.
10. *Remove what the shape replaces*: the body's trailing `* * *` and any body passage, `## Problem`, `## Forces`, `## Solution`, `## Consequences`, `## Behavioural position`, and the lead paragraph the opening now renders. These go by kind, not by exact title: a section that states the problem, its forces or its fit (`## The problem`, `## When to use`, `## Support & transitions`) is read into the opening, essence or account before it goes. Forces may keep a heading where real tensions make the problem hard, as a `###` under the rendered *Problem* mark, never a `##`; a short list takes a lead-in sentence instead. Sections that hold realisation (`## Metrics`, `## Guidance`, `## Repair affordances`) are sorted under step 7, not deleted. Before dissolving a section, search the corpus for inbound `#anchor` links to it and repoint them. Keep `## In practice`, `## Related components`, `## Research on this pattern`, `## Resources & references`, `## To-do`.
11. *Run the checks* below, restart the dev server if the frontmatter schema or the plugin changed, and read the rendered page once from top to bottom, including the far side of each edge sentence.

Checklist for the finished page, the three read-aloud tests first:

- The opening says what this pattern does for the larger ones and where the maker now stands; it could begin with "now".
- The passage tells the maker what to do next and what to leave room for, mostly in the imperative, and grades what is optional.
- The instruction can be followed by someone who read only the two bold paragraphs, and the essence could be argued against.
- The opening grounds the pattern in the larger patterns it follows or helps complete (and names a sequence only when it helps explain the context), names a lateral neighbour only as a partner used together with it, runs one to three sentences, and opens with a typed `… `.
- The passage's last sentence ends with a typed `…` and no period (a link-final sentence is fine; the dots follow the link).
- Exactly two bold paragraphs: the essence and the instruction, and no bold run-in labels in lists either.
- Every clause of the old resulting list is said somewhere in the prose.
- Every edge the page authors is linked in its situations; the extractor reports none unspoken.
- No pair carries both a directed and a symmetric type; the extractor reports 0.
- After Therefore, only the caution and at most one demo; further demos sit in `## In practice`, or one above the opening paragraph.
- The passage is the last thing before the tail sections, and the body does not end in `* * *`.
- No `sets-up` remains in the frontmatter; `precedes` is authored under `relationships`.
- The voice holds: plain practitioner language, "actor", one addressee.
- Every pattern either situation names has a page of its own, seeded in this sitting if it did not exist, and no link stands in for a pattern the language does not have.
- The page is connected above and below its level: at least one `enables` edge in either direction (a part it is made of, or a whole it helps complete) or a sequence appearance. A page with neither is isolated, and the vocabulary's skip arms are the only accepted reasons: a minimal primitive is the floor and links upward only; an unbounded stance has no level. Choice-arm primitives are out of this plan and are not held to this.

### Seeding in a sitting

A seed made in a sitting is written from the side that called for it, so the connection exists on both ends from the start. A pattern seeded from a page's resulting situation gets its initiating situation written, because the sentence that called for it already is that situation told from the other end; a pattern seeded from an initiating situation gets its resulting situation, since it is the larger move whose product the page acts on. The other situation waits for the seed's own sitting. Beyond that a seed carries what [resuming](../../apps/patterns/src/content/patterns/resuming.mdx) and [review before submitting](../../apps/patterns/src/content/patterns/review-before-submitting.mdx) carry: `seed: true`, `evidence: [observed]`, a disclosure saying which page it was split from and that it has not been looked at closely, a paragraph or two of body stating the move, the edge back to the page that seeded it (the seeding page authors that edge; the seed adds only a reverse note through the inverse alias where it needs different words, and never a second edge of its own, which for symmetric types would store the pair twice), and a `## To-do` naming what the seed does not yet know, including where it ends and its nearest neighbours begin. A seed is not the place to settle those neighbours; the to-do is.

Three tests before seeding, in order:

- *Is there a page already?* Search by the move, not the name: a part described without a link on a shaped page is the usual case (wizard's five). If a page covers the move, link it and write the sentence.
- *Is the nearest page the same move?* A page that shares the domain but describes a different move is a strained fit (saving against resuming; change review against review before submitting). Seed, and name the distinction in the seed's relationships note and to-do.
- *Is it this pattern compressed with another?* One element may realise two patterns at once (a sitting wall that is also the seat spot; an undo toast that is also the status feedback). A part that is another pattern compressed into this one is linked as that pattern and the compression said in the sentence; it is not split out as a seed.
- *Is it a pattern at all?* A part that is only this pattern's anatomy, with no life outside it, stays in `## In practice` or `realised_by`. A seed has to be reachable from more than one page, or plausibly so; the wizard's completion step is held back on this test until another page needs it. Holding back is a deferral, not a verdict: a language loses coherence by dropping its smaller and intermediate moves for economy, and the parts that stay in `## In practice` are where that loss would happen. The reread after five or six sittings goes through every `## In practice` section for parts that recur across pages, and seeds those.

A seed closes the gap it was made for and nothing else. A sitting on a page that is itself a seed records the parent candidates it finds in its to-do and does not seed them. A seed's written situation does not render until the seed is shaped (an open item below), so the seed's body states the move in a sentence or two that does not depend on it. Writing its second situation, gathering its sources and sorting its own realisation is a sitting of its own, picked by the same priorities as any other page. A shaped seed carries the mark on its title (§The confidence mark), the hedge in the disclosure line and a sentence in the account. A seed is not a hypothesis about the move, which is in use in many systems, but a page whose shaping has not yet had the author's own judgement, and the seed marker says exactly that.

The unspoken-edge advisory counts a link anywhere in the file, the to-do, `## In practice`, `description` and frontmatter notes included (it reads the raw file, not the body), so a zero does not show that each edge is spoken in the situations, where its sentence comes from. ## Testing the shape first

Before any ordering by priority, pick pages that fit the shape badly, because those are where the back and forth will happen. Each candidate stresses a different part; the probes of 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-19 drafted one page per stress outside the repo, and the shape held on all of them in source and in render, so the stresses are kept for the real sittings rather than as doubts about the shape:

- A conversation-family page: thirteen patterns whose initiating situation is which stage the encounter has reached. The opening can be a stage in a sequence rather than a structure in place. Abort's opening covers both a stop in conversation and a stop of an agent run, which two sequences route to it; possibly two moves, and the abort sitting decides.
- A page that owns a decision tree (the deletion family): the tree sits in the figure slot after Therefore, and the page keeps its own opening; the tree arm removes the leaves' openings only, and only where the tree is the leaf's only route in (undo is a deletion leaf but is reached from at least five other pages).
- A page with many demos and variants (deletion, item view or data view): the order after Therefore, and whether the passage still reads as the close.
- A `seed: true` page: what the two bold paragraphs look like when the shaping has not had the author's judgement.
- A page with many incoming edges and few outgoing (undo): whether the far pages' sentences read well in this page's index.
- Done: a page whose body describes several parts without links (form), and a page the sequences lean on (deep linking).

One test runs across pages rather than on one. The shape is read a page at a time, but a language is validated by reading a cluster upward: the parts first and the whole last, so that each larger pattern is met with its constituents already accepted. The wizard sitting produced such a cluster, wizard with its five linked parts and two seeds. Read it bottom-up: each part's page, then wizard's opening and passage, and check that the edge sentences and openings chain across the levels instead of reading well in isolation. The parts' own openings do not yet lead up to wizard, since only form is shaped. Do this again at the reread with whichever cluster the sittings have grown largest.

When a sitting changes the rule or the machinery, change the steps above and the rule, and note in the sittings record only that it happened. After five or six sittings, reread the rule against the record and settle what has held, reread the seeds record for seeds that have since merged, split, or turned out to be anatomy, and sweep the `## In practice` sections for parts that recur.

## Order after that

Pick by where the next sitting is anyway. Two useful priorities:

- Pages the sequences lean on hardest (the shared stations in [2026-08-sequence-map.md](2026-08-sequence-map.md)), because their openings will name several sequences and test the opening's form. Count appearances from the rendered Appears in.
- Pages with `sets-up` clauses, because each one moves a `precedes` edge into `relationships` and retires a use of the earlier form.

## The far-side display

Decided 2026-10-19. The index beside a connection shows this page's note or its own sentence; where the page has neither, it quotes the far page's sentence in quotation marks, so a sentence written for another page's reader is read as that page's words and not as this page's. The edge carries a sentence per endpoint (`situation` from the side read first, `farSituation` from the other when both link), so no page loses its own words.

The display is kept because it is the instrument of the sitting's second step: on a page with many incoming connections the quoted sentences are how the language uses the pattern, and the sitting reads them to write its own opening. A seed with no passage is defined by its callers' quoted sentences. The book shows that a hub's own opening need not answer them.

What the decision retires. The sentence-shape rules existed because a sentence was shown unattributed on another page: that it must stand alone with no opening "it", "this" or "nor", that it links neighbours of one type only, that it opens with a capital. A quotation carries its own context, so these become authoring advice, not checks: a sentence that will be quoted reads better without a dangling pronoun, and that is all. The one-relationship-per-sentence advisory is informational; the book's closings bundle several delegated moves in one sentence and so may a passage. The directed-plus-symmetric pair check stays, since it is about edges, not sentences. The imperative passage quotes well: on the part's page, "End with a review of every answer, each linked back to its step" beside the wizard is the wizard's instruction, read as such.

## The confidence mark

Decided 2026-10-19. The book marks each title with two, one or no asterisks for how far the solution is an invariant, and tells the reader to treat the unmarked ones with some disrespect. Here the mark is on the title too, and nowhere else: a seed's title wears a dashed underline, the mark the library already gives a proposed name and the graph gives a seed node. It derives from `seed` alone. No ladder is added: the spec refuses rungs nothing occupies, `evidence` names kinds and not degrees, and every page with a `disclosure` is a seed, so the disclosure stays prose for the reader who reads on. A shaped seed therefore no longer reads in its skim path like an established pattern; the title says it has not had the author's judgement before the bold paragraphs claim anything. If a middle rung ever fills (a page the author has shaped but will not stand behind), it is declared then, through the spec, not now.
