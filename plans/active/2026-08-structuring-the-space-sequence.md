# Structuring the space — the second sequence

Phase 5 of [the sequence-map plan](2026-08-sequence-map.md): the first sequence authored after the pilot. The sequence's authoritative home is `apps/patterns/src/content/sequences/structuring-the-space.mdx`; this document is the authoring trace — the sequence as first written, the read-aloud walk, the test walks with their backtracks, and the corpus changes the authoring surfaced. The corpus changes are recorded here, not executed — each is judgment work for its own sitting.

Steps are decisions, written as verbs, each invoking a pattern as its rule. Constituents ride inside steps in parentheses. The trunk's second position is the representation's first choice point. As first authored it delegated its judgement to [navigation-overview]'s decision tree; finding 8 (2026-09-24) records why that tree dissolved and the position became a recognition step, and the sequence file is authoritative for the current wording. The A2 text below is kept as the authoring trace.

## Presupposition (the head)

The information architecture is settled: the actor's objects, activities, and labels are named (the information-architecture foundation records this; its `precedes` edges into workspace, searching, and localization state initiating situations, not steps). And the product's posture is tool-shaped — a spatial surface is the primary frame. A conversation-first product does not walk this sequence from the top; it invokes structure from inside the encounter, as the map's ordering section says. A product that cannot state the naming work is not ready for this sequence; that work is process-register and is discharged upstream.

The sequence enacts one quality per sub-sequence, near enough: *privacy* at the partition (the boundary carries access), *agency* at the choice point (each model is a distribution of control over movement), *shareability* at the addressing pass (a link can only carry state that has a name).

## The sequence

### A. Laying out the space — the trunk

1. *Partition the work into contexts.* Boundaries follow how attention and responsibility actually divide — ownership, topic, activity — so entering one context puts the rest out of the way rather than out of mind. The boundary carries access as well as attention: partitioning the work also partitions visibility. Movement *between* contexts belongs to this step — the switcher, not the topology choice. Rule: [workspace]. Optional — a product whose work forms one context takes the decision and moves on. (Constituent: hub-and-spoke — the switcher over the contexts; the workspace list is a hub, each context a spoke.)
2. *Choose each space's navigation model.* A choice point, taken once per context; the judgement belongs to [navigation-overview]'s decision tree, and the choice starts one step before topology — the shape of what is being navigated decides whether there is a topology to choose at all. Exclusive per space; a product that needs two models in different regions is combining them, which is C's territory. The arms:
   - *Navigate by viewport* — one continuous space; movement is viewport control. Rule: [pan-and-zoom]. Orientation and addressability arrive as obligations: the wider viewport kept in step is the coupling handoff into composing views, and position and scale must be made nameable before B can touch them.
   - *Prescribe the order* — a sequence with dependencies. Rule: [step-by-step].
   - *Offer the sequence, allow jumps* — a sequence without strict dependencies. Rule: [pyramid].
   - *Dissolve into the collection* — one collection browsed item by item; the topology question dissolves and the design continues in composing views' splitting sub-sequence. Rule: [overview-detail].
   - *File into a hierarchy* — a deep hierarchy of many pages. Rule: [multilevel-tree].
   - *Keep one surface* — a single workspace with a rich toolset. Rule: [flat-navigation]. Holds only while the tools fit one surface; C owns what happens when they stop.
   - *Connect everything to everything* — heterogeneous sections, routine cross-section journeys. Rule: [fully-connected].
   - *Route through a hub* — independent sections, rare cross-section journeys. Rule: [hub-and-spoke]. The hub becomes the natural reporting surface across the spokes — the handoff toward the curation chain.

*Resulting context.* The space has a shape: contexts bounded, each with a topology the actor can learn; movement between contexts is the switcher's job, movement within each is its model's. A surface now exists to host views — composing views' presupposition — and some of the actor's work is now somewhere they aren't looking, which hands off to attention and tempo ([workspace] → [notification]).

### B. Making the space addressable

*Initiating situation.* States worth returning to exist — a context entered, an item open, a place reached in a space — and the only record of how to get back is in the actor's head. The topology's vocabulary is settled enough to name destinations.

1. *Give every state worth returning to an address.* Three jobs — what identifies the thing, what narrows the view, what points inside it — and the address works in both directions: opening it reconstructs the state. What the actor set once for themselves stays out. Names survive renaming: an identifier resolves, the wording rides along. Rule: [deep-linking].
2. *Decide which changes deserve a Back step.* Journeys append; corrections, defaults, and rapid adjustments overwrite. Going back rebuilds the view from the address. Rule: deep-linking's history dimension.
3. *Expose the link.* The address bar keeps up with the view; an explicit copy-link where the visible address isn't the one worth sending. Rule: deep-linking's sharing surface.

*Resulting context.* Any state worth naming has a name that restores it. Addressable state has left the actor's control — a link travels. And the links are now a contract: named states have to keep resolving, which constrains how freely the space can be reorganised — C inherits that constraint. Hands off: a link can be sampled in place only when its target resolves ([link-preview] — composing views' cross-references sub-sequence).

### C. Growing past the model

*Initiating situation.* The space has outgrown its model: the one surface no longer fits its tools, the global chrome no longer fits its sections, or journeys the topology makes expensive have become routine. Growing is re-entering A2 with the space's history in hand, not a fresh choice.

1. *Step up within the family.* The worn path: a flat surface whose item count outgrows the screen splits into a hub and spokes (the typed `flat-navigation → hub-and-spoke` edge). Other outgrowths re-run the choice point over the region that broke. Rule: [hub-and-spoke].
2. *Combine models rather than force one.* At scale no single model fits; pair per region — a global tree with spatial sections, a hub over flat workspaces. Rule: [hybrid-patterns].
3. *Keep named states resolving.* Every escalation is constrained by the addresses already minted: a reorganised space owes an answer at every address it ever handed out. Rule: deep-linking's contract.

## The read-aloud walk

The test from Book 2 ch. 11 §5: read aloud, the design should form stepwise, no step contradicting what previous steps built.

> The objects, activities, and labels are named; a product is to be laid out. First the work is partitioned into contexts that match how attention and responsibility divide — each boundary carrying access as well as attention, a switcher standing over the contexts. Then each space is given its shape: a continuous space is navigated by viewport; a dependent sequence runs in prescribed order, a flexible one through a hub that states its shape and admits jumps; a single collection dissolves the question into overview and detail; a deep hierarchy files into a tree, one rich surface stays flat, routine cross-journeys connect everything to everything, and independent sections route through a hub. Once places exist, every state worth returning to is given an address that reconstructs it, the journeys that deserve a Back step are chosen, and the link is put where the actor can take it. When the space outgrows its model — the surface its tools, the chrome its sections — the flat surface splits into hub and spokes, models pair per region, and every address the space ever handed out keeps answering.

Verdict: passes. Every step's material exists when the step arrives; nothing built is contradicted later.

## Test walks

Recorded per ch. 11 §7: a sequence fails where a later step forces undoing an earlier step's product. Rationalisations — steps really parallel, or recognised after the fact — are findings, not failures.

*Walk 1 — a team's project tool (multi-context).* A1: partition into project workspaces along ownership; the boundaries carry access, and the switcher rides the step as its hub constituent. A2 per workspace: the tree lands on flat navigation — a board and a small toolset on one surface. B: workspace and board identified, the filter narrowing, the card pointed at; identifier-plus-wording names. C: the toolset outgrew the one surface, and flat → hub held exactly as the typed edge says; the minted addresses kept resolving through their identifier half — C3 is where that obligation became visible. *Backtrack found:* the first draft opened with the choice point, per the map's candidate spine. The tree's questions — deep hierarchy? single workspace? one collection? — had no single answer for the product: each project workspace answered differently, so a model chosen for the whole had to be undone once the partition landed. Moving the partition first cleared it; the tree is asked per space, so the partition has to exist before the question does (finding 1).

*Walk 2 — a collaborative design canvas.* A1: team and project spaces. A2 ran twice inside one workspace: the file browser answered "one collection" and dissolved into overview-detail — the design continued in composing views, as the arm's exit says — while the open file answered "one continuous space" and took pan-and-zoom, its two obligations (the coupled second viewport, nameable position and scale) both arriving on schedule. The two regions pairing up is hybrid-patterns recognised after the fact — a rationalisation, recorded, not a failure. B was central: position and scale made nameable is pan-and-zoom's own resulting clause, and it carries no typed edge into deep-linking (finding 3). *Backtrack found:* the draft took the switcher over team spaces as an A2 choice — hub-and-spoke selected as the product's model. Reaching A2 per space showed the double-count: between-context movement was already the partition step's constituent, and the model choice governs within a space. The switcher moved into A1 (finding 2).

*Walk 3 — a phone podcast app and a docs site (2026-09-25, after the designer-use review, to exercise the genre defaults and the per-platform rule added that day).* The podcast app: A1 is one context, taken and skipped; the access sentence has nothing to bite on in a single-actor consumer product, which is fine. A2 at the top level, sections that differ in kind (Home, Library, Search, Downloads) behind a tab bar. *Backtrack found:* the genre default as first written said a phone app starts from a hub, and the hub page's own to-do calls a tab bar a persistent hub, but a bar whose every section is one move from every other is fully connected by the corpus's own definition; the hub reading only fits an app that opens on a launch screen of independent spokes. The default was corrected in the gloss (fully connected across a tab bar; a hub only behind a launch screen), and the hub page's to-do is now a candidate correction. Inside Library, shows are a collection and dissolved into overview-detail; inside a show, episodes are a collection whose order is derived from date, so the tiebreak sentence routed it correctly. B: the episode identifies, the timestamp points inside, and step 3 fires on every surface because a phone has no address bar at all. The Back-step decision met the platform's per-tab history stacks, where switching tabs is not a step and each tab keeps its own; the gloss assumes one history and should not, recorded as finding 10. C: the tab bar outgrew five sections and the fix was a More tab, a small hub for the overflow. The chrome pain landed on the hub arm, not the tree arm the gloss named, so the tree arm's gloss now admits the phone variant. Two things the walk found no home for: the full-screen player, a surface with no navigation of its own that is not a change corridor, so the modal pointer to making changes does not cover it (finding 11); and the show page's structure, which is not a region in any of the four kinds but the item-view's detail rung, which is composing views' material and confirms the dissolution arm's exit.

The docs site: A1 partitions by product, with the switcher as the hub constituent; the version switcher is a second axis of context the constituent note does not mention, and a version reads as narrowing in the address until it changes the content, when it identifies (finding 12). A2 per platform gave the same kind on desktop and phone (the tree keeps its shape and only the chrome changes, sidebar to drawer), so the per-platform rule cost nothing and confirmed itself. Guides are sections that differ in kind and took the tree; the getting-started took step-by-step because later steps depend on earlier ones; the tutorials took pyramid because their order is authored and their items independent; the API reference is browsed item by item and dissolved into overview-detail. The tiebreak and the four kinds held with no forced call. B: pages identified, headings pointing inside, version narrowing or identifying as above; the in-page table of contents is a view-produced move and step 2's caveat correctly left it open. C: guide-to-reference trips are routine from the first day, so the sideways-paths arm was invoked at layout rather than at growth. *Backtrack found:* not a revoked decision but a rate finding — for genres whose lateral traffic is known in advance, the growth choice's arms are taken at layout, and the recognition gloss should say the growth arms are available then (finding 13). The version churn exercised C3 exactly: every old address redirects or answers, which is the contract as written.

Verdict: the spine held on both products. Two glosses were corrected in the sitting (the phone default, the tree arm's phone variant), the sideways-paths arm became the weak form because no pattern owns the authored cross-link, and four findings were recorded.

*On the transfer question:* even coarser than composing views. The choice point carries most of the guidance, and order does real work at three joints only — the partition before the choice, the vocabulary before the addresses, the addresses constraining growth. A sequence whose middle is a choice point guides by gating and exiting more than by ordering; worth carrying to the encounter and delegating work, both of which contain protocol choice points.

## Findings — corpus changes the authoring forces

Recorded for their own sittings; none executed here.

1. *The trunk's head is workspace, not the navigation model.* The map's candidate spine read `navigation model → workspace → deep-linking`. Walking it, the model choice taken first has nothing to govern: the tree's questions are asked per space, and the partition decides what each topology's space is. The graph agrees quietly — the information-architecture foundation's `precedes` edge lands on workspace, and no `precedes` edge orders the models against workspace in either direction. No edge changes; the map's sequence entry is corrected.
2. *Between-context movement belongs to the partition step.* The switcher is hub-and-spoke riding workspace as a constituent (the existing `enables` edge), not an invocation of the choice point; walking it as a model choice double-counts. Same evidence class as the pilot's finding 2: step-versus-constituent status is exactly what the sequence layer stores and the graph cannot.
3. *Candidate edge: pan-and-zoom's addressability clause sets up deep-linking.* "A continuous space has no addresses of its own; position and scale have to be made nameable" is B's initiating situation in prose, but the pair carries only `complements`. Candidate: `sets-up: [deep-linking]` on that resulting clause.
4. *Candidate edge: fully-connected's chrome-budget clause sets up hybrid-patterns.* The escalation currently lives in a `complements` note ("can be combined with a multilevel tree as content grows") — condition text in an edge note, which the vocabulary bans. Candidate: a resulting clause with `sets-up: [hybrid-patterns]`, and the note reduced to the mechanism.
5. *Classification: the overview-detail arm is a dissolution, not a topology.* Choosing it exits into composing views' splitting sub-sequence; navigation-overview's surveys note already says this in prose ("the collection regime's handoff"). No edge change — recorded in the map's interconnection inventory.
6. *Classification: hub-and-spoke → purpose-keyed-view is a handoff into making sense at scale.* The hub-as-reporting-surface clause gives the map's thinnest territory a concrete entry point (reporting surfaces join the curation chain); feeds that sequence's spine hunt.
7. *Shared-station candidate: deep-linking.* This sequence invokes it at four steps across two sub-sequences, and composing views' framings, saved views, and cross-references all want addresses without yet calling it. If a third sequence reaches for it (the encounter's closing — what survives the session as a link — is a plausible site), it joins the map's shared-stations list.
8. *The choice point is a recognition, and the navigation tree dissolved into it.* Executed 2026-09-24, unlike the findings above. Both test walks already showed the shape: each region "answered" its kind at once (flat, one collection, one continuous space) and nobody walked the tree's seven questions. In practice the kind of a space is given by its material and genre, the deliberation among topologies happens later and locally when a region hurts, and hybrids are the normal case rather than the leaf reached when nothing fits. *The Nature of Order* agrees: Book 3 ch. 11 §8 calls a one-shot choice among a half dozen alternatives "inevitably a phony choice", and every published sequence in Books 2–3 carries variation as conditional clauses inside a step keyed to what earlier steps built (Pasadena step 3, Santa Rosa 24b–d, the tea house's "if there is a family dwelling") — never as a chooser. So A2 became "Recognise what kind of space each context is", taken per region, with the four kinds as cues and the arms carrying their conditions; the tree's depth, surface, and cross-traffic questions moved into C's initiating situation and C1's gloss as the pains that re-run the recognition; and the seven model pages took their `situation.initiating` back, written as the path the tree used to route. Navigation is a field guide. What the change did not touch: the `tree` field and the "decision tree behind" appearance kind stay in the schema for the trees that remain; whether deletion's tree goes the same way is its own sitting; the read-aloud walk above still passes with "given its shape" read for "each space is given its shape".

9. *Designer-use review, executed 2026-09-25.* The sequence was read as an experienced designer trying to use it, and the straightforward fixes were applied to the file. The presupposition now covers names only; the hierarchy is decided inside the sequence and the tree arm says it is a guess to check against the actor. The partition step names access as the line drawn first. The recognition choice is marked exclusive (one arm per region; the render said "combine any"), and its gloss gained the region definition, the sequence-versus-collection tiebreak (authored order versus derived order), the per-platform rule, the genre defaults, the depth-as-cost note on the tree arm, and a pointer sending the modal corridor to making changes' routing choice. Two obligations the ordered arms create are now recorded and collected: the resumable step at addressing/1 and progress at memory and continuity's restored place (a new hands-off, via step-by-step). Addressing/1 carries the address's three jobs again; addressing/2 says the view-produced moves it does not decide. The growth head became a choice keyed by pain, because the single step's rule was hub-and-spoke while three of its four paths landed on other patterns; the fourth arm invokes command-menu with searching riding, a judgment call. The growth situation names the evidence classes a pain is read from, and growth/2 is reframed as re-partitioning. Candidates not executed, each its own sitting: a wayfinding step (no pattern owns orientation; breadcrumbs and section markers are components only), a landing-surface decision for the tree and fully-connected arms, a step or call placing an embedded conversational surface in the topology, a third test walk through a phone app and a docs site, and the folded "Applies when" render, opened by default the next day (the spec records it). Placing an embedded conversational surface in the topology was considered and left out: practice has placements (copilot pane, inline assistant, command bar) but no settled pattern, and it belongs to conversation or the encounter rather than to a step here. The sequence-versus-collection tiebreak and the genre defaults now live on navigation-overview's page; the recognition gloss keeps the per-region and per-platform conditions and links to the defaults.

10. *The Back-step gloss assumes one history.* Phone platforms keep a history stack per tab and treat a tab switch as no step at all. Candidate: a sentence on deep-linking's history section, and the addressing/2 gloss saying the decision is taken per history the platform keeps.
11. *A place with no navigation that is not a change corridor.* The full-screen player, the lightbox, the reader mode: surfaces the actor enters, does one thing in, and leaves by closing. Tidwell's modal panel covers both this and the form corridor; the corpus routes only the corridor (making changes' wizard arm). Candidate: a pattern or a clause on the recognition step that names the modal place as a fifth kind with no topology and one exit.
12. *Context has more than one axis.* Product and version on a docs site, organisation and project in most team tools. The partition step's constituent note names one switcher. Candidate: the workspace page's variants section already nests scopes; the step gloss should say the switcher is taken per axis.
13. *Growth arms taken at layout.* Where a genre's lateral traffic is known in advance (docs, reference material), the sideways-paths arm is invoked at layout, not after a pain. Candidate: a sentence on the recognition gloss saying the growth choice's arms are available from the start for regions whose pain is foreseeable, which is the same move as hybrid patterns applied from the start.
14. *Candidate patterns from the review and walk 3.* Signposting (wayfinding: show the actor where they are, whatever the topology; breadcrumbs and section markers are its components and nothing names the move), and contextual links (the authored cross-link that cuts across a hierarchy, Rosenfeld and Morville's contextual navigation; the IA foundation describes it and dynamic-hyperlinks covers the inferred kind). Both are pattern-classifier sittings. The hub page's to-do calling a tab bar a persistent hub is a candidate correction to fully connected.


## Revision to the step-length rules (2026-09-27)

The sequence was revised under the step-length rules in docs/specs/sequences.md §Authoring. The file went from about 1,990 words to about 1,470. No step, rule, order, or connection changed, and sub-sequence ids are unchanged, so the walks and every incoming `at` address still stand.

The recognition choice's gloss went from about 290 words to about 115. It held two kinds of content. The conditions on how the step is taken stay in the gloss: read the kind, one arm per region, again per platform, sections start from the genre's default, and the corridor goes to making changes. The field-guide content moved to navigation-overview's "Kinds of space" section, where each judgement now has one home: the order tiebreak (authored order makes a sequence, derived order a collection) and the list of genre defaults. The gloss links to that page for the defaults.

Where the rest of the removed text went:

- The lead listed the three sub-sequences. It now says the sequence is usually walked first, gives composing views its surface, and is walked again per region as the space grows.
- The layout and addressing `resulting` clauses were removed. What they carried is in the connections (the surface for composing views, the work out of sight), on deep-linking's page (the link that travels, the contract), or moved into a step: the address contract is now created in addressing/1's gloss and collected at growth/3.
- The pan-and-zoom arm's first obligation (a wider viewport kept in step) is carried by its connection to composing views, whose note now says so.
- Pattern content already on the pages: the hierarchy as a guess (multilevel-tree) and access carried by the boundaries (workspace).
- The remark that the sideways-paths arm is the weak form is marked by its missing `rule`. The remark that addressing/2 leaves view-produced moves open until composing views invokes the rule was authoring status.
- The growth `initiating` no longer lists the four pains; the four arms state them.

Step-list test: the step lines, with both choices and their arms, read as a walk without their glosses.
