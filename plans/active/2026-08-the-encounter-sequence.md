# The encounter — the fourth sequence

Phase 5 of [the sequence-map plan](2026-08-sequence-map.md): the third sequence authored after the pilot. The sequence's authoritative home is `apps/patterns/src/content/sequences/the-encounter.mdx`; this document is the authoring trace — the sequence as first written, the read-aloud walk, the test walks with their backtracks, and the corpus changes the authoring surfaced. The corpus changes are recorded here, not executed — each is judgment work for its own sitting.

The map called this territory "already sequence-shaped", and the sweep record says why: the conversation family's `precedes` edges survived the register sweep under the protocol-family coincidence — a protocol's design object is itself a sequence, so the design order and the run order largely coincide. The authoring still corrected the map's spine in five places; the coincidence made the steps easy to find, not the joints.

Steps are decisions, written as verbs, each invoking a pattern as its rule. Constituents ride inside steps in parentheses.

## Presupposition (the head)

The collaboration posture has settled on conversation: dialogue is this surface's primary mode, not one affordance among many — a tree-shaped decision (embedded, conversational, or delegated) that no page yet owns; it is the map's standing open question. And the agent's capabilities are inventoried, so there is a boundary to state: what it can act on, what it can only retrieve, what it can only discuss. Nothing in the corpus records the inventory work; it is process-register and discharged upstream.

The sequence enacts one quality per region, near enough: *agency* at the footing and the gate (who may open, and the actor judging the intention rather than the fait accompli), *conversation-quality* at the business (dialogue as the primary mode, meaning built turn by turn), *learnability* at the boundary and the scaffold (the encounter teaching what it can do and what a good request contains).

## The sequence

### A. Setting the footing — the trunk

1. *Draw the capability boundary.* A conversational surface looks identical whether it can act, only retrieve, or only talk, so the boundary is stated rather than discovered by walking into it: what may be promised, and what an out-of-scope request meets — a refusal with somewhere to go, never a dead end. Held per encounter, not made once per product (walk 2's backtrack; finding 4). Rule: [capability-and-scope].
2. *Decide who can open.* A choice point, non-exclusive — most products take both arms:
   - *Let the agent open* — footing first, the capability statement embedded, a first topic proposed. When the agent may open at all is an interruption judgement this sequence does not own (finding 8). Rule: [agent-opening].
   - *Recognise the actor's open* — the agent aligns to whatever was brought; its shape routes the activity, and the too-loose opening reaches the embedded ask before anything else. Rule: [user-opening].

*Resulting context.* Footing set, a topic aligned, every activity below consumes it. The actor has committed to nothing, so a way out before any activity starts is owed — an obligation the trouble pass collects.

### B. Carrying the business

*Initiating situation.* A topic is aligned, and the actor's business arrives in a few shapes — a question answerable as asked, a goal with steps behind it, content that will not fit one turn. Each shape gets its exchange designed.

1. *Answer the askable directly.* The minimal pair, present in every dialogue form: answer first, next moves after, turns short. Evidence rides the non-trivial answer, or the actor is taking the agent's word for it — the per-surface call into calibrating reliance. Rule: [inquiry-user].
2. *Frame the goal-shaped request.* Goal, constraints, assumptions and sources made explicit, so what is missing shows up as named gaps. Rule: [open-request]. (Constituents: inquiry-agent — the embedded ask, only for what the agent genuinely lacks; prompt-scaffolding — structure for composing a complete request without knowing what completeness looks like.)
3. *Hold the floor for the long telling.* Scope announced, advanced on continuers, skip, back and stop throughout; also the repairs' escalation target. Optional — answers that fit single turns skip it. Rule: [extended-telling].
4. *Give turns a working surface.* Where words are the wrong vehicle the message becomes a thing to act on, and the transcript a record of actions as well as words; the gate's confirm card and the repairs' one-tap corrections consume this later. Optional — an encounter that stays in words skips it. Rule: [inline-interface]. Promoted from the map's constituent listing (finding 1).

### C. Standing between the reading and the act

*Initiating situation.* Some requests end in the agent acting on its reading — real work spent, or effects not cheaply taken back — and the reading can be wrong. The gate is decided per act, graded by consequences; the routine act keeps a clear path.

1. *Match the gate to the act's grade* — two gates, unordered between themselves *(parallel)*; the act that reverses cleanly takes neither and leans on the reverse path:
   - *preview the substantial work* — intention as a reviewable object: steps, targets, effects, cost; over-asked, the actor stops reading plans. Rule: [plan-preview].
   - *take the go-ahead in place* — the single irreversible step gets a second commitment where the intent was expressed: one decision of friction, not a change of place. Rule: [inline-confirmation].

### D. Designing for trouble

*Initiating situation.* Any activity can break in two directions — the agent misreads, or the actor does not follow — and sometimes the actor simply wants out with the goal unmet. An encounter that only works when every turn lands is not designed yet.

1. *Repair from both sides* — unordered between themselves *(parallel)*; both resume at the step that broke:
   - *surface the agent's reading for correction* — interpreted intent and parameters editable; one missing value narrows to the embedded ask. Rule: [agent-repair].
   - *re-deliver what did not land* — paraphrase, definition, worked example; when none suffice, the agent takes the floor properly. Rule: [user-repair].
2. *Build the exit that is not failure.* Not repair, not closing: the sequence halts where it stood, what changed is stated, loose ends stay visible. Changes already applied leave the conversational frame for their reverse path (finding 6). Rule: [abort].

### E. Ending and parking

*Initiating situation.* Delivery the agent can see is not the same as landing — only the actor's receipt tells the difference. And the encounter is bigger than any one activity: endable, and pausable without being ended, with the difference visible.

1. *Close the loop on each activity.* The third-position receipt; an acknowledged completion is the natural place to offer a next move and the easiest place to over-prompt. Rule: [sequence-completion].
2. *Settle the two endings* — a cluster whose primacy is a posture fact (walk 1's backtrack; finding 3):
   - *end the encounter deliberately* — last topics invited, handled or scheduled; anything deferred is a commitment kept outside this exchange. Rule: [closing].
   - *let it park without ending* — saved state whose persistence the actor can see; re-entry lands on the openings with history, and resuming confirms the aged assumptions, the capability boundary included. Rule: [disengage-without-closing].

## The read-aloud walk

The test from Book 2 ch. 11 §5: read aloud, the design should form stepwise, no step contradicting what previous steps built.

> The posture has settled on conversation and the capabilities are inventoried. First the boundary is drawn — what this exchange can do and cannot, stated rather than discovered, held per encounter rather than promised once. Then the doorways: the agent's opening, footing and capability first and a topic proposed; the actor's opening, recognised and aligned to. With a topic aligned, each shape of business gets its exchange: the askable answered directly with its evidence riding along; the goal-shaped request framed until what is missing has names, the embedded ask fetching what only the actor knows and the scaffold shaping composition; content too big for a turn walked with the floor held on continuers; and the turns that outgrow words given working surfaces. Where a request ends in an act, a gate is matched to its grade — substantial work previewed, the irreversible step confirmed in place, the reversible act waved through. Then trouble is designed for: the agent's reading surfaced for correction, what did not land re-delivered, and the exit that is not failure built. Last the endings: the receipt that closes each activity, the deliberate close that invites last topics, and the parking that saves the exchange with its assumptions to be confirmed on return.

Verdict: passes. One deliberate debt inside the walk: the footing owes an exit before any activity exists, and the trouble pass pays it — an obligation carried forward, not a contradiction.

## Test walks

Recorded per ch. 11 §7: a sequence fails where a later step forces undoing an earlier step's product.

*Walk 1 — the issue tracker's assistant (continuing the pilot's walk 1; the embedded entry — arriving via making changes' conversational hand-off at its routing choice point).* A1: create and change issues, query the tracker, draft release notes; cannot touch members, permissions, or project deletion — the out-of-scope answer routes to the settings surface. A2: both arms — the actor opens with requests; the agent opens with the overnight triage proposal, and the walk immediately asks by what right it interrupts (finding 8). B: "how many open bugs in checkout?" answered with the count and a link into the framed view that produced it; "clean up the backlog" framed, with staleness criteria and target project fetched by the embedded ask; the release-process walkthrough takes extended telling thinly; label chips and confirm cards as working surfaces. C: the bulk clean-up previewed — 40 closures, 12 relabels — and approved; closing a single issue reverses cleanly, so it takes no gate and leans on undo. D: "by *old* I took 90 days — edit"; "*stale* means no activity, not no assignee" re-delivered; abort mid-clean-up halts with 12 of 40 done and the 12 stated — their reverse path is the safety pass's. *Backtrack found:* the first draft marked parking optional and the close unmarked — the conversation-first shape. The tracker's assistant is the inversion: nobody closes it (they click back into the board mid-answer), the deliberate close is the rare case, and parked state is the norm. Marking either step optional encodes one posture; the endings were rewritten as an unordered cluster whose gloss hands the primacy question to the posture (finding 3).

*Walk 2 — a conversation-first research assistant (the map's second entry mode: the conversation is the workspace).* A1: search and read connected sources, summarise, draft documents; the capability statement at first run is the actor's onboarding moment (the learning connection). A2: the actor opens with goals; the agent opens on return with parked work to resume. B: direct answers carry citations — the calibrating-reliance call, taken at nearly every turn; "survey the literature on X" framed and handed to delegation as a prompt; the findings walked with the floor held; source cards and scope chips as working surfaces. C: the survey previewed with sources and time cost, then approved; an abort during the live run drains in from delegating work. D and E as authored. *Backtrack found:* the first draft's boundary step produced a statement made once, and the footing's resulting context claimed the boundary settled. Parking aged it: the assistant gains a source between visits, and re-entry on the openings walked into a stale promise — disengage-without-closing's own page already requires resuming to confirm assumptions and propose an updated scope. A later step was revising an earlier step's product, so the earlier step was rewritten: the boundary is held per encounter and restated where it changed (finding 4).

*On the transfer question:* the protocol-family coincidence held — design order and run order coincide here more than in any spatial sequence, which is why the map could call the territory already sequence-shaped. But both backtracks were posture joints (embedded versus conversation-first), not step-order errors: the guidance again concentrated where sequences meet products, and this sitting's joints were between postures rather than between sub-sequences.

## Findings — corpus changes the authoring forces

Recorded for their own sittings; none executed here.

1. *Inline interface promoted from constituent to step.* The map listed it as a constituent, but its product is consumed by two later regions — the gate's confirm card, the repairs' one-tap corrections — which is the same spent-later shape that promoted item-view in the pilot. Authored as the business sub-sequence's closing step, optional for encounters that stay in words.
2. *Extended telling was missing from the map's spine entirely.* It joined as a step (the floor-holding decision) and is the repairs' escalation target — user-repair's resulting clause is its typed form. The map entry is corrected.
3. *The endings are posture-inverted.* A conversation-first product closes deliberately and parks as the exception; an embedded surface is mostly walked away from and closes rarely. Neither step can carry `optional` without encoding one posture, so the two are an unordered cluster whose gloss hands primacy to the posture. Candidate for a sitting: the `closing ↔ disengage-without-closing` alternative edge carries no note about posture deciding the primary — one side's note says "preferred alternative", which is the conversation-first reading stated as if universal.
4. *The capability boundary is held, not stated once.* Walk 2's parking aged the statement; disengage-without-closing's page already demands scope re-confirmation on resume, but capability-and-scope's resulting clauses only bind the statement within one exchange. Candidate: a resulting clause on capability-and-scope about the promise ageing across encounters — restated where capability changed.
5. *Shared station confirmed: plan-preview.* This sequence's gate and governing autonomy's spine (bounded-autonomy → plan-preview → approval-gate) both step on it, and the `open-request precedes plan-preview` edge is the encounter-side approach. Second station confirmed from an authored walk, after making changes' undo; the map's stations list gains it.
6. *Abort's undo relation undersells a drain.* The bare `related` edge between abort and undo hides that an aborted sequence with changes already applied needs the safety pass — authored here as a hands-off into making changes at the reverse-path step. Candidate for a sitting: a resulting clause on abort ("changes already applied need their reverse path") in place of the untyped edge, if it passes the vocabulary's make-time test.
7. *The dialogue-form routing has no owner.* conversation.mdx's "Dialogue form selection" section is chooser prose on a pattern page — which form when, by task, goals, actor state, relationship stage — the same ownerless-judgement shape as making changes' routing finding and the map's posture question. The page also carries TODO stubs (conversation stages, interaction types) that this sequence now covers. Candidate: a cleanup sitting for conversation.mdx that decides what the page keeps once the sequence is the home of stage knowledge.
8. *An unprompted agent opening is an interruption.* When the agent may open — by what right, at what priority — is exactly the judgement attention and tempo presupposes, sighted here from the opening arm. An entry point for that sequence's spine hunt, alongside the notification hand-offs the earlier sittings recorded.
9. *capability-and-scope → onboarding classified at the sequence layer.* The map's missing link is now an authored hands-off into learning via onboarding (the first capability statement is an onboarding moment). The pattern-layer edge between the two pages remains a candidate for its own sitting.


## Revision to the step-length rules (2026-09-27)

The sequence was revised under the step-length rules in docs/specs/sequences.md §Authoring, in the plain wording of the earlier revisions. The file went from about 1,785 words to about 1,330. One sub-sequence title changed ("Checking before the act"), as did two step titles ("Match the check to the act", "Show the agent's reading for correction"); no step, rule, order, or connection changed, and sub-sequence ids are unchanged, so the walks and every incoming `at` address still stand.

Where the removed text went:

- The lead listed the regions. It now states the situation and keeps the fact that in a conversation-first product the conversation is also the workspace.
- The presupposition's remark that the posture decision is tree-shaped and owned by no page is finding 7.
- Every reason and cost in the glosses was already on its pattern page: the identical-looking surface and the stale boundary (capability-and-scope), the answer carrying no working (inquiry-user), named gaps (open-request), review fatigue (plan-preview), confirming not being reversing (inline-confirmation), the loose end (abort), over-prompting at completion (sequence-completion), the deferred commitment (closing), and parked state ageing (disengage-without-closing).
- The footing `resulting` created the obligation of a way out before any activity starts. It now sits in the opening choice's gloss and is collected in the abort step's gloss. The business and trouble `resulting` clauses restated the next `initiating` or that collection and were removed.

Nothing was lost that is not on a pattern page or in the findings.

Step-list test: the step lines, with the choice, the three clusters, and their members, read as a walk without their glosses.
