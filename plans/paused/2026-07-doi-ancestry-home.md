# Shared theoretical ancestry: a home for the degree-of-interest formalism

Paused on an explicit trigger — there is no work to do until it fires.

## The datum

Semantic zoom and focus-and-context both descend from Furnas's degree-of-interest (DOI) formalism, which sits *below* both patterns. The typed-edges research takeaway was explicit: don't force a graph edge through a construct the language doesn't have. So the ancestry currently lives as parallel prose on both pages, under an `alternative` edge between the two descendants. That is the settled state, not a defect.

## The trigger

A third DOI descendant lands in the corpus — candidate shapes: fisheye distortion, or overview+detail treated as a move in its own right. Two descendants are handled fine by parallel prose; at three, the repetition starts to argue for a named ancestor.

## The decision, when triggered

Whether the ancestor wants a named home, and of what kind:

- *Foundation node* — only if DOI functions as design material/substrate whose application produces structure later moves act on. Measure against Bridging Gulfs, per the standing foundation-vs-theory rule.
- *References + woven vocabulary* — the default for explanatory design theory: a `references/` entry with the formalism's vocabulary woven into the descendant pages' prose. The current lean, since DOI reads more as an explanatory formalism than a substrate — but the trigger sitting decides with the third descendant in hand.
- *No named home* — extend the parallel-prose treatment to three pages, if the repetition turns out cheap.

## Notes for iteration

This is an outline, not an executable spec. When the trigger fires, iterate: name the third descendant's actual relation to the two existing pages (sibling `alternative`? `instantiates` against a new foundation?), check whether the situation constructs or a later vocabulary state change the calculus, and record the decision through the vocabulary doc's changelog if any graph structure results.
