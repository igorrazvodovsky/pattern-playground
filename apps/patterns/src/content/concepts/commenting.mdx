---
title: Commenting
added: 2026-10-18
purpose: Let people attach remarks to a passage of shared material and settle them.
principle: >-
  If a member marks a passage and writes a remark, others see the remark beside
  the passage and can reply; once the matter is settled, whoever resolves the
  thread takes it out of view without deleting it.
state:
  - "author: Comment -> one User"
  - "about: Comment -> one Item"
  - "anchor: Comment -> opt Range"
  - "thread: Comment -> one Thread"
  - "replyTo: Comment -> opt Comment"
  - "resolvedBy: Comment -> opt User"
actions: [addComment, replyToComment, resolveThread]
misfits:
  - A thread whose passage is deleted keeps its remarks and loses its place.
  - Resolving hides a thread from the people who have not read it yet, and what was settled there is invisible to anyone reading only the material.
  - A passage that starts being referred to, not only discussed, is a weak object; a highlight with a thread has no identity of its own.
aliases: [discussion, annotation, review comment, thread]
appears_in:
  - Google Docs
  - Figma
  - GitHub pull-request review
  - Notion
---

Commenting is the concept behind every "leave a note here" gesture. The remark
belongs to a place in the material rather than to the whole of it, it is
visible to the other people working on that material, and it has a lifecycle
of its own: open, answered, settled. The last point is what separates
commenting from chat beside a document. A chat message is never resolved; a
comment is.

Three variants matter to design, and each is a different setting of the state
above:

- *Anchored or whole.* `anchor` is optional. A comment with a range sits
  beside the passage it names; a comment without one is about the item as a
  whole. The two read differently and resolve differently, since a
  whole-item comment has no passage to lose.
- *Resolved or deleted.* Resolving records who settled the thread and takes it
  out of view; the remarks survive. A system that deletes instead has a
  different concept, a note that can be torn off, and loses the record of what
  was agreed.
- *Flat or threaded.* `replyTo` is what makes a thread a conversation rather
  than a list. Without it every comment is a top-level remark and the
  thread is just a grouping.

In the fixture world a comment also carries a type and a priority
(`commentType`, `commentPriority`), which is a small merge: classifying a
remark rides on the act of making it. The world's one rule over this concept
is a synchronisation with [Notifying](/concepts/notifying): whoever wrote a
document hears about a new comment on it, and replies notify nobody, since the
thread is already in view.
