Conversation for action is the structure under every request made of
another party: ask, agree, do, report, accept. Its state is the conversation,
not the work. What the performer produces lives elsewhere, in a document or a
task; what this concept holds is who asked whom for what, what would count as
done, and how far the two have got towards settling it. The requester and the
performer are both of kind `Party`, which is supplied where the concept is
used, so either side can be a person, a team, a role or a system. The loop
has exits before satisfaction: the performer can decline or counter, the
requester can withdraw, and the requester can declare the reported work
unsatisfactory, which sends it back to the performer.

Three variants matter to design:

- *Explicit or implied promise.* In the full form the performer promises, and
  until then the request is only a request. Most systems collapse this: being
  assigned is taken as having agreed. The third misfit is the cost, and the
  design question is whether to give the performer a way to say no, or to
  offer a different time, before the work is counted as theirs.
- *Who closes.* In the full form the performer reports and the requester
  declares. Many systems let the performer mark the work done and treat that
  as the end, which hands the declaration to the wrong side; the reopen
  button is where the requester's declaration reappears. [Task
  tracking](/concepts/task-tracking) and [Staging](/concepts/staging) run this
  abbreviated loop in the fixture world: `completeTask` is the performer's
  report, and the notification it fires to whoever created the task is the
  only trace of the declaration arm.
- *Negotiation present or absent.* Counter and decline make the opening a
  negotiation rather than an order. Where one side is a system acting on
  instruction, the arm is usually missing, and whether to give an agent a way
  to counter (a different scope, a question before promising) is a design
  decision rather than a given.

Breakdown is the concept's native failure: a conversation that cannot reach
satisfaction, because the condition turned out to be impossible, the parties
read it differently, or one of them left. Nothing here repairs it; a repair is
a further conversation about the first, and the systems that handle it well
make the broken one easy to find and refer to.
