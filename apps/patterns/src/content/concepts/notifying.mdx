---
title: Notifying
added: 2026-10-18
purpose: Tell an actor about something that happened while they were not looking.
principle: >-
  If something happens that concerns a member who did not cause it, the system
  records a notice for them; when they next attend, they find the notice and
  can follow it to the item it is about.
state:
  - "notified: User -> set Item"
actions: [notify]
misfits:
  - A notice that arrives in the middle of other work costs attention whether or not it was worth it; the concept says nothing about when it is delivered.
  - Notices pile up when nothing clears them, and a long backlog is read as noise rather than as a record.
  - The same event can reach an actor through several channels at once, each with its own notice.
  - A notice about the system itself (planned downtime, an outage) has no item to follow to, and the concept as held here has nowhere to put it.
aliases: [alert, notification, ping]
appears_in:
  - Slack
  - GitHub
  - Linear
  - iOS and Android system notifications
---

Notifying is the system's own act, not the actor's. Nothing in the concept says
who is told or why: that is composition, and a system's rules decide it. In the
fixture world two rules fire it, one from [Commenting](/concepts/commenting)
(the document's author hears about a new comment) and one from
[Staging](/concepts/staging) (whoever asked for a task hears when someone else
finishes it). The rule's `where` clause is what keeps a member from being told
about their own acts.

The concept holds only the fact that a member was notified about an item. How
the notice is delivered, how urgently, and on which surface are not its
business, which is why the pattern pages that act on it are mostly about
delivery and attention rather than about the notice itself.

Most shipping systems add a second fact, whether the notice has been seen, and
an action that clears it. The world holds neither, so the entry lists neither;
the piling-up misfit above is what their absence costs, and the pattern page
that needs them is where they get added.
