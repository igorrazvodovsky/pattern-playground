**When the actions for a selected item sit in a distant toolbar or a searchable command list, the actor has to move focus away from the target, scan past controls that do not apply, or recall a command by name. In nested content—such as a word inside a paragraph inside a section—an action can also modify a larger container than the selection without making that scope clear.**

Contextual actions appear through several mechanisms: a floating toolbar on text selection, a popover menu on right-click or long press, hover actions on a list row or card, a menu attached to a block handle, or an action bar that appears when list items are selected. Each mechanism trades discoverability against visual interference. A floating toolbar that appears on every text selection keeps actions close at hand, but covers adjacent lines and interrupts actors who highlight text to read or copy it. A right-click or long-press menu avoids visual clutter until requested, but hides available actions behind an unlabelled gesture. Hover toolbars reveal actions without persistent chrome, though they have no direct equivalent on touch screens.

Scope ambiguity is the primary failure mode when content is nested. A text selection sits inside a block, and some operations apply to the inline span (bold, link, rewrite sentence) while others apply to the containing block (convert to heading, move, comment on block). Mixing both levels in a single menu without distinguishing their reach causes actions to modify more than the actor selected. Multi-block selections compound the ambiguity when the same operation, such as an AI revision, can run either on the highlighted span or on the surrounding blocks. When scope is explicit, selecting a target first and invoking a reusable prompt action from a toolbar is faster and requires shorter instructions than describing the target in chat ([Masson et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03691)).

Therefore,

**Surface the actions that apply to the selected or targeted item directly beside it, and omit actions that do not apply. Let the selection define the scope of every action. When an action operates on a larger container than the selection—such as the whole block or row—preview that container boundary before execution and name the target in the action's label. Keep the contextual surface limited to high-frequency actions, leaving the full command set to a searchable [command menu](/patterns/command-menu).**

## Resources & references

- [Shape of AI — Inline action](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/inline-action) — preset actions on selected content in AI tools; letting the actor set the scope (word, sentence, section, block) before invoking; presenting results as a suggestion rather than an immediate overwrite
- Scott & Neil (2009) [Designing web interfaces](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-web-interfaces/9780596155353/) — contextual tools: always visible, revealed on hover, revealed on toggle, multi-level, and secondary menus
- Tidwell, Brewer & Valencia (2020) [Designing interfaces, 3rd edition](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-interfaces-3rd/9781492051954/) — hover and pop-up tools
- Masson, Malacria, Casiez & Vogel (2024) [DirectGPT: a direct manipulation interface to interact with large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03691) — selecting an object and applying a reusable prompt tool; faster editing with fewer and shorter prompts than chat
