* * *

**When contributions from different people, automated rules, and models sit in the same document or record and look alike once written, an actor meeting a passage or a change cannot tell whether they are looking at a colleague's deliberate choice, a rule firing, or a model's draft. Without knowing what produced it, they do not know whom to ask about it, whether they are free to overwrite it, or how to check it.**

Where a surface already shows who is speaking, such as the system's turn in a dedicated chat thread, an extra badge adds noise without adding information. Provenance marking earns its place when contributions from people, rules, and models share one surface, or when output leaves the surface that made it. Copy, paste, export, and screenshots strip surrounding interface chrome first, so a mark that lives only in the frame disappears at the boundary where the next reader needs it most.

Marking origin also changes what the unmarked means. The moment any part of a document or feed is labelled as machine-made, everything left unlabelled is read as a claim of human origin. Nor can origin be reconstructed after the fact: once generated text is edited by hand or merged into a shared draft, what came from the model and what came from a person is lost unless it was stamped when written.

Across tools, the mark takes four recurring forms: disclosure labels on AI turns and features, visual conventions (colour, iconography) reserved for machine output, per-span or per-event authorship records in shared documents, and watermarks or embedded file metadata readable by people or software after export.

Therefore,

**Attach a mark of what produced the content—a person, a named rule, or a model—at the moment the contribution is written. Keep the mark quiet where the surface already shows who is speaking, and bind it to the content itself wherever the output mixes with other people's work or can be copied and exported out of the tool.**

Do not rely on interface chrome alone when content will be pasted, exported, or shared as a file, since the chrome is the first layer stripped in transit. Because marking some spans turns every unmarked span into an implied claim of human authorship, state what the absence of a mark means whenever older or imported content has no provenance recorded.
