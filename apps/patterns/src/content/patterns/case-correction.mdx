* * *

**When a standing rule, filter, recommender, or agent gets one case wrong, the actor meets the mistake on that case and wants it fixed there, with or without changing what happens next time. If the only remedy is to leave the work and edit the rule in settings, most people put up with the error or turn the automation off; if every one-off fix silently rewrites the rule, a temporary exception corrupts future behaviour.**

Skipping a song and hiding it for good are different requests, and music services offer both on the track. A spellchecker's "Ignore once" and "Add to dictionary", a mail client's "Not spam" and "Filter messages like these", a browser blocker's "Allow this time" and "Always allow on this site", and a feed's "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel" all separate the immediate override from the standing change, and put both on the item itself. That separation is what keeps [delegation](/patterns/delegation) workable once a rule or model starts making mistakes in use.

Therefore,

**Put the repair on the case itself, and separate fixing this case now from changing what happens next time. Offer, in rising order of commitment: undo or skip this once; make a standing exception for this item or attribute in either direction; or change the rule or preference that produced it, starting from an explanation of why this case matched. Accept the smaller corrections in one gesture without demanding a reason, while letting the actor narrow why if they choose.**

Keep standing exceptions in a list the actor can review and clear, so overrides accumulated one by one do not silently cancel the rule they qualify.

A wrong refusal is harder to meet than a wrong act, because the message filtered out or the payment declined is out of sight. Where the rule refuses or filters out, keep what it refused where the actor can open it and reverse it, as a spam folder does, with the reason on each entry and the least certain entries first. Few people open such a list unasked, so now and then ask about a close call, as a bank asks whether a declined payment was the actor's own. Where a held item may be harmful, show enough to recognise it, such as the sender and subject, and not the item itself.

## Research on this pattern

- Separating a one-off override from a standing change: when a learning thermostat treated every manual adjustment as a change to the standing schedule, [temporary exceptions corrupted future behaviour and people hesitated to adjust it at all](https://doi.org/10.1145/2493432.2493489) — the basis for keeping "this once" distinct from an exception or rule change.
- Correcting the reasons on the case rather than only relabelling the item: when a classifier showed why it predicted a label on a single item and let the actor adjust those reasons, [actors formed a more accurate picture of the system's logic and fixed mistakes with far fewer corrections than through item-level feedback alone](https://doi.org/10.1145/2678025.2701399) — the basis for starting a rule or preference change from the case's explanation.
- Reliance after seeing a mistake: people who saw a forecasting algorithm err abandoned it when they had to take or leave its output whole, and [kept using it when they could modify what it did, even slightly](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2643).
