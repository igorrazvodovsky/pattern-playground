* * *

**When access is set action by action for each person, nobody can read who plays which part, and every new member is a checklist. Named roles fix that, but no small, fixed set fits how every organisation divides its work. When no role fits, whoever manages the space gives a wider one for convenience. When the product lets them build roles from scratch instead, each exception gets a role of its own, and the list soon becomes as hard to read as access set person by person.**

Therefore,

**Offer a small set of stock roles named after the parts people play in the work. Let an organisation add a role only by copying a stock role and changing it, so every role keeps a named role it was made from. Describe each role in plain sentences of what it allows, and show on each role the people who hold it.**

## Resources & references

- Sandhu, Coyne, Feinstein & Youman (1996) [Role-based access control models](https://doi.org/10.1109/2.485845) — rights attached to roles and roles to people, with role hierarchies and constraints
- Burghardt (2009) *Working Through Screens*, C5 "Permissions and views tailored to workers' identities", B7 "Object ownership and availability rules"
- GitHub: [About custom repository roles](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/organizations/managing-user-access-to-your-organizations-repositories/managing-repository-roles/about-custom-repository-roles) — every custom role starts from a stock role and adds to it
- Choate. [Roles and permissions redesign](https://edwinchoate.com/articles/roles-permissions-redesign/) — copying an existing role and stock templates; assigned users shown on the role
- Figr. [Permission management UI](https://figr.design/blog/permission-management-ui) — grants readable as one sentence; templates against the pull toward broad roles
- Kosmalenar. [Roles and permissions UX](https://kosmalenar.com/en/guides/roles-and-permissions-ux/) — role names that show their limits
