**When a collection outgrows what can be scanned or filtered in place, walking its structure forces the actor to guess how someone else organised it; asking by query shifts that work from recognition to recall, where the actor's words rarely match the system's.**

Where [navigation](/patterns/navigation-overview) walks the structure the maker built and [filtering](/patterns/filtering) narrows by exposed attributes, searching ranks the collection against the actor's own words. Because [spontaneous terms for the same object rarely agree](https://doi.org/10.1145/32206.32212) and what comes back often changes what the actor asks next, a query box cannot stand in for the [information architecture](/patterns/information-architecture): it relies on the names and [tags](/patterns/tag) that architecture establishes, and leans on recognition patterns around it—[recents](/patterns/recents) and [autocomplete](/patterns/autocomplete) going in, [suggestions](/patterns/suggestion) and facet [filtering](/patterns/filtering) coming back—to bridge the gap between what the actor types and what the collection holds.

Therefore,

**Accept a query in the actor's words, match it across the names, content, and [tags](/patterns/tag) of the collection, and return a relevance-ranked [data view](/patterns/data-view) where matches can be judged and narrowed in place without losing the view the search was launched from.**

{/* Do not hide the [information architecture](/patterns/information-architecture) behind a search box alone; when a query returns no exact matches, keep [navigation](/patterns/navigation-overview) and relaxed matches in reach rather than stranding the actor on an empty page. */}

## In practice

### Queries parsed into filters

When a natural-language query is translated into structured attributes, render the inferred criteria as editable [filter](/patterns/filtering) chips on the surface so the actor can see and change how their query was parsed.

### Search beside prompting and composed answers

Where one input accepts both search queries and [prompts](/patterns/prompt), or where search places a [generated content](/patterns/generated-content) summary above the ranked [data view](/patterns/data-view) (or returns matches inside a [conversation](/patterns/conversation) as an [inline interface](/patterns/inline-interface)), the risk shifts from finding nothing to accepting an unsupported synthesis without checking the sources. Ground each claim with [citations](/patterns/citation) back to its passage and keep the ranked source items inspectable below it.

<div class="callout">
Search also needs to be reachable by an [outside agent](/patterns/outside-agent) acting on the actor's behalf. Expose query, scope, and filter parameters as machine-readable primitives returning structured matches—and, where the agent renders UI in its reply, self-contained result views that take explicit inputs rather than depending on the surrounding page layout.
</div>

## Research on this pattern

- Vocabulary mismatch: [two people naming the same object across five domains chose the same term less than 20% of the time](https://doi.org/10.1145/32206.32212), which is why keyword lookup fails without synonym matching and recognition aids around the query box.
- Search plus structure: [pairing keyword search with faceted navigation reduced dead ends and was preferred by 91% of participants](https://doi.org/10.1145/642611.642681), and [categorised overviews alongside results helped searchers explore deeper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ipm.2007.07.014) while skewing collected items toward what the taxonomy covered (82.7% vs 75.4%).
- Generative and agentic search: commercial generative search engines [varied widely in how many statements their citations supported (11.1%–68.7% recall; 63.6%–89.5% precision)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848), while participants in low-stakes tasks [clicked fewer than one reference per session](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05880) and [switched from chat back to traditional search only when doubt set in](https://doi.org/10.1145/3698204.3716455); across 3.97M agentic search sessions, [machine trajectories iterated across turns (52.2% multi-query) but favoured local refinement over broadening (under 11%)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17637).
