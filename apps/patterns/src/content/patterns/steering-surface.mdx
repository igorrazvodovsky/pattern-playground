The actor is working on something and the way to act on it is to instruct rather than
to touch: in language, in commands, in a request rather than a gesture. Held in one stream, the work fragments
across the acts that changed it and scrolls away between them, so reading the current
state means reassembling it from a history.

A steering surface separates the two. The work sits on its own surface and holds
still. The acts on it accumulate on another surface beside it — a record of what was
asked, which drives the work rather than displaying it.

## Coupling the two surfaces

[Coordinated views](/patterns/coordinated-views) applies here, and applying it is worth
doing: a turn linked to the version it produced, a command linked to the change it made,
designating one and having the other mark itself. Nothing about the surfaces being
different kinds prevents this — a map and a table are different kinds too.

What it cannot reach is the part that does not correspond. The two surfaces are not two
renderings of one model; they overlap on the work. Much of the instruction surface has no
counterpart in the work — questions, rejected options, talk about something else —
and changes arrive that were never discussed. Coupling can only join the overlap.

The residue also behaves the opposite way to a stale view. Coordinated views treats
divergence as a defect, fixed by propagating faster or by greying the view out until it
catches up. Here past instructions should *not* update when the work changes: they
record what was asked at the time, not what exists now. A change made by hand correctly
leaves no trace on the instruction side.

So the decision this pattern owns is what happens at the gap — whether the instruction
surface is told about changes it did not cause, and which surface an actor is meant to
read as the truth.

## Instances

- a chat pane beside a document or canvas the conversation is writing
- a chat pane beside a file tree, acting on the files
- a REPL beside the editor whose buffer it evaluates
- a terminal beside the file it operates on
- a properties panel or inspector beside the canvas it configures
- a formula bar beside the sheet

The last three are the older cases and they show the move is not conversational by
nature. What conversation adds is that the instruction surface is also a record of
intent, which is what makes the divergence matter.

## To-do

- The name is provisional. It names the instruction half; a name for the arrangement
  rather than one of its halves might be better.
- The divergence force needs grounding. CSCW work on shared artefacts as coordination
  media is the place to look, and whether either surface should be treated as
  authoritative is an open design question rather than a settled one.
- Which surface owns the truth after a direct edit, and whether the instruction surface
  should be told about it, is the decision this page names but does not yet answer.
  Products differ and the trade-off has not been worked out.
