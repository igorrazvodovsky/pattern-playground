* * *

**When items belong to several dimensions at once or the vocabulary describing them is still shifting, forcing each item into a single folder or fixed taxonomy makes actors guess where a thing should live—and later guess where someone else put it. Yet leaving [labelling](/concepts/labelling) completely unstructured lets near-synonyms multiply and meanings drift until the labels no longer bring the right items back.**

Where each item belongs to one settled category, a hierarchy or a single-value field is clearer than tags; running two competing classification schemes—such as folders and tags, or categories and labels—in the same artifact splits effort so items filed in one cannot be found through the other. Open vocabularies do partially converge on common terms over time (Golder and Huberman, 2006), yet near-synonyms, plural variants, and personal shorthand still accumulate unless the input nudges reuse. System-managed labels—such as status markers or an inbox label—cause a second failure when they share the look of actor-applied tags but cannot be removed or trigger side effects when toggled.

Therefore,

**Let actors attach and remove multiple reusable keyword labels on any item in place, without requiring them to pick a single category or define a taxonomy first. Make every applied label an interactive handle that narrows the collection to matching items, suggest existing labels as the actor types to encourage convergence while still allowing new terms, and distinguish system-generated or structural labels clearly from actor-applied ones.**

Do not require tags before an item can be saved: friction at capture causes actors to skip labelling altogether. Before deleting a label that is in use across a collection, show how many items carry it and keep the removal reversible.
