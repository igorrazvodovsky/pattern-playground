The actor is working through a collection item by item — scanning many candidates, verifying one of interest — and no single projection serves both motions: none is at once compact enough to scan and rich enough to verify.

## Forces

- *Scanning vs. verifying* — showing everything at full fidelity overwhelms; one item at a time loses orientation and comparison
- *Compression vs. answerability* — the fewer attributes the compressed rung carries, the more scanning questions can only be answered inside items, forcing pogo-sticking: the roundtrip into each item just to answer a scanning question
- *Preference vs. performance* — users consistently prefer having an overview even where it doesn't improve performance, so the overview claims screen space its measured benefit may not justify

## Solution

Hold two projections of the same collection open at different abstraction rungs: an *overview* rendering the whole population compressed and uniform, and a *detail* view rendering the current item in full, the two coupled by the current item propagating between them. The coupling is focus propagation — the item is navigated to as a position, not acted on as a target ([selection](/patterns/selection) draws this line).

## Three dimensions

The pattern varies along three dimensions (Min et al.'s content analysis of 303 instances — roughly 83% of sampled sites carry the pattern):

### Content

Which attributes live in which view: overview only, detail only, or both — and interactive elements count as attributes too (a save button can live in both views or only one). Overview attributes are often *abstractions* of their detail counterparts: visual (colour shading for seat availability), semantic (a generated title standing for a conversation), or computational (a calendar of cheapest prices). When the abstraction is computed or generated, the overview stops being a subset and becomes a derived rung.

### Composition

How many of each, and how they nest: several overviews sharing one detail (list + map over the same hotels), one overview opening several details, recursion — the pattern nests inside detail views (suggested articles inside an article) — and intermediate detail tiers that soften the jump (tooltip card → full profile). The composition can even invert: a thesaurus entry opens on the detail view, with overviews of synonyms nested inside it — detail-first, the overview arriving second.

### Layout

Where the detail opens: side by side, in place, in a popover, or on a new page. Simultaneous split panes — what this pattern is usually reduced to — is one value here, the topology-preserving one. The list-page → item-page roundtrip that structures most of the web is the same pattern in its *new-page* layout: the coupling survives the page break, carried by navigation state instead of shared screen. Practitioner libraries split these into separate patterns keyed to screen-space conditions; the conditions select a layout, not a different move.

## Scope

The pattern is scoped to item-structured models: a population of discrete items, each holding more than its compressed rendering shows. Continuous-space overview+detail — the minimap beside the source file, the overview map with a field-of-view box — shares the name but couples *viewports over one space*, not rungs over items; that territory belongs to [pan and zoom](/patterns/pan-and-zoom) with the coupling supplied by [coordinated views](/patterns/coordinated-views) (navigational slaving).

## Consequences

- Two rungs of the representation ladder stay open over one collection, the current item propagating between them: the actor scans the list and verifies details without back-and-forth navigation, and switches quickly between items to compare them.
- The overview's attribute subset now silently fixes what the actor can compare, sort, and filter by (Airbnb's map shows only price) — a ceiling only [malleability](/patterns/malleability) lifts, by letting the actor surface, hide, and re-filter attributes across both views.
