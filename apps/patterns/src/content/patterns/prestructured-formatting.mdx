* * *

**Formatting controls that govern appearance force the writer to make typographic choices rather than focusing on meaning. When formatting is about look-and-feel rather than structure, writers spend attention fiddling with fonts and sizes, and documents accumulate inconsistent, brittle visual styling.**

Writing needs structure to be legible. Without headings, lists, quotations, and emphasis, prose collapses into an undifferentiated block of text that is difficult to scan and understand.

When an interface provides direct typographic controls—font families, point sizes, margins, and arbitrary colours—it forces the writer to act as a designer. Every structural decision becomes a visual layout task. Writers spend cognitive effort adjusting point sizes and line spacing instead of shaping their thoughts.

Direct formatting conflates visual appearance with structural meaning. A writer might make a line bold and 16 points high to indicate a heading, or use spaces to indent a list. Because the system records only the visual styles and not what the text represents, subsequent edits break the layout. Changing font sizes or pasting text from another source introduces conflicting styles, leaving documents visually inconsistent and brittle.

Therefore,

**Give the writer a small, fixed vocabulary of roles that describe what each part of the text means: heading, body, list, quotation, code, emphasis, and link. Render each role immediately in place, but withhold visual styling controls like font, size, and colour from the editing interface. Fit the vocabulary to the writing surface: short comments need only inline emphasis and lists, while documents need hierarchical headings.**

## In practice

### Roles by scope

- *Document roles*: elements that define the document as a whole, such as a title fixed as the first line.
- *Block roles*: container-level elements that structure paragraphs, headings, ordered and unordered lists, quotations, and code blocks.
- *Span roles*: inline styling within a block, such as emphasis, strong emphasis, strikethrough, inline code, and links.

### Input mechanisms

- *Contextual floating toolbar*: displays semantic roles on selection, keeping formatting controls out of sight until needed.
- *Command menu*: typing a slash (`/`) opens available block roles directly at the caret without moving to a fixed toolbar.
- *Keyboard shortcuts*: direct key combinations for frequently applied roles (`Cmd+B`, `Cmd+K`, heading shortcuts).
- *Input rules*: typing plain-text conventions (such as `#` for a heading or `*` for a list item) converts text into the corresponding role immediately, keeping the writer in flow.
- *Hybrid preview*: renders formatted roles in place while revealing editable syntax at the active caret, blending plain-text efficiency with immediate visual confirmation.
