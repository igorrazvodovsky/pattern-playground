Two lines of work were allowed to diverge — a branch, a draft, an offline stretch — and both changed the same thing. Merging silently would discard one intent without anyone deciding; refusing to merge stops the work. Reconciliation makes the collision a first-class object: both versions shown, comparable, with reintegration blocked until a person picks and the choice recorded.

The pattern is as much about placement as mechanism. Live co-editing surfaces never show conflicts — concurrent edits are absorbed silently, and the industry has spent a decade retiring even paragraph locks in favour of silent merge. The reconciliation surface belongs exactly where divergence was deliberate: the offline return, the branch merge, the pull request. There it blocks: the merge button deactivates, the branch review lists each collision side by side, and a person picks.

The documented failure is surfacing without support: a conflicted copy dropped beside the original, with no way to compare the versions — the collision preserved but the deciding made impossible.
