<Figure name="reconciliation" />

**When two actors have changed the same thing and the mechanism can decide which of them meant it. A merge that picks silently throws one intent away without anyone knowing; a merge that refuses stops the work; and a conflicted copy dropped beside the original keeps both while making the choice impossible, since nothing shows how they differ.**

The pattern is as much about placement as mechanism. Live co-editing surfaces never show conflicts: concurrent edits are absorbed silently, and the industry has spent a decade retiring even paragraph locks in favour of silent merge. The reconciliation surface belongs where divergence was deliberate, at the offline return, the branch merge, the pull request. There it blocks: the merge button deactivates, the branch review lists each collision side by side, and a person picks. The documented failure is surfacing without support, the conflicted copy left beside the original with no way to compare the two.

Which merges can be done without a person, and which collide, is set by the merge model beneath (locking, operational transformation, CRDTs), and that model is substrate rather than part of this pattern. What the pattern claims is only that the residue the model cannot resolve is shown and decided, not dropped.

Therefore,

**Where a deliberate parting returns and both sides changed the same thing, show the collision as both versions, comparable, and hold reintegration until a person picks one and the choice is recorded.**

Show this only at the return of deliberate divergence. A live ground merges concurrent edits silently, and a collision surfacing mid-flow says the boundary between live and reviewed work was drawn wrong for that kind of work, not that this surface is missing. The grain is set by the parting's: what the mechanism can show separately is what a person can pick separately, and no finer.

## Resources & references

- Kleppmann, Wiggins, van Hardenberg & McGranaghan (2019) [Local-first software](https://martin.kleppmann.com/papers/local-first.pdf): the same-property residue a merge cannot resolve, and the Evernote conflicted-copy failure
- [Semantic conflict model for collaborative data structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19231): implicit CRDT resolution as "opaque to users"; the case for explicit, visible conflicts
- GitHub Docs: [About merge conflicts](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/reference/merge-conflicts); Figma Learn: [branch conflict review](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/5665728006423-Get-updates-from-main-files)
- Burghardt (2009) *Working Through Screens*, B7 "Object ownership and availability rules": the locking era's form of the same legibility claim
