**Actors judge how much to trust a statement partly by how confidently it is stated. Because a generative model writes with the same fluent tone whether it is stating an established fact or guessing, actors treat guesses as facts unless the output itself distinguishes between them.**

Uncertainty can be shown in four ways across an interface: hedged wording in prose ("records from that period are patchy"), numeric or temporal ranges ("19.5–21.5 SEK"), coarse categorical tiers ("possible match", "low confidence"), and visual marks on doubtful spans within a longer passage. What stays constant across them is a visible contrast between the parts the system has grounds for and the parts where its ground is thin, which is the signal-raising step of [assistance](/patterns/assistance).

Two tensions govern the mark:

- *False precision*: a number such as "80% confident" looks like a measured probability, but in a generative system it may be another uncalibrated claim. When precision in the mark overstates precision in the ground, coarse tiers or ranges are more honest than a percentage.
- *Selective marking vs. blanket hedging*: hedging every sentence, or placing a standing caveat at the foot of every reply, is as useless as asserting everything. Actors quickly stop reading a mark that never varies; the signal comes from the difference between the marked span and the unmarked prose around it.

Therefore,

**Mark the specific spans, values, or proposals where the system's support is thin—using hedged phrasing, value ranges, coarse confidence tiers, or span highlights—and leave well-supported parts unmarked so the contrast directs the actor's check.**

Do not display a percentage or decimal confidence score unless the underlying model is calibrated so that the number tracks actual accuracy; use coarse tiers or ranges instead. Avoid blanket disclaimers attached to every output, which shift liability without telling the actor which part of the output to check.

## Research on this pattern

- In a large-scale AI search experiment ([Li & Aral, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435)), highlighting uncertain spans lowered trust in wrong answers where hallucinated citations raised it.
