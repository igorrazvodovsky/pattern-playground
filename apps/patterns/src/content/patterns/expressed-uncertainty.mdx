**Actors judge how much to trust a statement partly by how confidently it is stated. Because a generative model writes with the same fluent tone whether it is stating an established fact or guessing, actors treat guesses as facts unless the output itself distinguishes between them.**

Uncertainty can be shown in four ways across an interface: hedged wording in prose ("records from that period are patchy"), numeric or temporal ranges ("19.5–21.5 SEK"), coarse categorical tiers ("possible match", "low confidence"), and visual marks on doubtful spans within a longer passage. A hedge can also name its grounds ("not all the sources I checked agree"), which tells the actor what the doubt rests on as well as where it lies. What stays constant across them is a visible contrast between the parts the system has grounds for and the parts where its ground is thin, which is the signal-raising step of [assistance](/patterns/assistance).

Three tensions govern the mark:

- *False precision*: a number such as "80% confident" looks like a measured probability, but in a generative system it may be another uncalibrated claim. When precision in the mark overstates precision in the ground, coarse tiers or ranges are more honest than a percentage.
- *Selective marking vs. blanket hedging*: hedging every sentence, or placing a standing caveat at the foot of every reply, is as useless as asserting everything. Actors quickly stop reading a mark that never varies; the signal comes from the difference between the marked span and the unmarked prose around it.
- *Unexplained marks*: when an indicator does not say what it measures, actors infer their own meaning: the chance the claim is right, the share of the output that is accurate, faithfulness to a source, or a sign that the system has already verified it. The last assumption reverses the mark's purpose, turning a prompt to check into a reason to skip checking.

What the mark rests on decides whether the actor can check it. Where the output draws on sources, the mark can be graded by how much evidence supports the claim and how far that evidence agrees, in terms whose meaning is stated. This is how the [IPCC's calibrated language](https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf) derives confidence from evidence and agreement, and how [GRADE](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.39489.470347.AD) rates the certainty of a body of medical evidence. Where there is nothing to retrieve, as with a completion, a classifier's match, or an estimated range, the mark rests on the model's own signal and cannot be checked in the same way.

The two errors carry different costs for trust. An incorrect claim marked as confident damages trust more than an accurate claim marked as doubtful. Where calibration is uncertain, understating confidence is safer than overstating it.

Therefore,

**Mark the specific spans, values, or proposals where the system's support is thin—using hedged phrasing, value ranges, coarse confidence tiers, or span highlights—and leave well-supported parts unmarked so the contrast directs the actor's check. Where the output draws on sources, grade each mark by how much evidence supports the claim and how far it agrees.**

Do not display a percentage or decimal confidence score unless the underlying model is calibrated so that the number tracks actual accuracy; use coarse tiers or ranges instead. Say what a mark stands for; a grade defined by evidence and agreement says it by construction. Avoid blanket disclaimers attached to every output, which shift liability without telling the actor which part of the output to check.

## Research on this pattern

- In a large-scale AI search experiment ([Li & Aral, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435)), highlighting uncertain spans lowered trust in wrong answers where hallucinated citations raised it.
- In interviews, [participants interpreted the same confidence mark in conflicting ways](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32883)—including as a sign that the system had already verified the answer—and several changed interpretations during a single interview. In the study's experiment (5,151 participants), a single confidence label or percentage for a whole answer shifted initial trust only slightly, and the effect disappeared once correctness became known.
- In the same experiment, [trust fell further after an error when the answer had been marked high-confidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32883). Marking confidence as agreement or disagreement across sources, paired with source icons, was rated the most informative condition.
