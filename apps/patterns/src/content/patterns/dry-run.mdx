The actor has written something the system will apply to many cases: a mail rule, a find-and-replace across a project, a rename over a folder of files, a formula in a column, a template that will be merged with a list. Reading it does not say what it will do. The cases it will meet are either out of view or have not arrived yet, and a mistake lands on all of them at once. A dry run applies the thing the actor wrote to real or sample cases and shows what would happen, without letting it happen.

Replace-all with a preview of every occurrence, a batch rename that lists old and new names side by side, a mail-merge preview, a regex tester, an automation's "test this step", and replaying a mail rule against the inbox are all this move.

## Forces

- *Representative vs. cheap* — the cases that decide whether the rule is right are the ones near
  its edge, and a random or first-N sample rarely holds them. A country-music rule previewed
  against a library that is mostly not country shows no country at all.
- *Rehearsal vs. the real run* — what the run shows is true of the cases it ran on. The rule meets
  other cases later, and the actor's confidence from the rehearsal carries over to them.

## Forms

- *Replay the past.* Apply the thing written to what already happened and show what would have
  come out differently. This is the form shown to improve people's success. It works because the
  cases are real and the actor already has opinions about them.
- *Run against the standing set.* Step a new rule through alongside the rules already in place, so
  conflicts and loops show up before saving. This is the only form that reaches the interaction
  between one rule and the list it joins.
- *Show the edge cases.* Show the cases that now match for the first time, the cases that match but
  only just, and the cases that miss but only just. This is the form to use when there is no
  history to replay.
- *Live result.* When the thing written applies to a visible collection, the result updates as each
  part changes and the dry run never stops. [Filtering](/patterns/filtering) is this form.

## Research on this pattern

- [Replaying a changed rule against past cases was the repair that raised success](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3569506)
  in end-to-end debugging of trigger–action rules.
- [Simulating a rule against the whole standing set](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3290605.3300618)
  is the one tested form that reaches conflicts between rules.
- [Offered the system's correct fix among alternatives, only half to three-fifths of people chose it](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3569506):
  seeing the effect does not by itself tell the actor which change repairs it.
- Untested: the edge-case preview, and every form outside rules.
