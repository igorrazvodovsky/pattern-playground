The actor needs stretches of uninterrupted work, but every channel is open by default and every sender judges their own message urgent. The only way to protect the time is to close the tools, which also hides the few things that genuinely cannot wait. So people either work interrupted or work anxious.

Protected focus lets the actor declare stretches, scheduled ahead or switched on in the moment, during which each channel holds what arrives. A narrow way through, limited to named senders or the highest grade of event and to a few uses, means the protection does not have to be all or nothing. Others can see the status and how long it lasts, which reduces how many messages are sent in the first place.

## Where it is practised

- Phone and desktop focus modes that silence notifications on a schedule, with exceptions for chosen people and repeated calls.
- Chat tools with "do not disturb" and working hours, shown to others on the actor's profile, with a choice for the sender to notify anyway.
- Calendars that block focus time and decline meetings during it.

## Resources & references

- Mark, Gudith & Klocke (2008), "The cost of interrupted work: more speed and stress", CHI — the cost of interruption to the people working through it
- Iqbal & Bailey (2008), "Effects of intelligent notification management on users and their tasks", CHI — holding notifications until a breakpoint in the task reduces their cost
