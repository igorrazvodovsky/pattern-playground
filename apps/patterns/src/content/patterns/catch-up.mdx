The actor returns to work that other people or agents changed while they were away. The history holds every change, but reading it from the beginning costs more than the work, and the actor's memory of the document, board, or codebase is now partly out of date. Without help they either read too much or notice too little, and edit over changes they never saw.

Catch-up marks what changed since the actor's last visit, in the work they have open: which parts changed, who or what changed them, and why, where the reason was recorded. It asks for no decision. The changes are already made, and the actor's task is to update their picture of the work before they continue it.

## Where it is practised

- Document editors that offer to show changes made since the actor last opened the file, highlighted in place.
- Design tools that mark frames edited since the actor's last view.
- Chat and tracker tools that place a "new since you were last here" line in a thread or list.
- Code review tools that show only the commits pushed since the reviewer's last pass.

## Resources & references

- Tam & Greenberg (2006), "A framework for asynchronous change awareness in collaborative documents and workspaces", *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* — what changed, where, who, how and why, as the questions a returning collaborator asks
