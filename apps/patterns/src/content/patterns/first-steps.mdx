* * *

**Newcomers start acting before they understand an artifact, and they skip instructions written for them. A first run that teaches by explaining is mostly ignored. A first run that lets them act without protection lets early mistakes cost them work or confidence, and they form wrong ideas about what the artifact is for.**

Carroll and Rosson describe this as the paradox of the active user: people want to get something done straight away, skip manuals, and carry over methods from tools they already know, even where new methods would serve them better. In Carroll and Carrithers' training-wheels study, a word processor with its error-prone functions blocked let new users learn faster and understand more than the full system did, and the control group spent almost a quarter of their time recovering from errors the reduced system made unreachable. The cost is that every scaffold put up for the first run has to come down later. A first run that is long, mandatory or never ends delays the result the actor came for and gets in the way once they are competent.

Therefore,

**Make the actor's first stretch of use a designed period with a clear start and end. Let them do real work in it rather than read about it, and make mistakes cheap to undo. Decide in advance what it must teach: which things exist and where they are kept, what happens when the actor acts, and what the artifact will do on its own. End it when the actor has completed something real, and take its scaffolding down.**

Do not make the first run a gate. An actor who already knows the artifact, or who wants to look around first, should be able to skip it and come back to it.

## In practice

### What the first run teaches

- *What exists*: the objects the actor will work with and where their state is kept.
- *What acting does*: the consequence of the main actions, shown on the actor's own content where possible.
- *What the artifact does on its own*: anything automated or delegated, with when it acts and how to override it.
- *What the limits are*: constraints stated in the terms of the domain rather than as unexplained rules.

### Making mistakes cheap

Give the first run [undo](/patterns/undo), drafts and previews, or a sample space the actor can change freely without affecting real data. A [layered interface](/patterns/layered-interface) removes some mistakes altogether by holding back the functions where they happen.

### Ending the first run

Record what the actor has completed or dismissed in [remembered state](/patterns/remembered-state), so that tips and tours do not come back, and keep a way to restart the first run from [help](/patterns/help).
