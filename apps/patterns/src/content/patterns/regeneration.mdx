* * *

**When a generated result misses the mark in a way the actor can recognise on sight but cannot easily put into words, rewriting the prompt from scratch guesses blindly at which words were to blame, and repairing a poor draft by hand takes on the work the system was meant to do. Yet if asking again simply overwrites what is there, every redraw is a gamble that risks trading a flawed but usable draft for a worse one.**

Because a generative model samples from a distribution of plausible answers, a single output is only one draw from what the request already allows. Drawing again tests the request without rearticulating it, shifting effort from composing instructions to judging candidates. Drawing multiple candidates in parallel turns that judgement into side-by-side comparison, reducing fixation on the first draft and revealing where the model is consistent versus guessing. Across all forms—whole output, selected region, or parallel variants—the new draw runs from the request rather than taking the rejected output as material to revise.

The move has a clear ceiling. When the prompt lacks a required constraint or rests on the wrong sources, redrawing only samples more wrong answers from the same underspecified distribution, and each extra candidate adds reading and comparison work.

Therefore,

**Let the actor draw another output from the same request with a single action—for the whole result, for a selected region, or as a small set of parallel variants—and keep every replaced draw reachable so the actor can step back or compare before choosing which version to keep.**

Cap parallel variants at a small set—typically two to four—so comparing them does not become a second reading burden, and never let a redraw silently destroy a draft the actor has already edited by hand. When the actor regenerates repeatedly without accepting a result, surface the prompt or its parameters for editing rather than encouraging endless re-rolls against a request that cannot produce what they want.

## In practice

### Whole-output redraw and version stepping

In a [conversation](/patterns/conversation) or single-shot generator, a regenerate control on the response draws a replacement turn from the same prompt. Keep the previous answer in a numbered version switcher (`1 / 2`, `2 / 2`) attached to the turn so the actor can step between draws and pick the better one.

### Scoped regeneration and inpainting

Selecting a span of text or masking an image region scopes the redraw to that selection while passing the surrounding content as fixed context, so the new draw fits its neighbours. When the selection is accompanied by a directive naming how the span should change, the move crosses into [instructed revision](/patterns/instructed-revision); when the actor simply asks for another try in that spot, it remains regeneration.

### Parallel variants

Instead of making the actor ask again after a disappointing single draw, a tool can generate parallel candidates up front or on demand—common in image generation, copy drafting, and layout exploration. Present them in place via a carousel, hover switcher, or side-by-side grid, and hand off to [comparing](/patterns/comparing) when differences need to be read directly across longer outputs.

## Research on this pattern

- *Parallel candidates vs. serial iteration*: [Dow et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1879831.1879836) found that generating and comparing multiple candidates in parallel before refining produced more diverse exploration, higher-rated outcomes, and less fixation on the initial draft than serial iteration.
- *Designing for generative variability*: [Weisz et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14484) identify generative variability as a defining property of generative AI and recommend surfacing multiple outputs, legible differences, and version tracking rather than forcing a single answer.
- *In-place variation management*: [Reza et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3641899) (*ABScribe*) showed that storing text variations adjacently in place for rapid hover-and-click comparison lowered task workload ($d = 1.20$) and improved writers' revision experience ($d = 2.41$) compared with sequential overwriting.
- *Self-consistency across samples*: [Cheng et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3641904) (*RELIC*) demonstrated that sampling multiple responses to the same prompt and visualising agreement and divergence helps actors judge which claims in long-form output are reliable.
