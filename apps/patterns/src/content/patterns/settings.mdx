* * *

**No single default fits every actor's context, working style, or privacy boundary, yet every option exposed to cover those differences adds another control that everyone else has to look past. When preferences are buried in primary workflows they clutter everyday work; when every unresolved design decision is dumped into a configuration screen instead, the surface grows until actors cannot find the few controls they actually need—and any preference they do change silently shapes future behaviour long after they have forgotten setting it.**

Whether a choice belongs in settings turns on whether it is genuinely preferential—time format, locale, notification tolerance, keyboard habits—where no single default fits everyone and the choice lasts across sessions. A setting also earns its place when it serves a smaller group whose need is essential to their use of the artifact, such as an accessibility or regional requirement, even if most actors never change it. Frequently toggled options belong in the primary interface as a mode switch or filter, inline adjustments made while working belong to [remembered state](/patterns/remembered-state), and unresolved product decisions belong to neither: every option nobody changes still slows down actors hunting for the ones they do. Beyond configuration, a clean settings surface doubles as a readable map of what the artifact lets the actor control.

Therefore,

**Provide a dedicated settings surface for genuine, infrequent preferences that override standing defaults across sessions. Keep the list small by making sensible defaults do the work for most actors, match the structure to the number of options—flat when there are seven or fewer, sectioned up to about sixteen, and split into domain pages beyond that—and separate personal, team, and organisational scopes clearly so every actor can see who a change affects and whether they have authority to make it.**

Never require an actor to visit settings before they can start working, and keep defaults safe for actors who never open the screen.

## In practice

- *Search at scale*: once settings split across pages, add search and match navigation labels to page titles so actors who know what they want can jump straight to it.
- *Inherited and locked values*: where personal, team, and organisational scopes nest, show whether an active value is inherited or overridden; when a control is locked by a higher role, state why and who can change it rather than disabling it silently.
- *In-context entry points*: link directly to the relevant setting from the screen where its behaviour shows, and offer to promote a repeated inline adjustment into a standing default.
- *High-impact changes*: pair organisational or wide-reaching overrides with confirmation friction proportional to how many actors they affect.
