* * *

**When a statement draws on sources that are out of sight, checking whether it is true or faithful to those sources means hunting down the original material from scratch. Because finding the passage behind a claim takes far more effort than reading the claim, people rarely check: they either accept what is written on trust—letting wrong or distorted claims pass alongside right ones—or refuse to rely on it at all.**

Five forces shape how citations work in practice:

- *Mark vs. follow*: the mark raises trust whether or not anyone follows it, and most actors do not. The sign of checkability gets read as the fact of having checked, even when the linked source is irrelevant or wrong.
- *Document vs. passage*: the characteristic failure of generated citations is not a dead link, but a real source that does not say what the claim says. A pointer to a whole document is cheap to produce and expensive to use, because the actor has to find the supporting sentence themselves. A pointer to the passage is what makes the check short.
- *Staying vs. leaving*: professional fact-checkers verify by leaving the page and reading laterally across sources, whereas hover cards and in-place excerpts keep the actor inside the answer.
- *Coverage vs. correctness*: citing narrows what gets said, because the system leans toward what it can point at. Citing during generation buys precision and loses coverage; attaching citations afterwards covers more and supports less.
- *Every claim vs. the claims that need it*: marking every sentence turns the marks into noise; marking nothing leaves a contested claim indistinguishable from an ordinary one.

Citations take forms ordered by how close the pointer sits to the evidence: a marker at the claim resolving to a highlighted span in the source, a domain chip opening a snippet popover, a source panel alongside the answer, or an unanchored list of URLs at the foot (each is described under In practice). What makes a citation verifiable is the two-ended anchor—from a specific claim to a specific span.

Therefore,

**Attach a pointer at each claim that needs verification—quotations, figures, and contested or surprising statements—resolving to the specific passage in the source that backs it. Let the actor preview the passage in place and open the source at that span, and place the citations where the actor meets them before any explanation of the answer.**

## In practice

### Passage at the claim

The marker resolves to the exact span, highlighted in the source, with the quoted text previewed on hover. NotebookLM's numbered markers open the document scrolled to the highlighted paragraph; Anthropic's Citations API makes the span the unit of the contract, with each claim carrying the cited text and its character range in a named document. Adobe Acrobat's inline highlights and Granola's transcript quotations are the same form on PDFs and recordings. The cost of following is lowest here; the risk is premature convergence on one source.

The same anchor works when the source is the actor's own input, as in a summary of text they pasted in. Highlights in the input and the output are paired, and pointing at or focusing one traces it to its counterpart, so the actor sees which part of what they supplied each part of the summary rests on.

### Chip with popover

A domain chip at the end of the claim ("northjersey +3"), opening an anchored card with title, snippet, favicon, and 1/N paging where several sources back one claim. Perplexity and ChatGPT both use it. The "+N" collapses multiplicity without cluttering the prose; the snippet is scent, not the evidence.

## Research on this pattern

- Citation marks raise trust in generated answers even when the citations are [random](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01303) or [hallucinated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435), and trust falls when participants check them — an example of the [authority heuristic](https://www.issuelab.org/resources/875/875.pdf).
- Following is rare: [most participants never open a source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08554), [answer engines draw a fraction of the source engagement search engines do](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22349), [links inside Google's AI summaries are clicked on 1% of visits](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), and [whether an actor verifies at all is set more by prior disposition than by the answer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28498).
- [Unfolding evidence in place deepened what readers took from sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00361), though it measured reading depth, not reliance. Separately, [sources reduced overreliance where explanations increased it](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08554) — the basis for placing sources before explanations in the instruction.
