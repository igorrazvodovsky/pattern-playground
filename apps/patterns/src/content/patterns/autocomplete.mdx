**When a field draws from a large or unfamiliar set of values, asking the actor to type from memory invites spelling errors and dead ends, while showing the whole list up front crowds the interface.**

Where the valid answers are few enough to display in full, typing to narrow adds a step the actor does not need. When the set does warrant narrowing, the actor's own recent and frequent entries often beat algorithmic rankings and deserve a place near the top.

A subtler boundary lies between *narrowing*—filtering a known index—and *expanding*—proposing related or trending terms the actor has not thought of. Narrowing is looked up and expanding is inferred, yet the same dropdown can cross from one to the other without changing shape, hiding the crossing unless inferred candidates are marked.

Therefore,

**Display a narrowing list of looked-up candidates beside the input as the actor types, placing exact, recent, and frequent matches first. Keep the actor's typed text intact until they commit a candidate, and label any inferred or expanded terms so the actor can tell what exists in the index from what the system is proposing.**

Make clear whether the actor may submit free text or must pick from the list; when that boundary is hidden, typed values are either silently discarded or rejected only on submission.
