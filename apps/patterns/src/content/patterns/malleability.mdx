The capacity for users to reshape their tools with minimal friction—from adjusting preferences to building custom workflows.

Where [adaptability](/patterns/adaptation) describes how the *system* responds to users, malleability describes how easily *users* can reshape the system itself. Both require [agency](/patterns/agency), but malleability specifically concerns user-driven modification.

## Scope of change

What can be made malleable, from surface to core:

- *UI*: Visual appearance, <ComponentRef id="foundations-layout--docs">layout</ComponentRef>, and presentation. Includes <ComponentRef id="foundations-color--docs">colour</ComponentRef>, [density](/patterns/density), [accessibility](/patterns/a11y) preferences, and [which attributes a view carries](/patterns/attribute-visibility).
- *Logic*: Behaviour, rules, and automations. [Interaction](/patterns/interaction) patterns and [saved views](/patterns/data-view#saved-views).
- *Data*: [Schemas, structures](/patterns/data), and entity definitions.

## Authorship

Who shapes the artefact, and when, is an axis of its own — running from design-time (one instantiation, fixed for everyone) to use-time (shaped by the person using it). The current decade is moving along it: the stronger stance is *malleable by default, selectively disabled*, where fixedness is a decision someone makes rather than the substrate's nature. Enforced commonality then becomes a deliberate ceiling — legitimate when a group needs to see one picture in common, and usually scoped to a ritual or a role (the shared ward display, the team's [saved view](/patterns/data-view#saved-views)) rather than imposed across the board.

## Gentle slope

Users progress from passive use to active creation. The goal: each step requires only incremental investment of learning, with no sharp "cliffs" that block progression.

### Use → Configure → Modify → Create

- *Use*: Interact with defaults as provided
- *Configure*: Adjust exposed settings and preferences
- *Modify*: Rearrange, combine, and extend existing elements
- *Create*: Build new views, tools, or workflows

### Avoiding cliffs

Sharp jumps in required skill block progression. MacLean et al. (1990) propose that each incremental increase in tailoring power should require only a small incremental investment of learning.

Key enablers:
- In-place toolchain: Edit where you use, no separate development environment
- Progressive disclosure: Reveal complexity only when needed
- Safe experimentation: Undo, drafts, and previews reduce fear. [Temporality](/patterns/temporality) enables versioning and history.

### Not everyone needs to reach the top

Most users stay in the lower and middle parts of the slope. "Local developers"—enthusiasts within a team or community—help others climb higher when needed. This enables collaboration between people with different skill levels.

## TODO: Communal creation

Malleability isn't purely individual. Customizations can be shared, and communities can collectively shape their tools.

- Individual modifications become [shareable](/patterns/shareability) templates
- "Local developers" guide others up the slope
- *Situated software*: tools shaped for specific community needs
- Shared modifications benefit groups, not just individuals
