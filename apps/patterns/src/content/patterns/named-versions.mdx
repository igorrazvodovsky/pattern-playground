The work has moved past the reach of undo. The session has ended, or many changes and many people came in between, and the actor needs a particular earlier state: the version that went to the client, the one that was approved, the one that shipped. A full history of every autosave exists in many systems, but it is too long to search and gives no sign of which states mattered.

Named versions mark the states that mattered. Some moments name themselves, such as a publish or a release. Others are named by the actor when they reach something worth keeping. Restoring then goes to a name rather than back a number of steps, and the restore is recorded as a change of its own, so nothing after it is lost.

## Where it is practised

- Document editors with a version history in which the actor can name a version and restore or copy it.
- Design tools that add a named version on publish and let the actor add their own.
- Version control, where tags and releases name commits within a much longer history.
- Operating system backups that list snapshots by date and let the actor restore one file or the whole set.
