The actor's work is broken off by a notification, the end of the day, a run handed back, or a crash. Most systems keep the content, but not the actor's place in it. Coming back means finding the spot again, recovering whatever was half-typed, and remembering what the next step was going to be. Studies of interrupted work find that this reconstruction, more than the interruption itself, is where the time goes.

Resumption keeps the place along with the content: the open document and the position in it, the views and their arrangement, the unsaved input, the selection, and where possible a cue to the step the actor was about to take. On return the system restores all of this, or offers to.

## Where it is practised

- Browsers and editors that reopen the previous session's tabs or files at the same scroll position and cursor.
- Forms and composers that keep a draft through a closed tab or a lost connection.
- Reading apps that return to the last page read, across devices.
- Operating systems that relaunch applications with their windows in place after a restart.
