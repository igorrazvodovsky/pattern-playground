* * *

**When people review shared work, most of what they have to say is about a particular passage, frame or line, and it needs an answer from someone else. Raised in chat or email, the point loses its place and the reader has to find what it refers to. Written into the work itself, it changes what is being discussed and has to be cleaned out later.**

Google Docs, Figma, Notion and GitHub pull-request review all keep comments in a layer beside the content, tied to a range, a point or a line. They differ mainly in what happens when the content under a comment changes: GitHub, for one, marks the comment outdated rather than dropping it.

Therefore,

**Let anyone who can read the work attach a [comment](/concepts/commenting) to a passage, a point or a line, and keep the thread in a layer beside the content, so the work reads as it is with the comments hidden. Show which passage each open thread belongs to, and keep that tie through edits. When the passage under a thread changes or is removed, keep the thread and show what it was about. Resolving a thread takes it out of the way, not out of reach: keep resolved threads listed and readable.**

## In practice

### Threading

- *Flat.* Replies form one list under the first comment. Enough where a point is usually settled in a few replies.
- *Threaded.* Replies can answer other replies. Suits longer discussions, at the cost of a thread that is harder to follow beside the content.

### Resolution

- *Kept until resolved.* A thread stays open until someone resolves it, which suits documentation and decisions that will be looked up later.
- *Fading.* Comments drop out of view after a while without being resolved, which suits quick feedback on work that changes daily.

### States

- *No comments yet.* The way to add one is visible where a passage is selected, without taking space from the content.
- *Open threads.* Each commented passage is marked, and the actor can move from one thread to the next.
- *Resolved threads.* Hidden by default and available on request, with who resolved them.
- *Draft.* A comment being written is saved as it is typed and is clearly not yet posted.

### Text documents

<Demo name="commenting" surface="white" label="Commenting via bubble menu" />

A comment marks its passage without changing the text, and overlapping comments keep separate threads. Later the actor can turn a commented passage into a quote. The words stay where they are, the thread moves to the quote, and other documents can refer to the quote by its identity. This is [incremental formalisation](/patterns/incremental-formalisation) applied to one passage: the passage gains structure when it starts to matter, not when the first comment is written. The demo cannot yet turn a quote back into a plain comment.
