* * *

**When interdependent edits commit one field at a time, half-finished changes leave the artifact in a state nobody asked for and leave the actor with no clean way to abandon an experiment. Yet holding edits back without making the pending state unmistakable causes actors to close the screen thinking their work was already kept, or to press a generic Save button without knowing what will take effect.**

Two legitimate drivers produce pending state: *transaction scope* (a settings panel, interdependent fields, or a batch of deletions that must land together or not at all) and *technical constraints* (expensive, rate-limited, or whole-record writes). Where neither holds and an explicit Save button is present only out of habit, [automatic saving](/patterns/automatic-saving) belongs instead.

Pending state is what makes genuine Cancel possible—discarding an attempt restores the prior committed state cleanly without stepping backward through individual edits—and it buys a pause for review before consequences commit.

Therefore,

**Hold the actor's edits in a visible pending state until they explicitly apply or discard the whole unit. Label the committing control for what it actually does (Apply, Confirm, or Update), and provide a reachable Cancel that restores the prior state cleanly.**

Do not silently mix screens that hold pending changes with adjacent screens that save automatically; where both exist in the same artifact, mark the boundary clearly so the actor knows which edits still need committing.

## In practice

### Save and cancel as confirmation

An explicit commit can protect against destructive operations. Deletions are held as pending—struck through or faded—and applied together, so one action confirms the whole batch and cancel restores everything. The value here is the batch scope and the reachable cancel, not the storage; see [deletion](/patterns/deletion).
