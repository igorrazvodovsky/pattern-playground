Events arrive in numbers that make reading each one cost more than it is worth: comments on a busy document, updates on a large project, messages in a crowded channel. Switching them off hides the occasional one that mattered, so the actor ends up choosing between constant interruption and not knowing.

A digest is a [notification](/patterns/notification) that carries many events at once. It collects events of the same kind and delivers them together at a few predictable times the actor has chosen. In a field experiment, batching notifications three times a day left people less stressed and more attentive than either constant delivery or none at all; with no notifications, people grew anxious about missing things. The digest says that it is a summary and links each point to the event it came from. Events of the highest grade are never folded in, because a summary tends to drop exactly the unusual item.

Where the digest is generated rather than grouped, it adds a new risk: a wrong summary, read as if the source had said it. Generated notification summaries on one phone platform produced false headlines attributed to a news organisation and were withdrawn for news apps; they returned set in italics, with a warning and a switch on the lock screen to turn them off. A generated digest is opt-in, set apart visually from the source's own text, and links back to every event it summarises.

## Where it is practised

- Email digests from forums, trackers, and social services, sent daily or weekly in place of one email per event.
- Chat tools that summarise unread messages in a channel, with links to the original messages.
- Code hosting and project tools with a choice between immediate notifications and a periodic summary.

## Resources & references

- Fitz, Kushlev, Jagannathan, Lewis, Paliwal & Ariely (2019) [Batching smartphone notifications can improve well-being](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1402953), *Computers in Human Behavior* 101 — a field experiment with 237 participants: batching three times a day against constant delivery and no notifications
- [Apple halts AI notification summaries for news apps after backlash](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/business/apple-halt-ai-notification-summaries-backlash-features) (Silicon Republic, January 2025) — generated summaries misattributed to a news source, then reintroduced in italics with a warning and an opt-out
