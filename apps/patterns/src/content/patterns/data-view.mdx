* * *

**A collection shown in one fixed way answers someone else's guess at what the actor is trying to do. Because the same items serve scanning, comparing, scheduling, and triaging, a hard-coded query, layout, and sort order forces actors to hold their own framing in working memory on every visit—or export the data into a spreadsheet to reshape it.**

A framing separates three independent choices over one collection: the *query* (which items), the *representation* (how each item renders—row, card, calendar block, map pin, plot point), and the *arrangement* (order and grouping). Every part fixed at design time is a comparison the actor cannot make; every part handed over is a decision the actor must now maintain, so sensible per-representation defaults still matter.

When an interface supports many representations, a long view-switcher dropdown becomes friction of its own. Letting actors select the attributes they care about—two numeric fields to open a scatter plot, a date to open a timeline, an extracted colour to open a colour space—invokes the matching representation directly from the question (Min et al., 2025). Actors also open multiple views over the same collection not only to compare visual encodings, but to partition sub-tasks (for example, keeping home and work candidates in separate views with their own criteria, then comparing across them).

Therefore,

**Give the collection a named projection built from three inspectable, adjustable parts: a query (which items), a representation (rendered how), and an arrangement (ordered and grouped by what). Keep the framing controls visible on the surface so the actor can switch representations, adjust visible attributes, and save or discard framings without altering the underlying collection.**

Do not bury framing controls in a separate configuration screen, and keep each representation's default attribute set tuned to its density budget.

## In practice

### Writable views

A view is not only read through—some edits are legal through the projection itself. Which edits, and through which representation, is a design decision: [dragging](/patterns/drag-and-drop) a card between board lanes writes the grouping attribute, while dragging across a computed or read-only band must be refused clearly.
