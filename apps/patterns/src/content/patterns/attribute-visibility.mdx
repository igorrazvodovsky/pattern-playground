* * *

**Every attribute a compressed view shows adds scanning cost on every item, and every attribute it omits blocks a comparison the actor came to make. Because actors diverge immediately in what they compare even on the same task, no single designer-chosen subset can fit everyone—leaving actors either wading through irrelevant fields or opening items one by one to check what the list hides.**

Empirical studies of overview–detail interfaces show how rarely products hand this control over—Min et al. (2025) found only 8.33% of surveyed sites offered any overview–detail customisation and only 2.56% offered content customisation—yet when participants were given Fluid Attributes, every participant surfaced fields and diverged sharply in which ones they picked. Two temperaments emerge as soon as hiding is allowed: *hoarders*, who avoid hiding attributes for fear of losing them and prefer dense defaults, and *minimalists*, who strip the overview to a clean slate and add only what matters. An optional cap on visible fields (automatically demoting older ones as new ones are surfaced), paired with a one-step reset, serves both without forcing either. Surfacing is also rarely an end in itself: actors consistently follow a *surface-then-operate* loop, immediately sorting or filtering by the field they just brought into view.

Interactive controls—a save button, an image gallery, or a status toggle—belong to the same per-view choice as static fields. Designed defaults still matter, now as a sensible starting point rather than a permanent ceiling.

Therefore,

**Make each view's attribute set a live control: let the actor surface attributes into the compressed rendering and hide unwanted ones per view, keeping every surfaced field immediately sortable and filterable while preserving each item's recognition across changes.**

Do not treat compact representations as infinitely elastic: on tight footprints such as map pins or icon-sized items, cap the visible slots and let a newly surfaced attribute replace an existing one rather than overflowing the layout (Min & Xia, 2025). Always provide a one-step reset to the view's default set so experimentation stays reversible.

## In practice

### Per-view scope

When a collection has more than one overview—such as a map beside a bookmarked list—keep each view's attribute set independent. An actor may surface review count onto map pins to judge popular areas while surfacing amenities in the companion list to scan preferences.
