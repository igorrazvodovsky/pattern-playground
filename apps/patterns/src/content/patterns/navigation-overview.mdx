A field guide to the models that shape how actors move between destinations, each balancing freedom of movement against orientation cost. Each model determines which [navigation behaviours](/patterns/interaction#navigation) are possible and how [agency](/patterns/agency) is distributed.

In practice the model is rarely chosen. The kind of space a region holds is read off its content at a glance, and for three of the four kinds the model follows from the kind. Only heterogeneous sections leave a topology open, and there a working design starts from its genre's default and corrects it under load, one pain at a time. The step that recognises the kind, and the growth steps that correct it, are walked in [structuring the space](/sequences/structuring-the-space).

## Kinds of space

*One continuous space* — a map, a canvas, a timeline. Movement is viewport control, not link-following.

- [Pan and zoom](/patterns/pan-and-zoom). Costs orientation: at any zoom the view carries no evidence of where it sits in the whole.

*An ordered sequence* — a checkout, a setup, a course. Movement is progression.

- [Step by step](/patterns/step-by-step) when later steps depend on earlier ones. Costs the actor who already knows where they need to be.
- [Pyramid](/patterns/pyramid) when they don't: the hub states the shape and the actor enters anywhere. Costs the hub the job of showing the shape.

*One collection, browsed item by item* — records, files, messages. Movement collapses into focus moving between two coupled views.

- [Overview and detail](/patterns/overview-detail). The topology question dissolves here; the design continues in [composing views](/sequences/composing-views).

A collection can have an order too, and where the order comes from tells the two kinds apart. An authored order, such as a course or a setup, makes an ordered sequence. An order derived from an attribute, such as date, name, or rank, leaves it a collection.

*Heterogeneous sections* — destinations that differ in kind. The one kind with a real choice among topologies.

- [Flat navigation](/patterns/flat-navigation) — everything on one surface. Holds only while the tools fit.
- [Hub and spoke](/patterns/hub-and-spoke) — a hub lists the sections and every journey returns through it. Costs the journey between two spokes, paid twice on every trip.
- [Fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected) — every section one move from every other. Costs chrome on every page, and the chrome is a fixed budget content growth eats into.
- [Multilevel tree](/patterns/multilevel-tree) — sections with subsections behind them. Costs lateral trips back up, and files anything that belongs in two places in one.
- [Hybrid patterns](/patterns/hybrid-patterns) — more than one of the above, per region. The normal case at scale rather than a fallback.

Each genre has a default to start from:

- A desktop tool with tens of sections: a [tree](/patterns/multilevel-tree) under a global menu.
- A phone app: [fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected) across a tab bar, or a [hub](/patterns/hub-and-spoke) when the app opens on a launch screen of independent spokes.
- An editor: [one surface](/patterns/flat-navigation).
- A space with under ten destinations: [fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected).

## Growing past a model

The topologies among sections are weighed against each other when a region hurts, not before. Each pain has a worn path:

- The one surface no longer fits its tools → flat splits into [hub and spoke](/patterns/hub-and-spoke).
- Journeys between spokes have become routine → the hub opens direct paths and becomes [fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected).
- The global chrome no longer fits its sections → the depth goes into a [tree](/patterns/multilevel-tree).
- The tree's lateral trips have become the common case → cross-links, or a [command menu](/patterns/command-menu) and [search](/patterns/searching) as the escape hatch, before the shape changes.

Every address the region ever handed out constrains the move; [deep linking](/patterns/deep-linking) owns that contract.

### Other ways to read the choice

Lenses for explaining a model already recognised, not steps to walk:

- By what the actor is doing: navigating to known locations suits [fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected) and the [tree](/patterns/multilevel-tree); browsing and discovering suits [pyramid](/patterns/pyramid) and [pan and zoom](/patterns/pan-and-zoom); focused tasks suit [step by step](/patterns/step-by-step) and [flat](/patterns/flat-navigation); monitoring several areas suits [hub and spoke](/patterns/hub-and-spoke), with the monitoring view itself a [purpose-keyed view](/patterns/purpose-keyed-view).
- By who drives: [fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected), [pan and zoom](/patterns/pan-and-zoom) and [flat](/patterns/flat-navigation) leave the most to the actor; [pyramid](/patterns/pyramid) and the [tree](/patterns/multilevel-tree) guide; [hub and spoke](/patterns/hub-and-spoke) and [step by step](/patterns/step-by-step) lead.
- By how much there is: under ten destinations, [fully connected](/patterns/fully-connected); tens, a [hub](/patterns/hub-and-spoke) on mobile, a [tree](/patterns/multilevel-tree) on desktop, a [pyramid](/patterns/pyramid) for sequences; past fifty, [hybrids](/patterns/hybrid-patterns).

### Hybrid combinations

Pairings that recur:

- Hub + fully connected — hub feels constraining, cross-section workflows frequent
- Tree + fully connected — large volume with clear sections, cross-section relationships
- Step by step + pyramid — logical sequence but flexible ordering, varying expertise
- Fully connected + pan and zoom — mix discrete and spatial content
- Hub + flat — multiple independent workspaces, each feature-rich
- Pyramid + tree — many sequences requiring category organisation
- Pyramid + filtering — large collections that need both sequence and narrowing

## Malleable Routing

In a [malleable system](/patterns/malleability), the target of a link is not a fixed location. Users can choose *how* they want to open a resource, affecting the [composition](/patterns/malleability#3-composition-topology) of their workspace:

*   **Standard**: Navigate to new page (Replace current context).
*   **Split**: Open side-by-side (Compare contexts).
*   **Peek**: Open in overlay/modal (Temporary reference).
*   **Background**: Open in background tab (Queue for later).

Navigation becomes a tool for composition.

## Implementation considerations

### Universal navigation features

All models benefit from:
- [Deep linking](/patterns/deep-linking) for addressability
- [Searching](/patterns/searching) for direct access
- <ComponentRef id="components-breadcrumbs--docs">Breadcrumbs</ComponentRef> for context
- [Command menu](/patterns/command-menu) for power users

### UI design independence

Models can be rendered in various ways:
- Tabs, menus, sidebar trees for *fully connected* or *tree*
- Cards, lists, grids for *hub and spoke*
- Progress indicators, stepper components, or next/previous for *step by step*
