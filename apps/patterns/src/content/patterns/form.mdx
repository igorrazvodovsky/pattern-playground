* * *

**People answer related questions as a set only when they can see them as one. Asked for the answers one at a time, wherever each happens to be displayed, they lose the relation between them, have no room to answer, and never get a moment at which the whole can be checked before it takes effect.**

A form gathers input on a surface of its own, which is also its cost: the actor leaves the thing they were working on, translates their situation into the system's fields, and commits to the system's shape before saying anything, a question of [formality](/patterns/formality). The structure that makes the input usable is the same structure that makes it hard to express what the fields did not anticipate. When the system can parse raw input against the form's schema, an image, a pasted text, a voice note, the actor's work shifts from entry to checking pre-filled answers; that shift, and the trust it asks for, belong to [data entry](/patterns/data-entry#ai--verification). Every field is a question somebody should be able to justify. For the mechanism see the <ComponentRef id="components-form--docs">form component</ComponentRef>.

Therefore,

**Collect the related inputs on one surface, each as a labelled field with the room it needs, with the constraints the system has shown rather than discovered on submission, and a single act that commits the set.**

Ask for nothing the system can infer, and let every field answer the question of why it is there. A form that has grown past one screen is a sign that it wants splitting or that some of its questions do not need asking.

## In practice

### Fields and groups

A question that takes several controls, a radio group, an address, a day-month-year date, is bound into one labelled group. That binding is anatomy of the form component rather than a move of its own, and is distinct from sections (disclosure) and grouping (comparison).
