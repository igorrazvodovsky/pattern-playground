* * *

**The actor has to supply several answers in a shape the system has fixed, and has to see what is asked, which answers are required, and how much is wanted, without reading instructions.**

A form asks for the answer in the system's shape before the actor has said anything, a question of [formality](/patterns/formality). The structure is what makes the input usable, and it is also what makes it hard to say what the fields did not anticipate. People fill in forms because they need something from the other side, so they want to be done with few mistakes. Every field is a question somebody should be able to justify. For the mechanism see the <ComponentRef id="components-form--docs">form component</ComponentRef>.

Therefore,

**Collect the related inputs on one surface, each as a labelled field with the room it needs and its label beside it, in an order that follows the meaning of the questions, with the constraints shown in the field rather than discovered on submission, and a single act that commits the set.**

Ask for nothing the system can infer. A form that has grown past one screen wants splitting, or some of its questions do not need asking.

## In practice

### Fields and groups

A question that takes several controls, a radio group, an address, a day-month-year date, is bound into one labelled group. That binding is anatomy of the form component rather than a move of its own, and is distinct from sections (disclosure) and grouping (comparison).

### Raw input

When the system can parse an image, a pasted text or a voice note against the form's fields, the actor's work shifts from entry to checking pre-filled answers; that shift, and the trust it asks for, belong to [data entry](/patterns/data-entry#ai--verification).

### The form as conversation

Fields can also be asked and answered turn by turn inside a [conversation](/patterns/conversation), each rendered in its message as an [inline interface](/patterns/inline-interface).
