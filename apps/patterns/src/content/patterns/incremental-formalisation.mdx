Content that entered informally has started to repeat itself: a label applied again and again, near-synonyms circling one meaning, prose that behaves like a list, a note field everyone fills the same way. The structure is visibly there — but it was never declared, because demanding a schema up front was rightly refused while the vocabulary was still moving.

Incremental formalisation promotes structure only after the content has earned it. The promotion starts from either side. The actor promotes a piece once it starts to matter: a commented passage that other documents now refer to becomes a quote with an identity of its own. Or the system recognises what recurs and offers to formalise — merge these two labels, turn this recurring mark into a field, treat this prose as a table — and the actor accepts, defers, or ignores. Structure stays optional and reversible: gained in place when it starts paying, shed in place when it stops.

## Examples

- A search refined by hand, offered back as a saved filter or smart folder
- Two near-synonym labels offered a merge; a recurring label offered as a field
- Prose recognised as a list or table and offered the conversion, reversibly — the block-editor instance
- A spreadsheet range recognised as a table and offered headers and types
- A commented passage turned into a quote: the words stay where they are, the thread moves with them, and other documents can refer to the quote — the [commenting](/patterns/commenting) instance, started by the actor rather than offered

## Research on this pattern

- [Demanded formality fails in four ways](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1008716330212): cognitive overhead, tacit knowledge that resists being written down, structure imposed before the task is understood, and structure that differs from one situation to the next. Actors either refuse the schema or fill it with guesses. The same paper names incremental and system-assisted formalisation as the remedy.
- [The move was named and built as incremental formalisation](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/191666.191768), with information entered informally and formalised later as its structure became clear.
- What remains untested: the sources are reports from building and deploying systems, not controlled comparisons. How often a system can offer to formalise before the offers become interruptions has not been measured.
