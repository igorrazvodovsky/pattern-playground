* * *

**When actors read, review, or study content they cannot or should not edit, their judgements have nowhere to land. Kept only in the actor's head or in a separate notebook, interpretations detach from the passages that prompted them and vanish between sessions; written into the content itself, they corrupt a record that other actors or future readers rely on unchanged.**

Across highlighting, margin notes, structured labels, and personal cross-references, annotation holds one thing constant: it keeps the actor's interpretation anchored to a specific passage or element while leaving the underlying content untouched. That layer differs from system-generated records such as [citation](/patterns/citation) or [attribution](/patterns/attribution), because the actor chooses what to mark and what to say about it.

As marks accumulate, they turn into *wear*—traces of where attention (read wear) and effort (edit wear) actually went—that remain readable long after the session ends. Making a mark slows rapid consumption into a reflective rhythm, and a single annotation often moves through a lifecycle from an ephemeral scratchpad thought to a personal reference and, occasionally, to shared documentation.

The pattern carries two predictable costs. First, private marks do not transfer cleanly to other people: empirical studies of shared reading show that only a small share of personal annotations are ever shared, and sharing one requires rewriting both the note and its anchor so another reader can follow it. Second, the annotation layer competes visually with what it annotates; past some density, the marks become what the reader sees first, and pruning them is nobody's job by default.

Therefore,

**Provide a separate, anchored annotation layer over read-only or shared content so the actor can highlight passages, attach notes, or link references in one low-effort step without modifying the original. Keep marks private by default, preserve their anchor as the content is viewed or revised, and let the actor toggle or narrow the layer so accumulated marks never obscure what they annotate.**

Do not treat private marks as ready-made team commentary or ask for formal categories up front: friction at the moment of marking causes actors to postpone and skip the mark, while exposing raw personal shorthand to colleagues creates noise without context.

## In practice

### Forms of annotation

- *Visual marking (read wear)*: highlighting or colour-coding that shows where attention went without requiring written commentary.
- *Textual notes (edit wear)*: written commentary, definitions, or questions anchored to a passage or element.
- *Semantic labelling*: structured labels or standardised markers that organise passages across a collection.
- *Reference linking*: the actor's own cross-references from a passage to external sources, related documents, or notes.

### Dimensions of variation

- *Permanence*: persistent annotations become part of the content's lasting context; ephemeral marks fade or expire; versioned annotations track changes over time alongside the content.
- *Visibility*: private marks serve personal reading, shared marks support a defined group, and public marks form collective commentary across all viewers.
- *Function*: marks may be descriptive (adding context or definitions), critical (questioning or evaluating), connective (linking related material), or generative (sparking new ideas).

### States

- *Clean slate*: no marks exist yet; subtle affordances show that annotation is available while leaving the original content unobstructed.
- *Active annotation*: visible marks with clear attribution, accessible annotation tools, and navigation from one marked passage to the next.
- *Dense coverage*: overlapping marks where filtering, layering, or hiding controls become necessary to keep the original readable.
