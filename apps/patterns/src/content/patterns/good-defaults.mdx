* * *

**Asking the actor to supply every routine value from scratch spends their attention on choices whose answer is predictable most of the time, invites avoidable format errors, and slows them down before they reach the work they came for. At the same time, people rarely change a value the system has already filled in, so a poor or self-serving guess goes through unread.**

A default does two jobs at once: it spares the actor from making a routine decision, and it shows the expected format of the answer. In a first run, a default belongs when it settles a decision that does not change the result the actor came for—such as a licence or a notification preference—so every question that can wait stays out of the way of reaching that result.

Whatever the source of the guess, the test is whether it is safe to go through unread. Even a reasonable guess carries two subtle costs: skipping a pre-filled field can leave the actor unaware that the setting or behaviour exists at all, and in social or consent contexts, switching a choice from opt-in to opt-out changes what declining means.

Therefore,

**Pre-fill fields and pre-select options with the system's best safe guess whenever one value fits most actors or follows from their context or past choices, and use the pre-filled value to show the expected format. Leave the field empty for identity, consent, and any choice where a wrong guess submitted unread would cause harm or carry unintended meaning, and make pre-filled values easy to spot and overwrite before submission.**

If an actor cannot tell which values the system filled in and which they entered themselves, the default hides mistakes instead of preventing them.
