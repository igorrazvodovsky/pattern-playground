* * *

**When everyone in shared work can see and do everything, each person meets content and options meant for others, can change parts they do not answer for, and an organisation loses its own rules once outsiders join. When the lines are drawn along the organisation chart, or by switching features off one at a time, they cut across how people actually work together, and people on either side of a line lose the common view they coordinate through.**

Therefore,

**Draw the lines along how responsibility is divided in the work as it is practised, between people, teams, organisations and agents. For each part, settle who answers for it, who may let others in, and what an outside party may reach and when. Where the parts differ enough, give each its own view rather than one view with features hidden. Keep something every side can see in common, and make each line visible, so that crossing it is a deliberate act with a named person who can permit it.**

- Burghardt (2009) *Working Through Screens*, C5 "Permissions and views tailored to workers' identities", A2 "Workers' interrelations and relationships", B7 "Object ownership and availability rules"
- Clark & Wilson (1987) [A comparison of commercial and military computer security policies](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.1987.10001) — separation of duties as a rule of commercial work
- Sandhu, Coyne, Feinstein & Youman (1996) [Role-based access control models](https://doi.org/10.1109/2.485845) — constraints between roles, including mutually exclusive ones
- Google Workspace: [Manage external sharing for your organisation](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/drive/manage-external-sharing-for-your-organization)
- Slack: [Slack Connect guide: work with external organisations](https://slack.com/help/articles/115004151203) — one channel, each organisation keeping its own policies
- Microsoft Entra: [B2B collaboration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/external-id/what-is-b2b) — guests sign in with their own organisation's identity; inbound and outbound settings per partner organisation
