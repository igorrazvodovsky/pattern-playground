* * *

**Forcing an actor to translate variable human input into a rigid syntax turns routine entry into avoidable formatting errors, whereas parsing loose input silently commits misreadings the actor never notices.**

The same move operates at two scales. At the field scale, a single input accepts multiple valid ways of writing one value—dates such as "tomorrow", "12/12", or "Dec 12", phone numbers with arbitrary punctuation, or a unified box accepting an ID, name, or status. At the form scale, an interpreter or model normalises unstructured input—pasted text, an image via OCR, or a voice transcript—directly into structured fields.

Both scales lower [formality](/patterns/formality) at capture by shifting the actor's work from typing to verifying. The asymmetric risk is that regional ambiguity (such as `04/05`) and plausible-looking model extractions go through unread and travel further than typed mistakes.

Therefore,

**Accept free-form input in whatever natural syntax or medium the actor uses whenever the system can parse it reliably into the required structure, and show the interpreted value back beside the input for confirmation or correction before it counts as agreed.**
