* * *

**Sections that differ in kind and are used one at a time need one place they are all reached from.**

The phone home screen is the pattern in its purest form: the apps are spokes, the home screen is the hub, and the way from one app to another is to leave the first and open the second from the list. Nobody is lost on a phone, and each app spends its whole screen on its own work. The same arrangement appears wherever sections are independent, in a banking app's home, a settings area or the rooms of a smart-home app. On a desktop the same sections are usually a sidebar visible from everywhere, which is a fact about the screen rather than about the sections.

Because every journey crosses the hub, it is also where the spokes report: a badge on an icon, a balance on the home screen. And because the topology forbids lateral travel, a journey between two spokes costs two moves, which is nothing while such journeys are rare and a tax once they are routine. The pattern holds across three arrangements: the pure form, where a spoke links only back to the hub; a few cross-links, where known routine pairs get a direct path; and global navigation, where every spoke carries the whole list, which is already the [hybrid](/patterns/hybrid-patterns#hub-and-spoke--fully-connected).

Therefore,

**Where a context holds sections that differ in kind and are used one at a time, give it a hub that lists the sections and nothing else. Make each section a spoke that links back to the hub, and use the hub to report across the spokes.**

Watch the journeys between spokes, and open a direct path for any pair that turns routine before the return through the hub becomes what the actor remembers of the design.
