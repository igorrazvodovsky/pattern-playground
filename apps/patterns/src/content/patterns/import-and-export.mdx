* * *

**Export usually flattens the work. The copy keeps the visible content and drops the structure, links, comments, history and record of who made what. Import has the opposite problem: the artifact takes in what it can parse and drops the rest without saying so. The actor finds out what was lost later, by which time the copy and its source have already diverged.**

Therefore,

**Offer import and export in formats other tools can read, and keep as much of the work's structure as the format can carry. Before an export, say what the copy will leave behind; after an import, show what was not taken in. Mark each copy with where and when it came from.**

## Resources & references

- Nouwens & Klokmose (2018) [The application and its consequences for non-standard knowledge work](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3173973): export as flattening, and documents diverging as they move between applications
- Litt, van Hardenberg & Henry (2020) [Project Cambria: translate your data with lenses](https://www.inkandswitch.com/cambria/), Ink & Switch: translating documents between the formats different programs read and write
