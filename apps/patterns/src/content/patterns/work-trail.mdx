* * *

**When an agent reaches an answer through several steps, returning only the result leaves trusting it and doubting it equally uninformed. Yet dumping raw model monologue onto the surface buries the answer, invites actors to rely on plausible-looking text rather than check it, and blurs what the agent actually did with what it says it thought.**

Four tensions govern the trail:

- *Verifiable actions versus unverified narration*: external tool calls and their outputs are checkable facts about what happened. <mark>Model chain-of-thought and thinking summaries are generated accounts that can omit hints, shortcuts, or biases that actually drove the result.</mark> Presenting both in the same voice lends the narration an authority it does not have.
- *Legibility versus compression*: collapsing raw thinking into milestone summaries reduces cognitive load, yet adds a second layer of omission over an already unfaithful account, stripping out the hesitation or dead end that would have warranted inspection.
- *Persuasion versus verification*: a fluent, step-by-step account makes an output look self-audited, raising overconfidence when reading the trail feels costlier than accepting the answer; flat text streams also make logical errors harder to spot than structured steps.
- *Exposure versus privacy*: <mark>a clean final answer does not guarantee a clean trail</mark>—internal reasoning and raw tool returns can resurface credentials, personal data, or system instructions that the reply omitted.

Therefore,

**Expose the intermediate path of a multi-step run as an inspectable trail that keeps observable actions—tool calls, retrieved sources, commands, and their results—visually distinct from the model's stated reasoning. Stream or group steps so the actor can follow progress while the run is under way, fold the trail to a compact summary once the reply settles so it stays reachable without pushing the answer away, and let the actor expand individual steps to inspect the underlying detail.**

Do not present model thinking or its summary as a literal record of computation. Filter or redact sensitive tool inputs, returns, and prompt material before surfacing a trail to other actors or saving it to a shared log.

## In practice

### Dedicated run views

Outside a chat turn—in a side panel, coding agent, or run log—the trail stays expanded beside the work rather than folding above a reply.

<Demo spec="work-trail-run" registries={["shadcn"]} label="A research run in its own view, part way through: the trail grouped by what the agent set out to find, each group holding its thinking, searches and reading" />

### Grouped and hierarchical trails

When a run spawns sub-agents, parallel tool calls, or backtracking branches, a flat chronological list buries where a branch went wrong. Grouping steps by sub-goal or showing a collapsible hierarchy makes errors easier to locate than a linear stream.

## Research on this pattern

- *Unfaithful chain-of-thought*: [Chen et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410) tested reasoning models when subtle hints influenced the answer and found that models frequently used the hints without mentioning them in their reasoning trace, showing that visible chain-of-thought is a generated account rather than a reliable printout of computation.
- *Structured interactive reasoning*: [Pang et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23678) organised verbose chain-of-thought into a navigable hierarchy of topics and found that actors spotted errors earlier, steered outputs more effectively, and formed clearer mental models than with flat text streams.
