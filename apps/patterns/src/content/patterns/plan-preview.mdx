* * *

**A list of steps says what the agent will do, not what the steps will touch or cost. An actor who approves a plan on that basis learns what it changed only once the work is done, when a wrong course is expensive to undo.**

Therefore,

**Before substantial work starts, state the plan as something the actor can read, correct or refuse: the steps, and for the run as a whole, what it will touch and what it will cost. Hold the plan until the actor starts it.**

## In practice

Recurring forms across the catalogues: a step list before execution, a scope statement ("this will modify 14 files"), and—where the preview executes the plan on a sample of items or simulates its side effects before the full run—a [trial run](/patterns/trial-run) paired with the plan.

<Demo spec="plan-preview" registries={["shadcn"]} label="A step list and its scope, held until the actor starts it; the run then works through the same steps" />

A standing run often has no plan to show before each run. Then the limits granted in [bounded autonomy](/patterns/bounded-autonomy) take the plan's place, previewed once when they are granted.
