title: "Attention and tempo"
added: 2026-08-29
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "Decide when the system may interrupt the actor: grade each kind of event once, give events that can wait a place where the actor can check them, and time each notification so the actor can get back to their work."
order: 7
presupposes: >-
  It is known who may be interrupted, by what, and when: the priorities and
  workloads involved are settled before any notification is designed. That
  judgement is made before this sequence, and no pattern here makes it.
enacts: [temporality, agency]
sub-sequences:
  - id: budget
    title: "Setting the attention budget"
    steps:
      - step: "Grade each kind of event by urgency"
        rule: status-feedback
        gloss: >-
          Take every kind of event the other sequences hand over, such as a
          publish, a hand-back, an unrequested proposal, or a background run
          finishing or failing, and give it one of a few fixed grades: the
          actor must act, must know now, or only needs to find it later.
          Grade each kind of event once, not each event. Keep the top grade
          rare, because it is the only one allowed to interrupt focused time.
      - step: "Route each grade to a channel"
        rule: notification
        gloss: >-
          Send each grade to one of three channels: interrupt the actor,
          announce without interrupting, or add silently to what is waiting.
          Below the top grade, let the notification say that something needs
          attention and let the actor decide when to look. Handle an event that
          needs a decision from the actor as the start of an exchange, not as a
          notification.
      - step: "Protect focused time"
        gloss: >-
          Decide which protections exist, such as a focus block, working
          hours, or a quiet day, which ones are scheduled and which are
          switched on in the moment, and what each channel does with events
          that arrive meanwhile: hold them, collect them, or discard them.
      - step: "Decide what may get through"
        gloss: >-
          For each protection, decide who and what may get through it, and
          how often. Use exceptions for named senders with a rate limit, such
          as once a day per sender, with a short window to try again for
          anything truly urgent.
      - step: "Let the actor change every setting"
        rule: settings
        gloss: >-
          The grades, channels, protections, and exceptions together make up
          the attention budget. Make all of it a default the actor can change
          per source, grade, and time of day. The actor's choice always wins:
          the system may lower a grade but never raise it back. Set the
          defaults as if nobody will ever open the settings, and treat a kind
          of event the actor keeps muting as wrongly graded.
  - id: glance
    title: "Showing what is waiting"
    initiating: >-
      Most events now arrive without interrupting. If the actor cannot see at
      a glance what is waiting, they will keep checking, and the checking
      costs more than the interruptions it replaced.
    steps:
      - cluster: "Give waiting events a place to be seen"
        gloss: >-
          Show waiting events in two places, in no set order: indicators on
          the screens the actor already uses, and a feed that collects the
          rest. When nothing has changed, both should say so clearly.
        steps:
          - step: "Show status where the actor already looks"
            rule: status-feedback
            aspect: "its indication shape"
            gloss: >-
              Grade indicators the same way as events: nothing, a dot, or a
              count. Reserve the count for messages addressed to the actor, so
              a number always means someone is waiting on them.
          - step: "Collect non-interrupting events in a feed"
            rule: activity-feed
            gloss: >-
              Collect everything below the interrupting channel in one feed.
              Decide its order and what the actor subscribes to, and design it
              to be checked quickly and left, not read to the end.
      - step: "Let the actor choose when to deal with things"
        gloss: >-
          Let the actor snooze a notification, ask to be reminded later, or
          schedule a message they send. Offer presets that point to the start
          of the next working block.
  - id: arrival
    title: "Timing each notification"
    initiating: >-
      Events happen at moments nobody chose, often in the middle of work the
      budget knows nothing about. These steps are taken for each event, using
      the grades, channels, indicators, and feed already set up.
    steps:
      - step: "Choose when to deliver"
        gloss: >-
          Delay delivery by seconds or minutes, until the actor reaches a
          natural break in their task; anything that can wait for hours
          belongs in the feed. Move a notification to the interrupting channel
          only if the quiet one went unnoticed. Batch notifications only on a
          schedule the actor chose.
      - step: "Summarise batches of events, and say so"
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Where events arrive in large numbers, offer a summary as an opt-in
          feature. Mark it as a summary rather than the original, and link
          each point in it to the event it came from. Skip this step when
          events are few enough to show one at a time.
      - step: "Show brief messages that dismiss themselves"
        rule: transient-feedback
        gloss: >-
          For events that only need to be noticed, show a short message that
          disappears on its own and never makes the actor stop. Decide how
          long it stays and whether it offers one action, usually undo.
          Anything important shown this way also goes to the feed.
      - step: "Help the actor return to their work"
        constituents:
          - slug: deep-linking
            note: "the interrupted place has an address, so returning means following a link rather than remembering where the actor was"
        gloss: >-
          At every grade, keep what the actor needs to resume: their place,
          their unsaved draft, and enough context that they do not have to
          work out again where they were. Do this for delayed notifications
          too.
    resulting: >-
      Each event arrives at a suitable moment, in a form that matches its
      grade, with any summary clearly marked, and with a way back to the work.
      Each notification also makes the actor a little more likely to switch
      notifications off.

  - kind: hands-off

    note: "when an interruption lasts beyond the session, that sequence restores the actor's place"
---

This sequence decides which events may interrupt the actor, when, and in what form. The events come from the other sequences: a document is published, a run finishes or fails, a limit is reached, a proposal arrives, a colleague makes an edit. Events that can wait until the actor comes back are covered by [memory and continuity](/sequences/memory-and-continuity).
