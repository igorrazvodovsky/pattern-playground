---
title: "Composing views"
added: 2026-08-28
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "Present a collection the actor can frame, search, follow links through, and split into coordinated views."
order: 2
presupposes: >-
  The space is structured, and there is a surface to host the views. The
  actor's objects and activities are named, and the collection exists as a
  named thing with settled attributes and labels. That naming happens before
  this sequence, and no pattern here makes it.
enacts: [malleability, density]
sub-sequences:
  - id: framing
    title: "Framing the collection"
    steps:
      - step: "Build the item's ladder"
        rule: item-view
        gloss: >-
          Decide how an item looks at each level of detail, from a glyph or a
          reference through a summary to the full item, and what keeps it
          recognisable at every level.
        constituents:
          - slug: progressive-disclosure
            note: "movement down the ladder is staged disclosure"
      - step: "Frame the collection as a named view"
        rule: data-view
        gloss: >-
          Choose the query (which items), the representation (which compact
          level of the ladder they show at, such as a row, a card, or a pin),
          and the arrangement. Set the defaults the actor meets before
          changing anything.
        constituents:
          - slug: good-defaults
            note: "the framing owns its defaults"
      - step: "Decide the labelling layer"
        rule: tag
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Where items belong to several categories at once, or the words for
          them are still changing, let items carry labels instead of a single
          filed place. Skip this where each item has one settled category.
      - step: "Decide what each view carries"
        rule: attribute-visibility
        gloss: >-
          Choose which attributes each view shows, including the labels, as a
          starting set, and let the actor change it.
        constituents:
          - slug: drag-and-drop
            note: "the strongest surfacing control"
      - cluster: "Open the arrangement and the query"
        gloss: >-
          Three steps in no set order, and most designs take all three. They
          come after the attribute step because an attribute has to be
          visible before it can be sorted by, grouped along, or filtered on.
        steps:
          - step: "Hand the actor the order"
            rule: sorting
            gloss: "Let the actor choose the order, and keep their choice."
            constituents:
              - slug: drag-and-drop
                note: "manual order"
          - step: "Give the collection visible structure"
            rule: grouping
            gloss: "Group items along an attribute the actor chooses."
            constituents:
              - slug: drag-and-drop
                note: "moving between groups is the write path"
          - step: "Narrow to items matching criteria"
            rule: filtering
            gloss: "Keep the active criteria visible on the surface."
            constituents:
              - slug: bounded-choice
                note: "each condition"
              - slug: embedded-intelligence
                note: "natural language into structured filters"
      - step: "Decide which edits pass through the view"
        rule: data-view
        aspect: "its writable dimension"
        gloss: >-
          Decide which representations accept edits, what each edit changes in
          the underlying items, and a way to make every drag-based edit
          without dragging. Editing inside the view follows
          [making changes](/sequences/making-changes).
        constituents:
          - slug: drag-and-drop
            note: "a card dropped in a lane writes the grouping attribute"
    resulting: >-
      The actor meets the collection framed, and can see and change the
      framing. Framings pile up, and naming, sharing, and pruning them is
      decided in [making sense at scale](/sequences/making-sense-at-scale).
  - id: search
    title: "Adding search"
    initiating: >-
      Narrowing is no longer enough: filtering leaves the actor at a dead
      end, their criteria are not ones the attributes can express, or they
      know the item's name rather than its properties. The information
      architecture is settled, and any labels are facets the search can
      narrow by.
    steps:
      - step: "Give intent an entry"
        rule: searching
        gloss: >-
          Decide between keyword and natural-language queries, global or local
          scope, and how the actor's words are translated into the system's
          vocabulary.
        constituents: [autocomplete, suggestion]
      - step: "Frame the result set"
        gloss: >-
          Treat the results, ordered by relevance rather than by the
          collection's own structure, as a named view, and take the framing
          steps again over them.
      - step: "Keep the search reversible"
        rule: searching
        aspect: "its stability obligation"
        gloss: >-
          Keep the context the search was launched from, so returning finds
          the actor's scroll position and framing intact.
  - id: cross-references
    title: "Cross-references in place"
    initiating: >-
      The collection's items are documents with many links between them. A
      collection of records with few links skips these steps.
    steps:
      - step: "Let links be sampled in place"
        rule: link-preview
        gloss: >-
          Preview links on hover or focus, with increasing commitment: peek,
          open alongside, open fully. Use the item ladder's summary level as
          the preview.
        constituents:
          - slug: progressive-disclosure
            note: "the lightest tier"
      - step: "Surface the unauthored connections"
        rule: dynamic-hyperlinks
        gloss: >-
          When the collection outgrows what anyone linked by hand, show
          inferred connections as ranked suggestions, never as authored
          structure, and give them previews, since nobody chose them.
  - id: splitting
    title: "Splitting and coupling"
    initiating: >-
      One view no longer serves both scanning many items and checking one,
      or several views have each earned a place by showing a different
      aspect.
    steps:
      - step: "Split the view"
        rule: overview-detail
        gloss: >-
          Show the collection as two linked views: an overview with every item
          at the same compact level, and a detail view of the current item in
          full. Use the named view as the overview and the item ladder for
          both sides.
      - step: "Decide the content dimension per view"
        rule: attribute-visibility
        gloss: >-
          Decide which attributes appear in which view. The overview's set
          decides what can be compared, sorted, and filtered, so let the actor
          change it.
      - step: "Decide layout and composition"
        gloss: >-
          Place the views side by side, in place, in a popover, or on a new
          page, and decide how many there are and how they nest.
      - step: "Couple the views"
        rule: coordinated-views
        gloss: >-
          Once there are more than two views, carry the actor's selection or
          focus between them, so relationships across views can be seen.
          Couple views only where it clearly helps; two views linked by a
          current item do not need it.
connections:
  - kind: hands-off
    at: framing
    to: making-changes
    via: selection
    note: "a framed collection makes picking items cheap, and picking is where that sequence starts"
  - kind: calls
    at: framing/6
    to: making-changes
    via: editing-in-place
    note: "editing inside the view follows that sequence's in-place arm"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: cross-references/1
    to: calibrating-reliance
    via: citation
    note: "the link preview is also how a citation shows its source"
  - kind: calls
    at: splitting/4
    to: making-changes
    via: selection
    note: "what coupling carries between views is sometimes a selection, which that sequence defines"
---

The space is structured and a surface is waiting for views. Every collection
needs the framing steps. Search, links between items, and a second view come
later, when the collection calls for them.
