---
title: "Working on the same thing"
added: 2026-08-29
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "Share work with named people, let them see each other working, contribute without interrupting, work apart and merge back, and pass work on with its context."
order: 3
presupposes: >-
  The working relationships are known: who shares what with whom, in which
  roles, and what each expects about watching and being watched. Even work
  that looks individual has shared variations, and the mapping covers them.
  That mapping happens before this sequence, and no pattern here makes it.
enacts: [privacy, shareability]
sub-sequences:
  - id: grant
    title: "Sharing the work"
    steps:
      - step: "Share the work with named people"
        rule: sharing
        gloss: >-
          Make sharing an explicit act, never a side effect: name the people
          and give each a level of access, such as view, comment, or edit.
          Access to a container passes down to what it holds, and nobody can
          grant more than they have. Each level promises later steps: comment
          access promises the discussion steps, and edit access promises
          everything an editor can collide with.
      - step: "Decide how much unfinished work others see"
        gloss: >-
          Decide where each kind of work sits between live, where edits
          appear as they are made, and reviewed, where work reaches others
          only when published. Decide it per kind of work, not once for
          the whole system: a tool's issues can be live while its code is
          reviewed. Live presence applies at the live end, and the parting
          steps make the reviewed end workable. Weigh the cost: when
          everything is watched, people explore less.
  - id: visibility
    title: "Seeing each other"
    initiating: >-
      Several people now work on the same thing on separate screens,
      each looking at a different part, so nobody sees the others. What a
      shared desk showed at a glance has to come through the work
      itself, without anyone having to write it up.
    steps:
      - cluster: "Show who is working on what"
        gloss: >-
          Two sides of one decision, in no set order: live presence and
          signed contributions. Show both where the actor already looks, and
          capture both as a by-product of working. People then arrange
          themselves around what they can see, and most collisions never
          happen.
        steps:
          - step: "Show live presence"
            rule: presence
            gloss: >-
              Match the marks to how live the work is: an avatar stack
              for who is here, named cursors and selections for where, and an
              activity signal for what. Add each only where the pace of work
              needs it, and fall back to a trail on slower surfaces.
              Following someone's view, or bringing others to yours, uses the
              same marks as a channel.
          - step: "Sign each contribution"
            rule: provenance-marking
            aspect: "its person-to-person face"
            constituents:
              - slug: activity-log
                note: "signed contributions add up to the space's history, readable per author"
            gloss: >-
              Attach the author to every contribution when it is made: the
              edit, the comment, the version. The signature lets others reply
              to the author and read the history later. When a contributor is
              a machine, the same signature routes its output to the checks
              on relying on it.
      - step: "Give everyone the same view"
        constituents:
          - slug: deep-linking
            note: "the view travels as a link, so opening it is one click rather than a rebuilt display"
        gloss: >-
          Let the actor hand over a view as one thing, with its filters,
          arrangement, and focus, so nobody has to describe how to recreate
          it. Hand it over as a link when people work at different times,
          and as a followed or spotlighted view, through the presence marks,
          when they work live. The view must not carry credentials or
          another person's private state, which the sharing step rules out.
  - id: contribution
    title: "Contributing in place"
    initiating: >-
      People can see each other, and now someone has something to say.
      Communication goes through the work itself rather than a separate
      channel, and a note that waits to be read is preferred over a message
      that interrupts.
    steps:
      - step: "Leave a mark in place"
        rule: annotation
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Let the actor leave a mark where it applies, read when others get
          there, instead of interrupting them. Skip this where each item is
          already the unit of discussion, such as an issue, a card, or a
          message; the thread then attaches to the whole item.
      - step: "Attach the discussion to its place"
        rule: commenting
        gloss: >-
          Turn the mark into a thread others can answer, kept beside its place
          in the work rather than inside it. Let threads be
          resolved, or the discussion only grows. Assigning a thread to
          someone is the handoff step at the scale of a comment.
      - step: "Use set forms for recurring reports"
        rule: status-feedback
        gloss: >-
          Give recurring reports, such as an item's state, a session summary,
          or a review's conclusion, a small shared set of forms, so the
          sender does not have to write them and the reader does not have to
          interpret them. Anything a colleague must see now goes out as a
          notification instead.
  - id: parting
    title: "Working apart and returning"
    initiating: >-
      Sometimes shared work needs to be private for a while: a rough draft
      without watchers, a large change tried without disturbing others, or a
      stretch offline. This is the reviewed end of the earlier decision made
      workable: people may work apart, and owe a return.
    steps:
      - step: "Allow working apart"
        rule: draft-and-publish
        gloss: >-
          Separate the working copy from what others read, with a deliberate
          act that moves one into the other: for the whole piece, such as a
          draft or a branch, or per change, such as a suggestion held for
          review. Each publish is a named version the history can keep.
          Working apart creates a return, which the next two steps handle.
      - step: "Review the returning changes"
        rule: change-review
        aspect: "its person-to-person face"
        gloss: >-
          Show what comes back as changes, what was there and what would
          replace it, and let the reviewer accept or reject them at the level
          the parting chose. Record the conclusion in the set report forms.
      - step: "Resolve conflicting changes"
        rule: reconciliation
        gloss: >-
          Where both sides changed the same thing, merge what can be merged
          and hold back the rest until a person picks between versions shown
          side by side. Show this only when deliberate parting returns. On
          live work, edits merge silently, and a conflict appearing
          mid-flow means the live-or-reviewed decision was wrong for that
          kind of work.
  - id: handoff
    title: "Passing work on"
    initiating: >-
      The work has to continue with someone else, at a set point in a
      workflow or informally. What passes is responsibility as well as
      the work itself. This happens at each transfer, whether or not anyone worked
      apart.
    steps:
      - step: "Pass the work on with its context"
        rule: handoff
        aspect: "its person-to-person face"
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Make the transfer an object, not only a message: a link into the
          work, a named responsibility such as review, resolve, or
          complete, and a subscription that keeps the recipient informed
          until the responsibility is done or explicitly given up. The sender
          is owed a view of whether it was received, reviewed, and acted on,
          which the next step provides. Skip this where the organisation
          already handles the transfer outside the tool.
      - step: "Answer the handoff openly"
        rule: status-feedback
        aspect: "its typed-answer shape"
        gloss: >-
          Let the recipient answer in the same set forms the reports use,
          such as accepted, done, declined, or changes requested, shown where
          the transfer lives, so neither side has to assume the other is
          watching.
    resulting: >-
      Responsibility moves with its context and is answered openly. Work
      continues with the next person instead of restarting, and nobody holds
      work without knowing it.
connections:
  - kind: calls
    at: visibility/1
    to: calibrating-reliance
    via: provenance-marking
    note: "a machine contributor's signed output is an AI surface in shared work, where that sequence's checks apply and mixed authorship makes the signature matter most"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: contribution/3
    to: attention-and-tempo
    via: notification
    note: "a mention, a reply, or a change to someone's item is an interruption that sequence grades, and scheduled send is the same decision from the sender's side"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: parting/1
    to: memory-and-continuity
    note: "each publish is a named version, and the history of working apart feeds that sequence's named states"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: parting/3
    to: making-changes
    via: undo
    note: "the version not picked when resolving a conflict is a deletion, and recovering it belongs to that sequence's safety steps"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: handoff/1
    to: attention-and-tempo
    via: notification
    note: "an assignment or review request claims a colleague's attention directly"
---

Several people work on the same thing, each on their own screen and often at
different times. They cannot see each other working, so the system has to
show them who has access, who is here, who changed what, and what is waiting
on whom.
