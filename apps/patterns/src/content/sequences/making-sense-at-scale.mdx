title: "Making sense at scale"
added: 2026-08-29
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "Work through a collection too large for one view: decide how detail and the overview share the screen, let the actor mark items while reading, and save the views that answer recurring questions."
order: 3
presupposes: >-
  A collection has grown too large for one view, such as an archive, a
  backlog, a library, or a record of runs, and the actor's question is not
  yet specific enough to write as a query. No pattern here settles this.
enacts: [density, formality, adaptation]
sub-sequences:
  - id: lens
    title: "Choosing how to show the collection"
    steps:
      - choice: "Choose how detail and the overview share the screen"
        gloss: >-
          Choose for each surface; the options can be combined. Each keeps
          the overview available at a different cost: a second view takes
          space, a scale control takes time, a graded view loses detail at
          the edges, and a generated summary has to be checked. In every
          case, open on the overview and show detail on request. Skip this
          step when the work is discrete items moving through a defined
          process, where the item and the queue already give enough
          structure.
        alternatives:
          - step: "Split the view"
            rule: overview-detail
            gloss: >-
              Show the overview and the detail as two linked views. The
              splitting steps of [composing views](/sequences/composing-views)
              continue from here.
          - step: "Change the representation with scale"
            rule: semantic-zoom
            constituents:
              - slug: item-view
                note: "supplies the set of representations the zoom moves between"
              - slug: attribute-visibility
                note: "for records, a coarser level shows the same items with fewer attributes"
            gloss: >-
              Show one level at a time under a scale control, with a few
              named levels such as year, month, and day. Decide where the
              thresholds between levels sit and how the change between them
              looks.
          - step: "Show full detail only where the actor is looking"
            rule: focus-and-context
            gloss: >-
              Show one continuous view with full detail at the focus and a
              reduced outline around it. Decide what produces the outline,
              and label it if it is generated.
          - step: "Generate summaries at several levels"
            rule: text-lense
            gloss: >-
              For prose, generate the levels, such as heading, abstract, and
              full text, instead of writing them in advance. For a whole
              collection, generate an overview that helps the actor gather
              items rather than judge them, and link every claim in it to its
              source.
      - step: "Show the difference directly"
        rule: comparing
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Give the comparison its own representation, side by side, overlaid,
          or with the difference drawn, so the actor does not have to keep
          one view in memory while looking at the other. Build it when the
          actor asks for it, within whichever option is in use. Skip this
          step when the questions are about items rather than differences.
  - id: marking
    title: "Marking items while reading"
    initiating: >-
      The actor reads, scans, and sorts the collection. Each pass produces
      judgements, and they are lost when the session ends unless the actor
      can record them. Marking has to take almost no effort, because a mark
      the actor postpones is usually never made.
    steps:
      - cluster: "Let the actor mark items where they are looking"
        gloss: >-
          Both kinds of mark, in either order, follow the same rules: made in
          one action where the actor is looking, private by default, and
          drawn from a flat vocabulary that grows with use. Do not ask for
          structure up front.
        steps:
          - step: "Annotate in place"
            rule: annotation
            gloss: >-
              Let the actor highlight and add notes to content they cannot
              edit. Keep the marks from obscuring the content as they
              accumulate.
          - step: "Label recurring themes"
            rule: tag
            gloss: >-
              Let the actor apply reusable labels, several per item if
              needed, and accept near-synonyms while the vocabulary settles.
              Each label becomes a facet to filter by.
      - step: "Offer structure once the marks show it"
        rule: incremental-formalisation
        gloss: >-
          When marks have accumulated, offer to add structure: merge
          near-synonyms, confirm the vocabulary, turn a recurring label into
          a field, or turn list-like prose into a list. Offer these changes
          rather than applying them automatically, and make them reversible.
      - step: "Share selected marks with others"
        rule: commenting
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          For the few marks that need a reply, rewrite the note and its
          anchor so that someone else can find and understand them; making
          private marks visible as they are is not enough. A shared mark
          becomes a thread others can reply to. Skip this step when the actor
          is the only reader.
  - id: curation
    title: "Saving useful views"
    initiating: >-
      When a view answers a question that comes up again, the actor rebuilds
      it by hand each time, because it was not saved.
      [Composing views](/sequences/composing-views) builds the view itself,
      and the labels provide its facets. The steps here decide what is
      saved, for whom, and how much of the judgement the system takes over.
    steps:
      - step: "Save the view that answered the question"
        rule: data-view
        aspect: "its saved-views dimension"
        gloss: >-
          Save the view under a name, as a saved search, a smart folder, or a
          label opened as a view, for one person, for a role, or as the
          default. Remove saved views when they stop being used.
      - step: "Give each recurring purpose its own view"
        rule: purpose-keyed-view
        gloss: >-
          Give each recurring purpose behind the saved views, such as a quick
          check, a review, or a follow-up, its own view, and keep the general
          view reachable from each. When the system offers a view on its own
          schedule, treat it as an interruption and show where its contents
          come from.
      - step: "Write the judgement down as rules"
        rule: problem-curated-view
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Write the recurring judgement down as detection rules, and show the
          result as a checklist that says why each item was flagged. Keep
          false flags rare, let the actor report them, and keep the
          unfiltered collection one step away. Skip this step when the domain
          cannot define its problems in advance.
    resulting: >-
      Recurring questions have saved answers, which need maintenance: stale
      views removed, rules corrected, and the general view kept one step
      away. The marks and saved views are now part of the collection, and
      the next actor starts from them.

An actor is working with a collection too large to take in through one view. They do not yet know exactly what they are looking for, so they cannot simply search or filter for it.
