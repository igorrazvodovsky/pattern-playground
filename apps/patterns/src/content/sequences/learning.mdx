title: "Learning"
added: 2026-08-29
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "Bring an actor from the first encounter to competent use, give them somewhere to take friction, open optional routes past the interface, and keep the skills that stop being exercised."
order: 8
presupposes: >-
  What competent use looks like is known: which objects matter, what fluent
  work with them looks like, and where the common ground with colleagues
  lies. Where the system will act on its own, competent use includes knowing
  what it will do unasked. That mapping happens before this sequence, and no
  pattern here makes it.
enacts: [learnability, temporality]
sub-sequences:
  - id: arrival
    title: "Shaping the first encounter"
    steps:
      - step: "Bound the first encounter"
        rule: onboarding
        gloss: >-
          Make the first run a designed, bounded period with low stakes,
          doing preferred to reading, and safe failure. Decide what it must
          teach from the picture of competent use: which objects exist and
          where state lives, what happens when the actor acts, and what the
          system will do on its own. For an actor joining a system already
          in use, add what is already here and why, and let them trace how
          it came to be. Every scaffold put up here has to be cleared later.
      - step: "Make the emptiness speak"
        rule: state-empty
        gloss: >-
          Use the first run's empty surfaces to teach: each says why there is
          nothing here and what would put something here. Decide only the
          first-run empty states here; the ones that come later, for filters
          and permissions, belong to the pass over every surface at the end.
      - choice: "Choose what the newcomer occupies"
        gloss: >-
          Fill the empty surface with something to occupy where
          possible, since occupying competent work teaches faster than reading
          about it. The options combine, and taking neither leaves the empty
          surface as the start. Generating the starting content from what the
          actor says they want is delegation, reached from here.
        alternatives:
          - step: "Start them inside competent work"
            rule: template
            gloss: >-
              Give the newcomer a shape that already reflects competent work,
              to adapt rather than invent. Give the set of templates an owner
              who revisits it as conventions change.
          - step: "Walk them through dependent setup"
            rule: wizard
            gloss: >-
              Where the first decisions depend on each other and are made
              once, present one at a time. Only the actor whose arrival
              includes that setup takes it, usually the first into a
              workspace; later joiners arrive into a system already
              configured.
  - id: channel
    title: "Meeting friction"
    initiating: >-
      Teaching ahead of time always misses something. The actor hits
      friction the first encounter did not cover, such as where they are,
      what a term means, or how to do a thing, and the answer is somewhere
      other than where they are looking.
    steps:
      - step: "Meet friction where it happens"
        rule: help
        constituents:
          - slug: annotation
            note: "attaches an answer to the specific element that raised the question"
        gloss: >-
          Keep a channel the actor can come to for the life of the surface.
          Place each answer at its point of need and pitch it at its subject:
          how to operate the interface, or why the domain works this way.
          Keep it true as the system changes. It re-teaches on demand what
          the first encounter taught ahead of time, and remains once the
          scaffolding is cleared.
  - id: expertise
    title: "Opening routes past the interface"
    initiating: >-
      The actor uses the tool daily. The scaffolding that got them started
      is now in the way, and what limits them is the interface rather than
      their understanding of the work. Most actors stay comfortable
      intermediates, so everything opened here has to be optional.
    steps:
      - step: "Clear the scaffolding and open optional routes"
        rule: mastery
        gloss: >-
          Clear the first encounter's scaffolding as competence grows. Open
          optional routes nobody is forced onto: accelerators for speed,
          depth for understanding, and the tool's movable parts for
          reshaping it. Keep the main path sufficient for those who never
          take them.
      - step: "Name every command"
        rule: command-menu
        gloss: >-
          Make every command reachable by name, so the chrome no longer has
          to show everything and discovery becomes typing a guess. This is
          the first optional route most actors take. The menu shows only what
          applies here, so explaining a command the actor expected but cannot
          find is left to the pass at the end.
      - step: "Bind the names to keys"
        rule: keyboard-shortcuts
        gloss: >-
          Bind keys to the named commands the actor uses often enough for
          motor memory. Show each shortcut where the actor already looks: as
          a hint on the slower path, and beside each name in the command
          menu.
  - id: upkeep
    title: "Keeping the skill"
    initiating: >-
      Some skills stop being exercised: procedures that matter but rarely
      come up, and work handed over to the system bit by bit. Unused skill
      fades, and two other sequences assume it has not: taking paused work
      back from the agent, and answering before the system does.
    steps:
      - step: "Choose what stays practised"
        gloss: >-
          From the record of what has been handed over, list the skills that
          must stay alive on the actor's side, graded by what losing them
          would cost: the work a takeback would return, the judgement that
          reviewing the system's output depends on, and the rare procedure
          that matters when it comes.
      - step: "Re-expose what would fade"
        rule: mastery
        aspect: "its retention face"
        gloss: >-
          Schedule re-exposure for the skills on that list: a reminder on
          return, an echo of past decisions when a complex area is re-entered,
          spaced resurfacing of what was learned once. Place it where the
          actor already is, such as a review, a takeback, or an answer they
          are asked to attempt first.
    resulting: >-
      The competence the collaboration depends on is kept up deliberately.
      Practice costs attention, so the list stays short and is revisited as
      more work is handed over.

---

An actor arrives without a working model of the system, and every other
sequence assumes they end up with one. Every actor goes through this. A
smaller version repeats whenever the system changes under them, such as a
new feature, a changed workflow, or the first run of a delegated capability.
