---
title: "Governing autonomy"
added: 2026-08-29
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "Set clear limits on what an agent may do on its own, add checkpoints where mistakes would be costly, hand work back and forth without losing context, and keep a record so the next set of limits is based on evidence."
order: 7
presupposes: >-
  The actor has already delegated work to an agent, and
  the open question is how much it may do without asking. The actor has also
  judged the risks: which actions are cheap to redo, what it costs if the
  agent keeps going unchecked, and which effects cannot be reversed. That
  judgement happens before this sequence, and no pattern here makes it.
enacts: [agency, temporality]
sub-sequences:
  - id: grant
    title: "Setting the limits"
    steps:
      - step: "Set limits on what the agent may do"
        rule: bounded-autonomy
        gloss: >-
          Set limits on scope, spend, time, and risk for each capability,
          rather than one blanket permission. Show the actor the limits in
          plain terms before they take effect. Commit to returning the work
          to the actor whenever a limit is reached; the handoff steps below
          carry this out.
        constituents:
          - slug: rule-composition
            note: "beyond the simplest case, the limits are several rules combined, and the actor reviews them together"
          - slug: settings
            note: "where the limits are kept between runs, so the actor can check what the agent is currently allowed to do"
      - step: "Show spending against each limit"
        rule: cost-transparency
        gloss: >-
          Show how much has been spent against each limit: an estimate before
          the run, a running total during it, and the remaining allowance.
          Track only the limits that matter to the actor. The estimate also
          appears in the preview shown before approval.
  - id: gate
    title: "Checking costly actions"
    initiating: >-
      Even within its limits, the agent reaches actions that matter: work
      that costs a lot, and effects that are hard to reverse. Checkpoints go
      where the consequences justify them, and routine actions go ahead
      without one.
    steps:
      - step: "Rate each action by its consequences"
        rule: action-consequences
        gloss: >-
          Rate each action by how long it takes to recover from, how much it
          affects, and whether it sets off further changes, using the risk
          judgement made before the sequence began. The rating decides which
          actions get a preview, which need approval, and which go ahead
          without either.
      - step: "Preview the work before it runs"
        rule: plan-preview
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Before the agent spends anything, show the steps, what they will
          touch, the expected results, and the cost estimate, so the actor
          knows what they are approving. Preview only work rated high
          enough. A continuous run skips this step, because its rules serve
          as its plan and the actor reviewed them when setting the limits.
      - step: "Ask for approval"
        rule: approval-gate
        gloss: >-
          Nothing rated as consequential goes ahead without the actor's
          approval, given per action, per batch, or per set of changes.
          Approval requests come from three places: a previewed plan, a
          review of changes from delegated work, and a limit that has been
          reached. Approving makes the actor responsible for the outcome,
          which is why the record later needs to show what each approval
          covered.
      - step: "Keep the run within what was approved"
        gloss: >-
          If the agent starts doing something the approval did not cover,
          treat it the same as reaching a limit: pause the run and decide
          what happens next.
  - id: transfer
    title: "Handing work over"
    initiating: >-
      The run has paused before finishing. A limit was reached, the agent's
      confidence dropped too low, a decision came up that needs a person, or
      the actor stepped in. This is the handback the actor was promised when
      setting the limits. Each pause needs its own decision about who
      continues the work and with what permissions.
    steps:
      - choice: "Decide what happens to the paused run"
        gloss: >-
          Decide for each pause. Two other outcomes lead out of this
          sequence: the actor takes over the work directly, or gives up on
          the goal.
        alternatives:
          - step: "Extend the limit and continue"
            rule: approval-gate
            gloss: >-
              Turn the reached limit into an approval request: show what has
              been spent and what more is needed, then either extend the
              limit for this run or stop. If the same extension is asked for
              again and again, note it for the review of limits later on.
          - step: "Hand the work to someone else"
            rule: handoff
            gloss: >-
              Pass the work on with its context: what has been done, what is
              left, and why it was handed over. The person taking over may
              be the actor, or someone with permissions or knowledge the
              agent lacked. Make clear who is responsible for keeping watch
              after the handover.
      - step: "Hand the work back to the agent"
        rule: handoff
        aspect: "its takeback direction"
        gloss: >-
          Treat giving the work back to the agent as a separate step. Confirm
          that whatever caused the pause has been dealt with, agree on what
          the agent resumes and under which limits, and for a while give it
          narrower limits and have it show more of its work.
  - id: account
    title: "Adjusting the limits over time"
    initiating: >-
      Many limits, approvals, and handovers have built up across runs. The
      actor is responsible for outcomes they only reviewed briefly, and
      without a proper record, the next set of limits will be based on
      impressions.
    steps:
      - step: "Keep a record of every decision"
        rule: activity-log
        gloss: >-
          Record every limit set, approval given, handover, and limit
          reached, with who acted, what they did, what it applied to, and
          when. The record should show exactly what each approval covered.
      - step: "Adjust the limits based on the record"
        rule: bounded-autonomy
        aspect: "its incremental regrant"
        gloss: >-
          Widen the limits after a history of runs without problems, and
          narrow them when the risk goes up. Make each change explicit and
          show it to the actor. Start with the extensions that keep being
          requested: a limit that has to be extended on every run is set in
          the wrong place.
    resulting: >-
      The actor decides how much the agent may do based on evidence. They
      are still responsible for work they only saw at its checkpoints, and
      that remains the cost of delegating.
connections:

    at: gate/3

  - kind: hands-off

    via: editing-in-place
    note: "when the actor takes over the paused work directly, they move from supervising to doing the work, and that sequence covers where they do it"

  - kind: hands-off
    at: transfer/1
    to: working-on-the-same-thing
    note: "the person taking over can be someone else, while handoff covers only handovers between a person and an agent"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: transfer/1
    to: attention-and-tempo
    via: notification
    note: "when work is handed back to an actor who is not watching, it reaches them as an interruption, and that sequence decides how urgent it should be"
  - kind: calls
    at: transfer/2
    to: learning
    via: mastery
    note: "taking work back only helps if the actor still has the skills to do it after delegating, and that sequence keeps those skills in use"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: account/1

    note: "the record of limits and approvals is part of what the collaboration keeps across sessions"

The actor has handed work to an agent and now has to decide how much the
agent may do without asking.

{/* A continuous run, such as an agent that sorts incoming requests overnight, works under rules set in advance, and those rules are its plan, so it skips the preview. A one-off run, such as a single research request, is previewed before it starts spending. */}

{/* The sequence does not end after one pass. When a run reaches a limit, the actor decides whether to extend it. Over many runs, the record shows which limits are set too tight or too loose, and the actor adjusts them. */}
