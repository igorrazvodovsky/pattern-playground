---
title: "Delegating standing work"
added: 2026-11-14
updated: 2026-11-15
description: "Attach a request to an occasion so it runs without being asked, keep it in view while it runs unattended, correct it from the cases it gets wrong, and end it when its purpose has gone."
order: 6
presupposes: >-
  The actor has work with no point of completion: it comes back on a
  schedule or whenever something happens, and it will go on for months or
  years. They have judged it worth leaving to run without asking each time,
  and have judged what a wrong run would cost. Neither judgement is made by
  a pattern here. The request for one run can be written, as in
  [delegating a task](/sequences/delegating-a-task#framing).
enacts: [agency, temporality, malleability]
sub-sequences:
  - id: commission
    title: "Setting it going"
    steps:
      - step: "Say when the work runs"
        rule: rule-composition
        gloss: >-
          Attach the request to an occasion, a time or something that
          happens, with its exceptions and a limit on how much one run may
          do, set together with the other limits in
          [governing autonomy](/sequences/governing-autonomy#grant). Show when it next runs. Call
          the request, the occasion and the limit together the arrangement;
          every later step acts on it or on a run it started.
        constituents:
          - slug: good-defaults
            note: "an arrangement that works acceptably before the actor has adjusted anything"
      - cluster: "Ready the arrangement before it goes live"
        gloss: >-
          Neither of these comes first. A trial over real past cases needs
          access to read them, a trial on stand-ins needs none, and access
          to act is not needed until the arrangement goes live.
        steps:
          - step: "Try it before it counts"
            rule: trial-run
            optional: true
            gloss: >-
              Run the arrangement before its occasion comes, in a form where
              a mistake costs little, and tell the actor which form it was.
              Where a run decides between cases, show the reason for each
              decision. Skip this where a wrong run is cheap and easy to put
              right; the first real runs then serve as the trial.
          - step: "Ask for the access that holds between runs"
            rule: permissions
            optional: true
            gloss: >-
              Ask for what the runs will have to reach while the actor is
              away, each grant at the point it is first needed and with its
              purpose. Keep the grants listed beside the arrangement; the
              last step of the walk withdraws them. Skip this where the
              arrangement reaches nothing outside the artifact.
      - cluster: "Start the work in view"
        gloss: >-
          Going live is a separate act from setting up. At that moment give
          the actor two things for the live arrangement. Both are owed from
          the first run and neither depends on the other.
        steps:
          - step: "Give the actor a pause"
            gloss: >-
              Introduce the pause as the arrangement goes live, so the actor
              knows how to hold it before they need to.
          - step: "Show whether the work is live, paused or waiting"
            rule: status-feedback
            gloss: >-
              On the arrangement's own entry, show whether it is live or
              paused, what it last did, and what it is waiting for, so that
              idle work can be told from broken work. Call this the status;
              choose its measures now, because the warnings later are drawn
              from them.
  - id: watch
    title: "While it runs"
    initiating: >-
      The arrangement is live and mostly unattended. The actor looks at the
      status often in the first days and seldom after that, so what they
      need to know has to reach them.
    steps:
      - step: "Bring a run's result to the actor"
        rule: notification
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Where a run produces something for the actor to judge, such as a
          draft, a report, or a list of matches, send it to them, and
          receive it as in
          [delegating a task](/sequences/delegating-a-task#receive). Skip
          this where runs only act; the status and the record show what
          they did.
      - step: "Warn while there is time to act"
        rule: notification
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          From the measures in the status, pick those whose limit would stop
          the work or call for the actor, and warn before the limit is
          reached. Each warning promises what happens if it is crossed, and
          the handover in
          [governing autonomy](/sequences/governing-autonomy#transfer) has to
          keep that promise. Skip this where no measure has a limit or an
          end date.
      - step: "Keep the actor able to take over"
        rule: mastery
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Where a warning can end in the actor doing the work themselves,
          change the arrangement so that it leaves them a share of the work
          or schedules turns at it, for the skills chosen in
          [learning](/sequences/learning#upkeep). Skip this where a poor
          takeover would cost little.
  - id: correct
    title: "Putting it right"
    initiating: >-
      A run has got a case wrong, or the actor suspects cases are being
      missed. The arrangement was reasonable when set up, and the fault
      shows only in what it did.
    steps:
      - step: "Keep what was passed over where the actor can open it"
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Keep the cases and occasions the arrangement refused or skipped,
          least certain first, each with its reason. Call this the
          passed-over list; the next step corrects from it as well as from
          wrong results. Skip this where a missed case costs the actor
          little.
      - step: "Correct the work at the case it got wrong"
        rule: case-correction
        gloss: >-
          On a wrong result or an entry in the passed-over list, let the
          actor fix this one, make a standing exception, or change what
          decided it, and show which part of the arrangement that was: the
          occasion, an exception, or the request. Keep the exceptions as a
          list; the next step works from it.
      - choice: "Choose how the arrangement itself changes"
        gloss: >-
          Choose for each fault. A fault that never recurs needs none of
          these, and its exception is enough. When the list of exceptions
          shows the same correction several times, offer the change; either
          side can notice the repetition.
        alternatives:
          - step: "Edit the part that decided the case"
            rule: rule-composition
            gloss: >-
              Open the arrangement on the occasion or the exception that
              produced the wrong case and let the actor change it there.
          - step: "Add an instruction in the actor's words"
            rule: standing-instructions
            gloss: >-
              Where the fault lies in how the request was carried out, and
              whatever runs it can take an instruction in prose, let the
              actor state the correction once so that it holds for every
              later run, as for a task in
              [delegating a task](/sequences/delegating-a-task#iterate).
          - step: "Show what was wanted"
            rule: tuning-by-demonstration
            gloss: >-
              Where the actor can do the right thing more easily than state
              it, let them do it on the wrong case and say what was taken
              from it.
      - step: "Replay the changed arrangement over past cases"
        rule: trial-run
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Run the changed arrangement over past cases and show which results
          differ, including the case that prompted the change and those that
          only just match or miss. Skip this where the change is plain to
          read and a wrong run is cheap to put right.
  - id: end
    title: "Ending it"
    initiating: >-
      The purpose of the work has gone or is going. The goal was met,
      nothing has matched for months, a pause was never lifted, or the
      actor has stopped answering.
    steps:
      - step: "Let the actor end it from any message"
        gloss: >-
          Put a way to end the arrangement in every message it sends, and
          ask for no reason.
      - step: "Ask whether to carry on after a long silence"
        rule: bounded-autonomy
        gloss: >-
          Set how long the arrangement may go with nothing to do, or with no
          answer from the actor, before it asks, and hold it if nobody
          answers.
      - cluster: "Close the arrangement"
        gloss: >-
          Neither comes first. Say what happens to runs in progress, and
          pass unfinished work to someone through a
          [handoff](/patterns/handoff) where there is one to take it.
        steps:
          - step: "Withdraw the grants it held"
            rule: permissions
          - step: "Hand over its record and the list of exceptions"
            rule: import-and-export
            gloss: >-
              Say what cannot be taken away, such as what an agent learned
              about the actor, rather than imply it goes with them.
    resulting: >-
      The work ran for as long as it was wanted and stopped by a decision.
      The actor could see it and correct it throughout, at a cost in
      attention that standing work never brings to nothing.
---

The actor has work that cannot be finished, only kept up, and wants it to run without being asked each time. The walk leaves an arrangement that starts by itself, can be seen and held while it runs, is corrected from its own mistakes, and has an end.

The first sub-sequence is walked once for each arrangement. Correcting recurs for as long as the work runs, and most of the design effort goes there, because the occasions when a run is wrong are the occasions when the actor is in contact with the work.

Each run is a task. [Delegating a task](/sequences/delegating-a-task) covers how its request is written and how the run is followed, recorded and received, and [governing autonomy](/sequences/governing-autonomy) covers what may be done without asking. This sequence adds what continuing adds. Standing work often begins as a task handed over several times, or as a procedure that [assistance escalation](/sequences/assistance-escalation) found recurring whole.

An agent may carry the arrangement, or a plain rule such as a saved search or a scheduled transfer. The steps hold for both, and a step that needs an agent says so.
