---
title: "Assistance escalation"
added: 2026-08-29
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "How much of the work the system takes on, from a default guess to a procedure the actor hands over, raised or lowered one level at a time on the evidence of use."
order: 4
presupposes: >-
  An actor is doing work the system can see, and there is evidence of
  typical use: what most actors need most of the time, judged from the
  task's shape, from general data, or from observed use. Every level of help
  is a bet on that evidence. It is also settled that the actor leads and the
  system steps in selectively. These judgements happen before this sequence,
  and no pattern here records them.
enacts: [agency, adaptation]
sub-sequences:
  - id: floor
    title: "Filling in what is known"
    steps:
      - step: "Set the default guess"
        rule: good-defaults
        gloss: >-
          Fill fields with the system's best guess from the task's shape and
          general data, without asking the actor for anything; the guess also
          shows the expected kind of answer. Decide which fields get one.
          Leave out identity, consent, and other sensitive choices, and use a
          guess only where it is acceptable for it to go through unread.
      - cluster: "Replace the guess with known values"
        gloss: >-
          Two ways a guess gives way to something known, each used where its
          condition holds. They answer different moments of the same field,
          in no set order.
        steps:
          - step: "Fill from stored values"
            rule: autofill
            optional: true
            gloss: >-
              Where the system already holds the actor's own value, from a
              profile, an earlier session, or the browser, fill it in place of
              the guess. Do this only where confidence is high, since filled
              fields tend to be submitted unread.
          - step: "Complete as the actor types"
            rule: autocomplete
            optional: true
            gloss: >-
              Where the valid answers are many and the actor is still typing,
              narrow the candidates to match. The candidates are looked up,
              not produced. Skip this where the answers are few enough to
              show in full.
  - id: offer
    title: "Joining the work"
    initiating: >-
      The guessed, stored, and looked-up values are in place, and what is
      left is work the actor would do themselves. The system can take part
      of it by producing content, which rests on inferred intent.
    steps:
      - step: "Read the intent from context"
        rule: embedded-intelligence
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Infer what the actor is doing from what the system already holds,
          such as the selection, the last action, or the actor's role, so the
          actor never has to restate it to get help. The next step turns the
          inference into offers. Skip this where the actor states the intent,
          as in a conversation.
      - step: "Make everything it produces an offer"
        rule: suggestion
        gloss: >-
          Settle once the terms every higher level inherits: the system
          proposes and the actor decides. Accepting takes one gesture,
          declining is cheaper than weighing the offer, and each offer shows
          its confidence. An offer shown before the actor has formed their
          own idea narrows what they consider next, and checking how far they
          rely on it is handled elsewhere.
      - step: "Continue what the actor is writing"
        rule: ai-completion
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Where the actor is composing, propose the continuation at the
          cursor, lighter than their own text and gone once they type past
          it. Stay silent when confidence is low. A block generated to be
          reviewed as a whole belongs with delegated results, not here. Skip
          this where nothing is being composed.
  - id: forward
    title: "Proposing the next move"
    initiating: >-
      So far the offers stay inside the step the actor is taking. The system
      can also see the points where one piece of work ends and another
      usually follows, and offer the next move.
    steps:
      - step: "Propose the next step"
        rule: next-best-action
        gloss: >-
          At a transition point, such as a closed record or a finished
          output, offer a whole action with the reason for it. These offers
          arrive uninvited, so spend the channel as carefully as the offers
          in it. If the system performs the action instead of proposing it,
          that is delegation, and this sequence ends there.
      - step: "Guard the accept by what it does"
        rule: action-consequences
        gloss: >-
          Grade each proposed action by its consequences and size the guard
          to the grade: an easily reversed action keeps its one-gesture
          accept, and a consequential one needs a confirmation or a look at
          what it will touch. The grade also caps how far the level can rise
          later.
  - id: climb
    title: "Adjusting the level of help"
    initiating: >-
      Every level above the first section is a bet on typical use, and the
      bets age. Acceptances, dismissals, and corrections build up on each
      surface, and without a record, whether a level still fits is judged on
      impressions.
    steps:
      - step: "Keep a record of how offers are used"
        gloss: >-
          Record what was offered, what was accepted, what was dismissed and
          how quickly, and what was corrected after accepting, per surface and
          per level. This record measures how much help fits; the record of
          reliance measures how closely offers are checked.
      - step: "Move each surface up or down"
        rule: assisted-task-completion
        gloss: >-
          Raise a surface's level where its offers are routinely accepted and
          the grade allows it, and lower it where they are routinely
          dismissed. Decide the lowering too, rather than letting a level
          fall out of use. A higher level must explain more of itself, and
          routine acceptance never raises a consequential one past its grade.
      - step: "Hand the recurring procedure over"
        rule: workflow
        constituents:
          - slug: rule-composition
            note: "each condition is written as clauses the actor can read, edit, and check before the procedure runs"
        gloss: >-
          When the record shows the same proposals accepted whole in the same
          situations, turn them into a procedure both the actor and the
          system can read, with steps, conditions, and transitions, and hand
          it over to run. The actor now inspects and repairs the procedure
          instead of accepting offers, which leaves this sequence.
    resulting: >-
      Each surface sits at the level its record supports, and what recurred
      whole now runs as a procedure. The cost stays: every level rests on
      typical use, and it fits worst the actor who is least typical.
connections:
  - kind: calls
    at: offer/2
    to: calibrating-reliance
    via: cognitive-forcing-functions
    note: "an offer shown before the actor has formed their own view narrows it, and that sequence supplies the friction that answers it, at each offer surface's own grade"
  - kind: calls
    at: climb/2
    to: calibrating-reliance
    via: transparent-reasoning
    note: "a surface that moves up owes a fuller account of its offers, which that sequence's evidence steps provide"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: forward/1
    to: attention-and-tempo
    via: notification
    note: "proposals arrive uninvited at moments the system picks, and what may interrupt whom, and when, is that sequence's decision"
  - kind: hands-off
    at: climb/3
    to: delegating-work/framing/1
    via: workflow
    note: "the procedure is handed over as a standing request, one of the two ways into that sequence"
---

An actor is doing work the system can see, and the system could do part of
it. Each level of help takes on more of the work, and a surface moves up or
down over its life rather than settling on a level once.
