---
title: "The encounter"
added: 2026-08-28
updated: 2026-09-27
description: "State what the agent can do, carry each kind of request, check before consequential acts, recover when the exchange breaks, and end or pause the conversation deliberately."
order: 4
presupposes: >-
  Conversation has been chosen as this surface's primary mode, not one
  option among many. The agent's capabilities are listed, so there is a
  boundary to state: what it can act on, what it can only look up, and what
  it can only discuss. Both are settled before this sequence, and no pattern
  here makes them.
enacts: [conversation-quality, agency, learnability]
sub-sequences:
  - id: footing
    title: "Setting the footing"
    steps:
      - step: "Draw the capability boundary"
        rule: capability-and-scope
        gloss: >-
          State what the agent can act on, look up, or only discuss, and
          decide what it may promise. Give an out-of-scope request somewhere
          to go, never a dead end. Restate the boundary at each encounter,
          since capabilities change between visits.
      - choice: "Decide who can open"
        gloss: >-
          Most designs take both arms. Either way the actor has committed to
          nothing yet, so a way out before any activity starts is owed; the
          trouble steps build it.
        alternatives:
          - step: "Let the agent open"
            rule: agent-opening
            gloss: >-
              Open by saying who is speaking, what it can do, and what this
              is about, including the capability statement, and propose a
              first topic the actor can accept, replace, or turn down.
          - step: "Recognise the actor's open"
            rule: user-opening
            gloss: >-
              Align to whatever the actor brought, and route it by shape: a
              question answerable as asked, a goal with steps behind it, or
              something too loose to act on, which the agent asks about
              first.
  - id: business
    title: "Carrying the business"
    initiating: >-
      A topic is agreed, and the actor's business arrives in a few shapes: a
      question answerable as asked, a goal with steps behind it, or content
      too long for one turn.
    steps:
      - step: "Answer the askable directly"
        rule: inquiry-user
        gloss: >-
          Answer first, offer next moves after, and keep turns short. For
          anything non-trivial, attach the evidence to the answer.
      - step: "Frame the goal-shaped request"
        rule: open-request
        gloss: >-
          Make the goal, constraints, assumptions, and sources explicit, so
          what is missing shows as named gaps.
        constituents:
          - slug: inquiry-agent
            note: "the agent asks only for what it genuinely lacks, as early as the loose opening"
          - slug: prompt-scaffolding
            note: "structure for composing a complete request without already knowing what a complete one contains"
      - step: "Hold the floor for the long telling"
        rule: extended-telling
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Run content that will not fit one turn as a structured sequence the
          actor controls: announce the scope, advance when they signal to go
          on, and keep skip, back, and stop available. The repair steps also
          hand over to this. Skip it where answers fit single turns.
      - step: "Give turns a working surface"
        rule: inline-interface
        optional: true
        gloss: >-
          Where words are the wrong vehicle, make the message something to
          act on: chips for a choice, a card to confirm, a chart to read. The
          confirmation and repair steps later use this surface. Skip it where
          the encounter stays in words.
  - id: gate
    title: "Checking before the act"
    initiating: >-
      Some requests end with the agent acting on its reading of them, by
      spending real work or making changes that are hard to undo, and the
      reading can be wrong. The check is decided per act, by its
      consequences, and routine acts go ahead.
    steps:
      - cluster: "Match the check to the act"
        gloss: >-
          Two checks, in no set order. Each act takes the one its
          consequences call for, and an act that can be cleanly undone takes
          neither.
        steps:
          - step: "Preview the substantial work"
            rule: plan-preview
            gloss: >-
              Show the steps, targets, expected effects, and cost before
              substantial work, so a misreading surfaces while it is cheap.
              Preview only work that warrants it.
          - step: "Take the go-ahead in place"
            rule: inline-confirmation
            gloss: >-
              For a single step that cannot be undone, ask for confirmation
              where the actor expressed the intent, without moving them
              elsewhere.
  - id: trouble
    title: "Designing for trouble"
    initiating: >-
      Any activity can break in two directions: the agent misreads the
      request, or the actor does not follow the agent. Sometimes the actor
      simply wants out with the goal unmet.
    steps:
      - cluster: "Repair from both sides"
        gloss: >-
          Two directions of trouble, in no set order. Both resume the
          activity at the step that broke, not from the beginning.
        steps:
          - step: "Show the agent's reading for correction"
            rule: agent-repair
            gloss: >-
              Show the agent's interpretation and parameters, and let the
              actor edit them. Where one specific value is missing, ask for
              that value instead of guessing again.
          - step: "Re-deliver what did not land"
            rule: user-repair
            gloss: >-
              Offer a paraphrase, a definition, or a worked example without
              the actor having to diagnose their own confusion. When none of
              them is enough, switch to a long telling.
      - step: "Build the exit that is not failure"
        rule: abort
        gloss: >-
          Let the actor stop an activity whose goal is no longer wanted: halt
          where it stands, state what changed, and keep what it collected
          visible for the actor to keep or discard. This is the way out owed
          since the opening. Changes already made are undone outside the
          conversation.
  - id: ending
    title: "Ending and parking"
    initiating: >-
      An activity has delivered, but only the actor's acknowledgement shows
      that it landed. The encounter as a whole has to be possible to end,
      and to pause without ending, with the difference visible to both
      sides.
    steps:
      - step: "Close the loop on each activity"
        rule: sequence-completion
        gloss: >-
          Treat the actor's acknowledgement, such as "thanks" or "got it", as
          the close of the activity. Offer a next move there only when the
          actor is likely to want one.
      - cluster: "Settle the two endings"
        gloss: >-
          Design both endings, and let the kind of interface decide which one
          the defaults serve: a conversation-first interface ends deliberately
          and parks as the exception, and an embedded surface is mostly left
          open and rarely closed on purpose.
        steps:
          - step: "End the encounter deliberately"
            rule: closing
            gloss: >-
              Invite last topics and either handle or schedule them, so both
              sides know it is over rather than paused. Anything deferred here
              is a commitment the actor expects kept.
          - step: "Let it park without ending"
            rule: disengage-without-closing
            gloss: >-
              Keep the state saved, show that it is saved, and keep resume
              within reach. On return, pick up with the history, and confirm
              any assumptions that may have gone stale, including the
              capability boundary.

An actor and an AI agent meet in conversation, and the conversation carries the
actor's business. In a conversation-first interface the conversation is also the workspace, and
[structuring the space](/sequences/structuring-the-space),
[composing views](/sequences/composing-views), and
[making changes](/sequences/making-changes) are all reached from inside it.
