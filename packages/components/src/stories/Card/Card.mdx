# Card

A card gathers everything known about one subject into a single scannable surface, so a reader can take it in at a glance and tell it apart from its neighbours.

<Canvas>
  <Story of={ CardStories.Card } />
</Canvas>

The only thing every card has in common is that it looks like one grouped surface. What goes inside — a title, an image, a price, a chart, a form — varies so widely that a component trying to lay all of it out ends up guessing at content it can't see, and guessing wrong. So a card here is a _recipe_, not a component: a small `.card` group class for the surface, plus composition classes the markup reaches for when it wants them. The variance moves into the examples, where it can be seen, rather than into the CSS, where it can only be predicted.

## Base — the surface

`.card` is the whole of what makes a card a card: a background, a border, a radius, and a positioning context. It sets no interior spacing, because spacing depends on what a card holds, and the card cannot see that. The container is the only required part; everything below is optional.

<Story of={ CardStories.Basic } />

Interior spacing is composed, not assumed. Pair `.card` with `.flow` for vertical rhythm between blocks and `.pad` for an inset, exactly as any other region would. A card that bleeds an image to its edges simply doesn't ask for the inset on that edge.

The surface imposes nothing on what it holds. A couple of stat lines with a highlighted figure, or a list carrying selection state and a badge, group the same way — the card only draws the boundary around them.

### Zones

Three thin zone classes position recurring regions without dictating their contents. `.card__header` and `.card__footer` are padded rows that spread their children apart when there's more than one; `.card__attributes` is a row for a run of badges. They arrange; the markup fills them.

## Variants

### Overlay

The content sits over a tinted background image rather than beside it.

<Story of={ CardStories.ImageOverlay } />

### Media, cover, and mosaic

A single image spans the card's width; a figure and a video get a height cap so a card doesn't become all picture by accident. Two opt-in classes take it further: `.card--cover` makes the image _be_ the card, filling it corner to corner, and `.mosaic` on a figure lays its images out as a grid. Both are asked for in markup rather than inferred from how many images happen to be present.

<Story of={ CardStories.Image } />

### Actions

Actions live either in the card's body or pinned to its corner with `.card__actions--secondary`; hover-only actions stay quiet until the card is under the pointer, for cases where their availability is obvious in context.

<Story of={ CardStories.Actions } />

## Compositions

The recipe is most itself when combined with the rest of the system.

### A whole-card link

Wrap a card's link or button in the `.stretched-link` utility and the whole surface becomes its hit target — the card lifts its border on hover and takes the focus ring when the link is focused. Inner controls (a menu, a badge button) stay individually operable above the overlay. The mechanic, its accessibility costs, and its containing-block caveat live with the utility itself, under _Utilities_.

<Story of={ CardStories.LayoutGrid } />

### Cards in a collection

Arranging many cards is the collection's job, not the card's: `.cards` inside a `.layout-grid` tiles them, and `.cards--list` stacks them flush into rows. A card in the row variant that carries media gains a leading image column; one that doesn't stays a simple stacked row.
