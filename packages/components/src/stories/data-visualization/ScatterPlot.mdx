> 🙂 *Fun meter: 2/5*. A settled chart with one live tension worth turning over: the point is the simplest mark, yet the scatter plot fails in the most interesting way — overplotting. Once the cloud is dense enough, adding data subtracts information. What earns a second look is the channel economy: position is spent on two variables before you start, so a third variable has to squeeze onto the weaker channels of area or colour, and the *bubble* is where that trade-off becomes visible.

# Scatter plot

A scatter plot answers whether two measured quantities move together, and where the exceptions sit. It owns the association branch of the <PatternRef slug="data-visualization">chart territory</PatternRef>: two quantitative variables, each on its own axis, one point per observation.

Position is the strongest perceptual channel, and the scatter plot spends all of it — x and y both carry a number, so the reader reads correlation, clusters, and outliers straight off the coordinates. That is also its limit: a third variable has nowhere strong left to go.

## Variants

### Association

Two numeric variables on position. The shape of the cloud is the finding — direction, tightness, and the points that refuse to join it.

<Story of={ ScatterPlotStories.Default } />

### Categories

A categorical variable colours the points into series, drawn from the shared [categorical palette](../?path=/docs/data-visualisation-elements--docs). Clusters that separate by colour say the groups differ on the two measured axes. Because any two points can sit side by side, a scatter plot carries at most four series before the colours stop being reliably distinguishable — past that, facet into small multiples or fold the tail into "other".

<Story of={ ScatterPlotStories.Categories } />

### Bubble

A third quantitative variable rides on bubble area — the scatter plot's neighbour rather than a separate chart. Area, not radius, is proportional to the value (via a square-root scale), so a value four times larger draws a bubble twice as wide, matching how area is perceived.

## States

### Empty

With no data the chart clears to nothing — no axes, no marks, no error.

### Dense (overplotting)

Past a few hundred points the marks overlap and the cloud reads as a solid mass. Mitigations — lower point opacity, smaller radii, sampling, or switching to a density representation — belong to the reading, not the data.

## Accessibility

### Screen reader support
- SVG role: `role="img"` with a descriptive `aria-label`
- Data table: a visually hidden table exposes label, x, y, and category for every point
- Live regions: keyboard navigation announces the focused point
- Focus management: the chart is a single tab stop; arrow keys move within it

### Keyboard navigation
- Tab: focus the chart
- Arrow keys: move between points (position is tracked, not reset)
- Home/End: jump to the first/last point
- Enter/Space: activate the focused point (fires the click event)
