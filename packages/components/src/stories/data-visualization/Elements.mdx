## Colours

### Categorical palette

For encoding *identity* — which series, which category — charts draw from a fixed
palette of eight hues. Reach for it when the distinction between marks is nominal
(team, region, endpoint); for magnitude or order, a single-hue ramp is the right
tool instead, so the reader sees the quantity in the lightness.

Two rules make the palette safe rather than merely pretty, and both are load-bearing:

- *The order is the accessibility mechanism, not decoration.* Slots are assigned
  `1 → N` in sequence and never reordered or cycled. The ordering was chosen so
  that neighbours stay distinct under red–green colour-vision deficiency
  (simulated protanopia and deuteranopia, measured as OKLab colour distance).
  Reordering breaks the guarantee. Colour also follows the entity, not its rank —
  filtering a series out must not repaint the survivors.
- *Colour never carries meaning alone.* Every chart pairs the palette with a
  second channel that names the series — a legend, direct labels, or the
  accessible data table and hover (a scatter leans on the last two). This matters
  most on the light surface, where three of the hues (magenta, yellow, aqua) fall
  below the 3:1 contrast bar by design and depend on that backup to stay legible.

### How many series the palette carries

The safe count depends on the chart, because it depends on which marks can end up
side by side.

<pp-table>
  <table>
    <thead><tr><th>Form</th><th>Which marks touch</th><th>Safe series</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Bar, line, stacked area</td><td>only neighbours in the stack or legend order</td><td>up to 8</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Scatter, bubble, small multiples</td><td>any two, anywhere in the cloud</td><td>up to 4</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</pp-table>

A scatter plot can place any two points next to each other, so every pair must be
distinguishable — a stricter test that the full eight cannot pass. Past four
series in an all-pairs form, the answer is fewer series (fold the tail into
"Other"), small multiples, or direct labels — never a ninth colour. On a dark
surface even the fourth series sits at the edge of the CVD margin, so the
second-channel rule above (a label or the table) does the real work there.

The eight hues are an OKLCH set seeded from a known-good CVD-safe palette and
validated for this system; they are a sensible default, not a brand commitment —
retune the hues and re-validate to taste. Tokens live in
`styles/variables-palette.css` as `--c-cat-1 … --c-cat-8` (mode-aware; the dark
steps are the same hues re-stepped for a dark surface).
